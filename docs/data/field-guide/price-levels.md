### `rollup.price_stats@v2.sma_20`

**How to read it.** The mean close of the last 20 sessions, in dollars: the one-month average, the short-term trend line a pullback returns to. The close above it is a short-term uptrend; feature.pct_vs_sma_20 is the distance as a fraction and feature.pullback_to_sma20 the dip-in-an-uptrend entry (within one ATR of it).

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| has a one-month average | `not_null` | hard | - | threshold the distance instead: feature.pct_vs_sma_20 |

**When the reading lies**

- An average, so it lags: after a gap the 20-day average takes weeks to catch up and 'above the average' says the gap happened, not that a trend exists.
- Null after any missing bar in the last 20 sessions.

Sources: Brock, Lakonishok and LeBaron (1992) for moving-average rules in general

### `rollup.price_stats@v2.sma_50`

**How to read it.** The mean close of the last 50 sessions, in dollars: the intermediate trend line, the best single rule in Brock, Lakonishok and LeBaron's long Dow test. Above it is an intermediate uptrend; with the 200-day average it defines feature.trend_state (close above 50 above 200: UPTREND). feature.pct_vs_sma_50 is the distance.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| has a 50-day average | `not_null` | hard | - | threshold the distance instead: feature.pct_vs_sma_50 |

**When the reading lies**

- Lags by about a quarter of its length: a trend change shows in the close weeks before it shows in the average.
- Null after any missing bar in the last 50 sessions or a shorter history.

Sources: Brock, Lakonishok and LeBaron (1992): https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v47y1992i5p1731-64.html

### `rollup.price_stats@v2.sma_200`

**How to read it.** The mean close of the last 200 sessions, in dollars: the long-term trend line, the daily version of Faber's 10-month average. Above it is the classic bull-market condition for the name; the 50-day crossing above it is the 'golden cross', below the 'death cross'. feature.pct_vs_sma_200 is the distance.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| has a 200-day average (ten months of history) | `not_null` | hard | - | threshold the distance instead: feature.pct_vs_sma_200 |

**When the reading lies**

- Needs 200 sessions of history: null for listings under ten months old and after any gap, so a hard criterion on it excludes every recent listing (the 'recent listing' situation).
- The crossover signals lag by months and whipsaw in ranges; the evidence is for indices and portfolios more than single names.

Sources: Faber (2007); Brock, Lakonishok and LeBaron (1992)

### `rollup.price_stats@v2.high_52w`

**How to read it.** The highest daily high of the last 252 sessions, split-adjusted, in dollars: the 52-week high investors anchor on (George and Hwang). A level to show next to the close; the distance to it is feature.pct_from_high_52w and the near-the-high band feature.near_52w.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| has a 52-week high | `not_null` | hard | - | threshold the distance instead: feature.pct_from_high_52w |

**When the reading lies**

- Split-adjusted but not dividend-adjusted: a large special dividend leaves a high the price cannot regain without the payout.
- Null under 240 bars in the last 252 sessions; feature.pct_from_high_avail uses the high since listing for younger names.

Sources: George and Hwang (2004)

### `rollup.price_stats@v2.low_52w`

**How to read it.** The lowest daily low of the last 252 sessions, split-adjusted, in dollars: the 52-week low. The distance above it is feature.pct_from_low_52w; LOW in feature.near_52w is within 10% of it.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| has a 52-week low | `not_null` | hard | - | threshold the distance instead: feature.pct_from_low_52w |

**When the reading lies**

- A low set on one panic day (a halt, a flash move) can sit far below any price the name has traded at since; feature.pct_from_low_52w then reads 'far from the low' for a name that is at its usual bottom.
- Null under 240 bars in the last 252 sessions.

Sources: George and Hwang (2004)

### `rollup.price_stats@v2.history_days`

**How to read it.** How many of the last 252 sessions have a bar, the session included: 252 is a full year, 240 is the floor for the 52-week fields, under 200 means the 200-day average is null, under about 60 is a listing a few months old. The one-number form of 'how much history does this name have'.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a full year of history | `gte 240` | hard | - | - |
| enough history for the long averages | `gte 200` | soft | 20 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- A low count is either a young listing (instrument.listed_on) or a name that trades rarely (rollup.price_history@v1.bar_status); the two need different treatment.

Sources: Site convention (the window floors of price_stats@v2)

### `rollup.momentum@v1.atr_14`

**How to read it.** Wilder's average true range over 14 sessions, in dollars: the typical daily range including gaps. The unit of swing-trading distances: a stop 2 ATRs below entry (the Turtle rule), a target 3 ATRs above, a pullback 'within 1 ATR' of the average. feature.atr_pct is the same number as a share of price, which is what a screen compares across names.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| has a range (15 consecutive bars) | `not_null` | hard | - | threshold feature.atr_pct instead |

**When the reading lies**

- Dollars are not comparable across names: $2 is 1% on a $200 stock and 10% on a $20 one. Threshold feature.atr_pct, not this.
- One gap day lifts it for two weeks; a takeover pins it near zero (the 'pending takeover' situation).

Sources: Wilder (1978); the Turtle rules (ATR-sized stops)

### `rollup.momentum@v1.prior_high_20d`

**How to read it.** The highest daily high of the 20 sessions before today, today excluded, in dollars: the level a breakout close has to clear. feature.breakout_20d is the close above it with volume at 1.5 times normal; this field is the level itself, to show next to the close.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| has a breakout level | `not_null` | hard | - | the breakout itself is feature.breakout_20d |

**When the reading lies**

- A level set by one spike high (a halt, a news candle) is easy to clear on a quiet day: the volume condition of feature.breakout_20d is there for that reason.
- Null after a gap among those 20 sessions.

Sources: Donchian channels (the Turtle rules); Raschke and Connors on failed breakouts

### `rollup.swing_levels@v1.swing_high`

**How to read it.** Resistance: the high of the most recent confirmed swing high above the close, in dollars. A swing high is a bar whose high is strictly above the five highs before it and at least the five after, so it is confirmed five sessions later. It is where sellers last showed up above the current price; feature.dist_to_resistance is the distance as a fraction and feature.dist_to_resistance_atr in ATRs.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| has resistance above | `not_null` | hard | - | threshold feature.dist_to_resistance instead |

**When the reading lies**

- Null at a one-year high (nothing above the close): a 'near resistance' criterion then rejects the names breaking out, which is often what you want, and a hard 'not_null' drops them.
- The most recent swing high above the close is not always the strongest: an older, higher one can matter more; rollup.swing_levels@v1.swing_high_date says when it formed.
- Confirmed five sessions late: a swing high from the last week is not yet a level.

Sources: docs/data/swing.md (pivot width 5); practitioner swing-point conventions

### `feature.pct_from_high_avail`

**How to read it.** The close against the highest high of the history available, minus 1 (at or below 0): the 52-week high for a name with a year of bars, the high since listing for a younger one (rollup.price_history@v1.range_sessions says how many sessions that is). The version of feature.pct_from_high_52w that does not go null on recent listings.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| near the high of its history | `gte -0.1` | soft | 0.05 | for names with a year of bars prefer feature.pct_from_high_52w |

**When the reading lies**

- For a young listing the 'high' can be the first day's print (IPO pops): 20% below it after three months is normal, not a discount. Check rollup.price_history@v1.range_sessions before reading the distance like a 52-week one.
- Null for a listing under 20 sessions old or a name trading too rarely.

Sources: George and Hwang (2004) for the 52-week version; site convention (ADR 0046) for the available-history fallback

### `feature.dist_to_resistance_atr`

**How to read it.** The distance to resistance in ATRs: how many typical daily ranges sit between the close and the last swing high above it. Under 1 the level is inside today's noise (at resistance); 2 to 4 is a swing trade's room; above 6 the level is out of play. The ATR scaling makes it comparable across calm and volatile names, which feature.dist_to_resistance (a fraction of price) is not.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| room to run (in ATRs) | `gte 3` | soft | 1 | soft, so a name at a new high (null) is listed rather than rejected |
| at resistance | `lte 1` | soft | 0.5 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Null at a one-year high (no resistance above) and when the ATR is 0: a hard 'room to run' criterion rejects the names at new highs.
- A tiny ATR (a pinned takeover target) makes any distance read as many ATRs; require feature.atr_pct gte 0.005.

Sources: The Turtle rules (ATR-scaled distances); docs/data/swing.md

### `feature.dist_to_support_atr`

**How to read it.** The distance to support in ATRs: how many typical daily ranges sit between the close and the last swing low below it. Under 1 the close is at support (a stop just below it is tight); 2 to 3 is the usual working distance for a swing entry; above 5 a stop at support is a wide one.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| near support (a tight stop) | `lte 1` | soft | 0.5 | with feature.trend_state eq UPTREND |
| a stop at support within reach | `lte 3` | soft | 1 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Null at a one-year low (no support below) and when the ATR is 0.
- Support in a downtrend breaks: pair with feature.trend_state eq UPTREND.

Sources: The Turtle rules (a 2 ATR stop); docs/data/swing.md

### `rollup.retest@v1.breakout_level`

**How to read it.** The level the latest breakout cleared, in dollars: the highest high of the 20 sessions before the breakout session (the prior_high_20d of that day), within the last 60 sessions. Broken resistance is the textbook place for new support: a pullback that holds near it is the retest. rollup.retest@v1.retest_state says where the close stands against it.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| has a recent breakout level | `not_null` | hard | - | judge the retest with rollup.retest@v1.retest_state |

**When the reading lies**

- A level set by one spike high (a halt, a news candle) is easy to clear and weak support afterwards; check rollup.retest@v1.sessions_since_breakout and the volume on the day (rollup.momentum@v1.rel_volume).
- Null when no session of the last 60 closed above its prior 20-session high (retest_state NONE); a hard 'not_null' drops every name that has not broken out lately.
- The close alone defines the breakout here, with no volume condition (unlike feature.breakout_20d).

Sources: docs/data/swing.md; Edwards and Magee, Technical Analysis of Stock Trends (broken resistance turning into support); Donchian 20-session channel entries

### `rollup.retest@v1.breakout_date`

**How to read it.** The session of the latest 20-session breakout among the last 60 sessions. A date to show with the level: a breakout from last week is fresher than one from two months ago, and rollup.retest@v1.sessions_since_breakout is the same fact as a count.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| has a dated recent breakout | `not_null` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Null when there was no breakout in the last 60 sessions; an older breakout is not shown.
- Only the most recent breakout is kept: a name that broke out twice shows the second one.

Sources: docs/data/swing.md

### `rollup.gaps@v1.gap_above`

**How to read it.** Overhead gap: the lower edge, in dollars, of the nearest unfilled down gap that lies wholly above the close. A down gap is a session whose high stayed below the previous session's low, so nothing traded in between; the zone starts at that session's high (where price rising would enter it) and is filled only when a later high reaches the previous low. Gaps are the classic place price tends to come back to and meet supply; feature.dist_to_gap_above is the distance as a fraction.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| has an unfilled gap overhead | `not_null` | hard | - | threshold feature.dist_to_gap_above instead |

**When the reading lies**

- A gap is not a wall: price often runs straight through a zone, and a gap from earnings (rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date) is a news reaction, not a technical level.
- Null when no unfilled down gap lies wholly above the close in the last 253 sessions: all of them filled, or the close sits inside a partly filled zone. A hard 'not_null' drops the names with a clean overhead.
- Bars are split-adjusted, not dividend-adjusted: a split is never a gap, but a large special dividend can print as one; rollup.gaps@v1.gap_above_date says when it formed.

Sources: docs/data/swing.md; Edwards and Magee, Technical Analysis of Stock Trends (gaps); Murphy, Technical Analysis of the Financial Markets (gaps as support and resistance)

### `rollup.gaps@v1.gap_below`

**How to read it.** Gap support: the upper edge, in dollars, of the nearest unfilled up gap that lies wholly below the close. An up gap is a session whose low stayed above the previous session's high; the zone is entered from above at that session's low and is filled only when a later low reaches the previous high. Price pulling back into the zone is the usual 'gap fill' trade; feature.dist_to_gap_below is the distance as a fraction.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| has an unfilled gap below | `not_null` | hard | - | threshold feature.dist_to_gap_below instead |

**When the reading lies**

- Most up gaps eventually fill, but 'eventually' can be a year: an unfilled gap says nothing about when, and a breakaway gap on news may never fill; check rollup.gaps@v1.gap_below_date and rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date.
- Null when no unfilled up gap lies wholly below the close in the last 253 sessions (all filled, or the close is inside a partly filled zone).
- Bars are split-adjusted, not dividend-adjusted: a large special dividend can print as a gap.

Sources: docs/data/swing.md; Edwards and Magee, Technical Analysis of Stock Trends (gaps); Murphy, Technical Analysis of the Financial Markets
