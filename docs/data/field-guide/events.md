# Field guide: events

### `rollup.earnings@v1.days_to_earnings`

**How to read it.** Exchange sessions until the next report: 0 reports today, 1 tomorrow, 10 is two weeks out. Options price the report from about ten sessions before (the IV ramp accelerates in the last two or three) and lose that premium within a session after it. 'Avoid earnings' for a short option held a month is days_to_earnings above the holding period; 'play earnings' is under 5.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no earnings in the next N sessions (short options) | `gt 10` | soft | 2, EVENT_RISK | N is the holding period; 25 for a 30-day short option held to expiry; or feature.earnings_before_expiry eq false |
| earnings this week (an event play) | `lte 5` | hard | - | suggest rollup.earnings@v1.date_confirmed eq true when the date must be right |

**When the reading lies**

- Null means no report date is known for the name, not 'no report soon': ETFs have none, and a company's next date is often unknown until it announces it (two to four weeks before). A hard 'gt 10' therefore rejects every ETF and every company that has not announced; use soft with EVENT_RISK.
- Unconfirmed dates move: rollup.earnings@v1.date_confirmed false (the Nasdaq calendar never confirms) means the date is an estimate from last year's pattern and can shift by days.
- rollup.earnings@v1.earnings_time says before the open (pre) or after the close (post): 'reports tomorrow, pre' means the move happens before you can act.

Sources: IV ramp and crush around earnings: https://flashalpha.com/articles/iv-crush-explained-earnings-volatility-collapse and https://optionalpha.com/learn/iv-crush

### `feature.earnings_before_expiry`

**How to read it.** True when the next report falls on or before the nearest listed expiry: a short option held to that expiry carries the earnings move. False is the clean case for premium selling at the nearest expiry; for a later expiry read rollup.earnings@v1.days_to_earnings against its days.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no earnings before the nearest expiry | `eq false` | soft | 0, EVENT_RISK | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Null when the report date or the nearest expiry is unknown (no date announced, no stored chain): a hard 'eq false' rejects those names; soft with EVENT_RISK keeps them listed.
- The nearest expiry can be a weekly a few days out; a 30-day short option needs days_to_earnings against its own expiry, not this flag.

Sources: Site convention over the earnings calendar and the stored chains

### `rollup.dividend_schedule@v1.dividend_status`

**How to read it.** Whether a next ex-dividend date is known: SCHEDULED (the source has listed an ex-dividend date after the session) or NOT_ANNOUNCED (none yet: a payer between declarations, or a name that pays nothing). The source lists a date only about 30 days ahead, so NOT_ANNOUNCED on a quarterly payer is normal for most of the quarter.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a dividend date is known | `eq "SCHEDULED"` | soft | 0 | a hard filter drops every payer between declarations; pair a known date with rollup.dividend_schedule@v1.days_to_ex_date |

**When the reading lies**

- NOT_ANNOUNCED is not 'no dividend soon': a payer's next ex-date is unlisted until about a month before it. For a 30 to 60 day option the dividend can fall inside the term and still be unannounced; rollup.dividends@v2.last_ex_date and div_ttm say whether the name pays at all, and the interval between last_ex_date values says when the next is due.
- A date a later listing moved is read from the latest listing; a date withdrawn without another listed stays until it passes (an empty fetch stores nothing to say so).

Sources: Site convention (the corporate-actions window, config/site/sources.toml)

### `rollup.dividend_schedule@v1.next_ex_date`

**How to read it.** The next ex-dividend date after the session, as the stored corporate-actions listings knew it on the session: a share bought on or after that day does not receive the dividend. A date to show next to an expiry: a short call held through it risks early assignment, a short put misses the dividend.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| has a known ex-dividend date | `not_null` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Null (NOT_ANNOUNCED) means none is listed yet, not none coming; the source lists dates about 30 days ahead only. See rollup.dividend_schedule@v1.dividend_status.
- A declared date can move; the listing the session saw is the one shown, and the next night's can differ.
- Special dividends count: the date can be a one-off, not the regular payment (rollup.dividends@v2.div_count_ttm says how often the name usually pays).

Sources: Site convention (Massive dividends, ex_dividend_date)

### `rollup.dividend_schedule@v1.next_div_amount`

**How to read it.** The next dividend's cash amount per share, in today's share terms (a split since the listing divides it). $0.25 on a $100 stock is a 0.25% dividend for the period: against the call premium it is the amount an in-the-money short call is assigned early to capture. Compare with rollup.call_wing@v1.best_call_mid: a dividend larger than the call's remaining time value is the early-assignment case.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a dividend worth watching against a short call | `gte 0.25` | soft | 0.1 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Per share, not a yield: divide by rollup.price_stats@v2.close, or read feature.div_yield for the trailing annual yield.
- A declared amount can be revised before the ex-date; an irregular payer's special dividend can be many times the usual.
- Null when no ex-date after the session is known (NOT_ANNOUNCED).

Sources: Site convention; the early-exercise rule (Hull, Options, Futures and Other Derivatives: a call is exercised early just before an ex-dividend date when the dividend exceeds the time value)

### `rollup.dividend_schedule@v1.days_to_ex_date`

**How to read it.** Calendar days from the session to the next ex-dividend date, 1 or more (an ex-date today is not the next one). A known date is at most about 30 days out, because that is as far ahead as the source lists. 1 to 3 is the early-assignment window for a short in-the-money call; 10 to 30 is a dividend inside the term of a monthly option.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| an ex-dividend date within a week (early-assignment risk for a short call) | `lte 7` | soft | 3, EVENT_RISK | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Calendar days, not sessions: rollup.earnings@v1.days_to_earnings counts sessions. Compare with rollup.call_wing@v1.target_dte or rollup.nearest_expiry@v1.dte, which are calendar days.
- Null when no date is known (NOT_ANNOUNCED): a hard 'lte 5' rejects every name without a listed date; use soft with EVENT_RISK.

Sources: Site convention

### `rollup.dividend_schedule@v1.next_pay_date`

**How to read it.** The payment date of the next dividend, which follows its ex-date by a week or two. Shown with the ex-date; it matters to a holder of the shares, not to an option holder (assignment and the dividend capture turn on the ex-date).

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| has a payment date | `not_null` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Null when no ex-date is known (NOT_ANNOUNCED) and also when the source gives no payment date for a listed one: a null here with a known ex-date is a gap in the listing, not a missing dividend.

Sources: Site convention

### `feature.ex_div_before_expiry`

**How to read it.** True when the next known ex-dividend date falls on or before the target expiry (rollup.put_wing@v1.target_expiry, the 30 to 60 day expiry both option wings use). For a short call that is the early-assignment risk: a call near the money goes through the ex-date with the dividend as the incentive to exercise first. For a short put it is a dividend the stock drops by and the seller does not collect; for a covered call the shares collect it. False is the clean case; null is not.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no ex-dividend date before the wing's expiry | `eq false` | soft | 0, EVENT_RISK | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Null when no ex-date is known (the source lists dates about 30 days ahead, so for a 45 day expiry a dividend 35 days out is not listed yet) or no target expiry: an 'eq false' criterion never passes a null, so a payer whose date is unlisted is unknown, not clean. Pair with rollup.dividends@v2.last_ex_date: a quarterly payer last paid about three months ago is due.
- Early assignment needs an in-the-money call and a dividend above its time value: an out-of-the-money covered call at 0.25 delta is rarely assigned early. rollup.call_wing@v1.best_call_delta says how near the money it is.
- The target expiry is the put wing's: the same expiry as rollup.call_wing@v1.target_expiry unless one right has no quotes at it.

Sources: Hull, Options, Futures and Other Derivatives: early exercise of American calls just before an ex-dividend date

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no dividend before the nearest expiry | `eq false` | soft | 0, EVENT_RISK | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Null when no ex-date is known or no chain is stored; the nearest expiry is often within a week, inside the 30-day window, so a payer's date is usually listed when it matters here.

Sources: Site convention

### `rollup.fund_reference@v1.reference_instrument_id`

**How to read it.** For a leveraged or inverse fund, the one stock it tracks (TSLL: Tesla), as an instrument id. The fund has no events of its own: its earnings, ex-dividends and big moves are its reference's, scaled by its leverage (instrument.leverage, negative for an inverse fund). Null for a fund that tracks a basket, and for every instrument that is not a leveraged or inverse fund.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| funds that track one stock (their events are the stock's) | `not_null` | hard | - | pair with rollup.fund_reference@v1.reference_kind eq single_stock |

**When the reading lies**

- Null does not mean 'no events': a basket fund (rollup.fund_reference@v1.reference_kind index, sector or commodity) inherits macro and market-structure events and, for an index or sector, the earnings of its largest holdings; read the kind before the id.
- The link comes from the fund's stored holdings when they name exactly one stock, else from its name (rollup.fund_reference@v1.reference_source says which). A name rule is a parse of text: check a surprising link against the fund's own page, and rollup.fund_reference@v1.reference_status UNLISTED means the stock is named but not in the reference snapshot.
- A fund whose holdings are not stored yet (etf-holdings has not read it) is linked by its name only; a NO_REFERENCE status is a gap in our data, not a fact about the fund.

Sources: Site convention (ADR 0050 decision 2: the holdings table, ADR 0035, with a name rule as the fallback)

### `rollup.fund_reference@v1.reference_kind`

**How to read it.** What a leveraged or inverse fund tracks: single_stock (TSLL), index (TQQQ, SPXL), sector (SOXL, NAIL), commodity (UCO, gold and bitcoin funds) or none (a volatility fund, or a fund nothing is known of). It decides which events the fund inherits: a single stock's own calendar, the macro and market-structure events plus the top holdings' earnings, or macro events only.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| single-stock leveraged funds | `eq "single_stock"` | hard | - | then rollup.fund_reference@v1.reference_instrument_id is the stock |

**When the reading lies**

- 'none' covers two different things: a fund that tracks no stock, index or commodity (a volatility fund) and a fund we know nothing about; rollup.fund_reference@v1.reference_status says which (BASKET with kind none is volatility, NO_REFERENCE is unknown).
- index versus sector is read from the name's keywords first and the holdings' sectors second; a fund with neither is an index by default, so a thematic fund can read as an index until its holdings are stored.

Sources: Site convention (ADR 0050)

### `rollup.fund_reference@v1.reference_source`

**How to read it.** What settled a leveraged or inverse fund's link: holdings (its stored holdings name one stock, or show the basket) or name_rule (its name states the ticker or the kind). Null when nothing did. Holdings are the stronger evidence: they are what the fund owns today, while the name is a parse of text.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| links settled by the holdings, not a parse of the name | `eq "holdings"` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- name_rule is the only source for a fund whose holdings are not stored; after etf-holdings reads it the same fund can move to holdings and, rarely, to a different reference.

Sources: Site convention (ADR 0050)

**How to read it.** Why a leveraged or inverse fund's reference is what it is: LINKED (one stock found), BASKET (an index, sector or commodity fund: no one stock by design), UNLISTED (a stock is named but is not in the reference snapshot, so no id) or NO_REFERENCE (neither holdings nor the name say what it tracks).

**The criterion per intent**

|---|---|---|---|---|

- Only BASKET with rollup.fund_reference@v1.reference_kind none is a settled 'tracks nothing' (a volatility fund); NO_REFERENCE is a gap to close by reading the fund's holdings.

### `feature.ex_div_before_call_expiry`

**How to read it.** True when the next known ex-dividend date falls on or before the covered-call wing's target expiry (rollup.call_wing@v1.target_expiry). It is feature.ex_div_before_expiry read against the call side's own expiry, so it answers the covered-call writer's question directly: will the shares go ex-dividend while the call is open? If so, a call near the money is the one assigned early (the holder exercises to capture the dividend), and an out-of-the-money one leaves the dividend with the shares. False is the clean case; null means the date or the expiry is unknown, not that there is none.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no ex-dividend date before the covered call's expiry | `eq false` | hard | - | for a covered call; with rollup.call_wing@v1.best_call_delta lte 0.3 the dividend is usually kept even when true |
| a dividend to collect while the call is open | `eq true` | hard | - | a covered-call writer who wants the dividend: pair with rollup.call_wing@v1.best_call_delta lte 0.3 so the call is not assigned first |

**When the reading lies**

- Null when no ex-date is known yet (the source lists dates about 30 days ahead; a quarterly payer whose last ex-date, rollup.dividends@v2.last_ex_date, was about three months ago is due even when unlisted) or when the call wing has no target expiry.
- Early assignment needs the call in the money with a dividend above its remaining time value; check rollup.call_wing@v1.best_call_delta (above about 0.6 is at risk, 0.25 rarely).
- The two wings usually share the expiry; when they differ (one right has no quotes at the monthly), this flag and feature.ex_div_before_expiry can disagree, and this one is the covered-call answer.

Sources: Hull, Options, Futures and Other Derivatives: early exercise of American calls before an ex-dividend date

### `rollup.earnings_reaction@v1.reaction_excess_return`

**How to read it.** How far the last report moved the stock beyond SPY: the close after the report over the close before it, minus SPY's return over the same two sessions, as a decimal (0.05 is +5% against the market). It is the surprise measure of the post-earnings-drift literature: the price reaction stands in for the surprise, so it needs no analyst estimates. Large and positive means the market read the report as good news.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a big positive earnings reaction (post-earnings drift) | `gte 0.05` | hard | - | the small-cap drift screen uses 0.05; combine with rollup.earnings_reaction@v1.reaction_status eq OK and a market cap ceiling |

**When the reading lies**

- The window is two sessions around the report, so it holds the move whether the company reported before the open or after the close, and it can include other news of those days (a deal, an offering, a guidance change). Read the headline before treating a +20% as an earnings surprise.
- Null while the window is open (the session after the report has not closed), when a bar of the stock or of SPY is missing, or for a name with no report known: a hard criterion rejects those names. Pair it with rollup.earnings_reaction@v1.reaction_status eq OK to say why a name was left out.
- The reaction is already in the price when you can first buy: the drift studies measure what follows it, over weeks, and it has faded in larger stocks; check feature.market_cap and rollup.earnings_reaction@v1.pre_event_adv_usd_20d.

Sources: Brandt, Kishore, Santa-Clara and Venkatachalam, Earnings Announcements are Full of Surprises (2008); Martineau, Rest in Peace Post-Earnings Announcement Drift (2022)

### `rollup.earnings_reaction@v1.reaction_status`

**How to read it.** Whether the name's last report has a measured reaction: OK when the report's two-session window has closed with every bar it needs; NO_REPORT when no report is known by the session (a name with none, such as an ETF); INCOMPLETE when a report is known but its window is still open or a stock or SPY bar is missing.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| only names with a measured reaction | `eq "OK"` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- OK says the numbers exist, not that the report is recent: rollup.earnings_reaction@v1.sessions_since_reaction counts the sessions since it, and a name stays OK for months until its next report. Add sessions_since_reaction when only fresh reactions matter.
- A report that only an 8-K dated (not the earnings calendar) is missed until the earnings feed adds its results rows, so a recent reporter can read NO_REPORT.

Sources: Site convention (docs/data/features.md, earnings_reaction@v1)

### `rollup.earnings_reaction@v1.pre_event_adv_usd_20d`

**How to read it.** The average dollars traded a day (close times volume) over the 20 sessions before the last report, the report's own volume left out. It is the liquidity you could count on before the news; after a big reaction the current volume is inflated for days and says little about what a later exit can trade.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| liquid enough to trade before the news | `gte 2000000` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Null when the history is shorter than 20 sessions before the report or a session has no bar: a hard floor rejects new listings. Check instrument.listed_on.
- It is measured before the report that moved the stock, so a name whose volume dried up since is not flagged; compare rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d for today.

Sources: Chordia, Goyal, Sadka, Sadka and Shivakumar, Liquidity and the Post-Earnings-Announcement Drift (2009)

### `rollup.earnings_expected@v1.expected_basis`

**How to read it.** How the name's next report date is known: SCHEDULED (a report date the calendars had published by the session), PRIOR_YEAR (no date published, so the year-ago report plus 364 days, the same weekday a year on) or UNKNOWN (neither: no report in the past year either). It says how far to trust rollup.earnings_expected@v1.expected_report_date: an announced date is firm, a year-ago guess is usually within days but can be weeks out.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a report date known by the decision session (an announcement edge) | `in ["SCHEDULED", "PRIOR_YEAR"]` | hard | - | SCHEDULED alone keeps only announced dates, which are a few weeks ahead at most |

**When the reading lies**

- UNKNOWN is not 'no earnings': a recent listing or a name the calendars do not cover has no date by either route. A hard 'in SCHEDULED, PRIOR_YEAR' criterion leaves those names out, which is the intent for an edge that must know the date by the decision session.
- PRIOR_YEAR dates miss when a company changes its calendar (a fiscal-year change, a merger): a window built on one can fall on the wrong sessions.

Sources: Frazzini and Lamont, The Earnings Announcement Premium and Trading Volume (2007): the year-ago date as the expected one

### `rollup.earnings_reaction@v1.earnings_volume_ratio`

**How to read it.** How much trading a report usually draws: over the last four reports, the mean dollars traded a day in the report's two-session window divided by the 20-session average before it. 2 means the window trades twice the normal volume; 5 is a name whose reports are events. Frazzini and Lamont found the earnings announcement premium concentrated in the names whose reports draw the most volume.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| names whose reports draw heavy volume | `gte 2` | hard | - | the earnings announcement premium screen ranks by it instead of cutting |

**When the reading lies**

- It describes the last four reports, not the coming one, and it is null until four reports have a closed window: a name with a short history is left out by a hard criterion.
- A report window can carry other news (an offering, a deal), which inflates the ratio without the report being the cause.

Sources: Frazzini and Lamont, The Earnings Announcement Premium and Trading Volume (2007)

### `rollup.earnings_surprise@v1.surprise_status`

**How to read it.** Whether the name's last known report can be compared with its forecast. OK: it has a reported result and a forecast. NO_REPORT: no reported result is known by the session (a name that has not reported, or an ETF). NO_FORECAST: the report has a result, but the calendar held no forecast. SIGN_CONFLICT: the sign of the forecast is in doubt, so the report has no surprise.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| only names whose last report has a surprise | `eq "OK"` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- OK says the figure exists, not that the report is recent. rollup.earnings_surprise@v1.last_report_date gives its date, and a name stays OK for months until its next report.
- NO_FORECAST is common for small names that few analysts cover. A hard criterion on a surprise field leaves them out, and this field says why.
- SIGN_CONFLICT is rare (fewer than 1 report in 1,000). The calendar sometimes drops the minus sign of a loss-maker's forecast, so 1.00 beside a result of -0.67 may be a forecast of -1.00. We flag a report when the vendor's own surprise matches the negated forecast, and we never flip the forecast ourselves.
- The check needs the vendor's figure. Where the calendar gave none, a lost sign goes unseen, so treat a positive forecast beside a negative result with care.

Sources: Site convention (docs/data/features.md, earnings_surprise@v1)

### `rollup.earnings_surprise@v1.last_report_date`

**How to read it.** The date of the latest report whose result the calendar had stored by the session. It tells how fresh every other surprise figure is: a report from last week is news, one from last year is history.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| names with a stored report to date the surprise figures by | `not_null` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- It is the date the calendar lists. For a report after the close the market reacts the next day: rollup.earnings_reaction@v1.reaction_end_date gives the last session of the reaction window.
- Reports the calendar never stored are missing, so the date can be older than the real last report. Compare it with rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date.

Sources: Site convention (docs/data/features.md, earnings_surprise@v1)

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| names with a reported result to read the surprise from | `not_null` | hard | - | - |

Sources: Nasdaq earnings calendar (the stored events/earnings rows)

### `rollup.earnings_surprise@v1.last_eps_forecast`

**How to read it.** The consensus forecast of earnings per share, in dollars per share, that the latest report was measured against. It is the figure the calendar showed with the result. Microsoft's forecast for the report of 2026-07-29 was 4.21.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| names whose last report had a consensus forecast | `not_null` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Null when the calendar held no forecast, which is common for small names. The forecast shown with a result can be the day's own figure, not the one a week before.
- For a company expected to lose money the calendar can drop the minus sign. The value is shown as stored: rollup.earnings_surprise@v1.surprise_status reads SIGN_CONFLICT when the vendor's figure shows it happened.

Sources: Nasdaq earnings calendar (the stored events/earnings rows)

### `rollup.earnings_surprise@v1.last_surprise_eps`

**The criterion per intent**

Sources: Site convention (docs/data/features.md, earnings_surprise@v1)

### `rollup.earnings_surprise@v1.last_surprise_pct`

**How to read it.** How far the latest report landed from the consensus forecast, in percent of the forecast: (reported - forecast) / |forecast|. A value of +12.6 is a report 12.6% above the forecast (Microsoft, 2026-07-29: 4.74 against 4.21). A value of -87.5 is a report far below it (Tesla, 2026-07-22: 0.04 against 0.32). We compute it from the two stored numbers, not from the vendor's figure.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a clear earnings beat on the last report | `gte 5` | hard | - | pair with rollup.earnings_surprise@v1.surprise_status eq OK and an upper bound, because a small forecast inflates the figure |

**When the reading lies**

- A small forecast makes the percentage huge: a result of 0.30 against a forecast of 0.06 is +400. It is null below a forecast of five cents; there rollup.earnings_surprise@v1.last_surprise_eps gives the size. Bound a threshold, for example below 100.
- Most reports beat: the middle name beats by about 6. Compare a surprise with rollup.earnings_surprise@v1.surprise_median_4q, not with zero.
- The price reaction can disagree with the surprise: a beat on weak guidance falls. Check rollup.earnings_reaction@v1.reaction_excess_return.
- Null when there is no forecast, the forecast is below five cents in size, or the report is a SIGN_CONFLICT. A hard criterion rejects those names, so pair it with rollup.earnings_surprise@v1.surprise_status eq OK.

Sources: Bernard and Thomas, Post-Earnings-Announcement Drift: Delayed Price Response or Risk Premium? (1989); Livnat and Mendenhall, Comparing the Post-Earnings Announcement Drift for Surprises Calculated from Analyst and Time Series Forecasts (2006)

### `rollup.earnings_surprise@v1.surprise_median_4q`

**How to read it.** The middle percent surprise of the last four known reports that can be compared. A median of +5 means the name cleared its forecast by about 5% in a typical report. A negative median means it fell short more often than not. Apple's reads +5.1, Tesla's -30.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a name that habitually clears its forecast | `gte 3` | hard | - | with rollup.earnings_surprise@v1.reports_4q eq 4, so the median rests on a full year |

**When the reading lies**

- It is a median, so one report with a placeholder result or a small forecast cannot move it. Reports with a forecast below five cents are left out, so it can be null while rollup.earnings_surprise@v1.beats_4q is not.
- It covers the last four reports however far back they are. With rollup.earnings_surprise@v1.reports_4q below 4, or a rollup.earnings_surprise@v1.last_report_date a year old, the history is thin.

Sources: Livnat and Mendenhall, Comparing the Post-Earnings Announcement Drift for Surprises Calculated from Analyst and Time Series Forecasts (2006)

### `rollup.earnings_surprise@v1.beats_4q`

**How to read it.** How many of the last four known reports came in above their forecast, from 0 to 4. It counts in dollars, so a small forecast still counts. 4 means every report beat (Apple, Microsoft). 0 means none did, which holds for about 6 names in 100 with four reports. A report equal to its forecast is not a beat.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a name that beat its forecast in every one of its last four reports | `gte 4` | hard | - | with rollup.earnings_surprise@v1.reports_4q eq 4 |

**When the reading lies**

- The count is out of rollup.earnings_surprise@v1.reports_4q, which can be below 4: 3 of 3 is a better record than 3 of 4.
- Beating is the norm: about 3 names in 10 with four reports beat every time, so 4 of 4 is a mild signal, not a rare one.

Sources: Site convention (docs/data/features.md, earnings_surprise@v1)

### `rollup.earnings_surprise@v1.reports_4q`

**How to read it.** How many of the last four known reports can be compared, meaning a result and a forecast whose sign is not in doubt. It is the base of rollup.earnings_surprise@v1.beats_4q. 4 is a full record; 1 or 2 is a newer listing or a thinly covered name.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| four reports with a forecast behind the beat count and the median | `eq 4` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- A low count is not a bad sign. It says the beat count and the median rest on few reports, so below 3 treat them as noise.

Sources: Site convention (docs/data/features.md, earnings_surprise@v1)

### `rollup.earnings_revisions@v1.revision_status`

**How to read it.** Whether the next report's forecast can be compared with an earlier one. OK: a snapshot of the calendar from a week ago holds the same fiscal quarter. BUILDING: no stored snapshot reaches back that far yet (they start on 2026-10-02). NO_PRIOR: one does, but it holds no row of this quarter for the name. NO_FORECAST: the next report has no forecast. NO_NEXT: no next report is known.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| only names whose forecast revision over a week can be measured | `eq "OK"` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- OK says a 7-day comparison exists, not that the forecast moved: most names read OK with a revision of 0. rollup.earnings_revisions@v1.forecast_revision_7d gives the size.
- NO_NEXT includes names that have reported lately. The calendar lists reports about 60 days ahead, so a company that reported last week can have none listed yet.
- rollup.earnings_revisions@v1.forecast_revision_30d is null until about 2026-11-01, whatever the status says.

Sources: Site convention (docs/data/features.md, earnings_revisions@v1)

### `rollup.earnings_revisions@v1.next_eps_forecast`

**How to read it.** The consensus forecast of earnings per share, in dollars per share, for the next report, as the calendar showed it on the session. Apple's was 1.99 on 2026-10-15. The revisions are measured on this figure.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| names whose next report has a consensus forecast | `not_null` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- A forecast built from one or two analysts moves with each of them. Read rollup.earnings_revisions@v1.estimate_count beside it.
- Null when the calendar lists the report with no forecast, which is common for small names.

Sources: Nasdaq earnings calendar (the stored events/earnings rows)

### `rollup.earnings_revisions@v1.estimate_count`

**How to read it.** How many analysts' estimates the next report's forecast averages. Apple's forecast averages 7, Microsoft's 13. A count of 1 or 2 is a thinly covered name. More estimates make a forecast steadier and a revision more meaningful.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a forecast with several analysts behind it | `gte 5` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- It counts the estimates the calendar's vendor holds, not every analyst on the Street.
- Null when the calendar gave no count for the next report.

Sources: Nasdaq earnings calendar (the stored events/earnings rows)

### `rollup.earnings_revisions@v1.forecast_revision_7d`

**How to read it.** How much the consensus forecast for the next report moved in a week, in percent of the old forecast, for the same fiscal quarter. A value of +5 means analysts raised it by 5%. A value of -12.5 means they cut it: Tesla's went from 0.24 to 0.21 in the week to 2026-10-15. Zero is common, since most forecasts do not move in a week.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| analysts raising the forecast for the coming report | `gte 2` | hard | - | with rollup.earnings_revisions@v1.estimate_count gte 5 and rollup.earnings_revisions@v1.revision_status eq OK |

**When the reading lies**

- A small forecast makes a big percentage: a move from 0.06 to 0.15 is +150. The figure is null when the old forecast is below five cents. Read it beside rollup.earnings_revisions@v1.next_eps_forecast.
- A forecast whose sign the calendar lost on one night shows as a revision of about -200 or +200. Treat a move that large between a positive and a negative forecast as a data error.
- One analyst moving a forecast built from two estimates shifts it a lot. Read rollup.earnings_revisions@v1.estimate_count, and prefer 5 or more.
- Null while rollup.earnings_revisions@v1.revision_status is not OK, and when the fiscal quarter changed between the two snapshots: a quarter roll is not a revision. A hard criterion rejects those names.

Sources: Gleason and Lee, Analyst Forecast Revisions and Market Price Discovery (2003); Chan, Jegadeesh and Lakonishok, Momentum Strategies (1996): the revision-in-earnings-forecasts effect

### `rollup.earnings_revisions@v1.forecast_revision_30d`

**How to read it.** How much the consensus forecast for the next report moved in 30 days, in percent of the old forecast, for the same fiscal quarter. It reads like rollup.earnings_revisions@v1.forecast_revision_7d over a longer window, so a steady drift shows where a week looks flat. It is null for every name until about 2026-11-01, because the snapshots start on 2026-10-02.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| names whose next forecast rose 3% or more in 30 days | `gte 3` | hard | - | history starts 2026-10-02, so the field is null before about 2026-11-01 |

**When the reading lies**

- The same quarter has to be in the snapshot 30 days back. A name whose quarter appeared on the calendar later reads null.
- A long window mixes old and new moves. For a report under a week away, a flat 7-day figure inside a large 30-day one means the move is over.

Sources: Gleason and Lee, Analyst Forecast Revisions and Market Price Discovery (2003)

### `rollup.earnings_revisions@v1.estimate_count_change_30d`

**How to read it.** How many more (positive) or fewer (negative) analysts' estimates the next report's forecast averages than 30 days ago, for the same fiscal quarter. A value of +3 means three more analysts now publish a forecast. It is null until about 2026-11-01, when the snapshots reach back 30 days.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| names gaining analyst coverage for the next report | `gt 0` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Coverage grows toward a report as analysts publish, so a rise is the normal course. Compare names reporting in the same week.
- Null when either snapshot gave no count, or the quarter is not in the snapshot 30 days back.

Sources: Site convention (docs/data/features.md, earnings_revisions@v1)

### `rollup.filing_activity@v1.filing_count_5d`

**How to read it.** How many 8-K and 8-K/A filings (the SEC's current-report form: results, deals, management changes, material agreements) the company had accepted from the session before the last 5 sessions through the session, as known on it (an 8-K filed after the close or over a weekend is counted from the first session it was known). 0 means it filed nothing in the week; 1 is routine for most companies; 3 or more is a busy corporate week.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no company filing in the week | `eq 0` | hard | - | or feature.news_free_5d eq true, which also checks the earnings windows |

**When the reading lies**

- Null means the table cannot say none: no filing is stored for the company by the session (never fetched, or never filed), the stored history starts after the window, or no filing is stored from the session itself yet (the nightly filings step failed or was skipped: a zero then would wrongly read as no news). A hard 'eq 0' therefore drops those names, which is the point.
- A filing is counted from the session its acceptance made it public (after 16:00 New York time on a session it still counts that session); a filing accepted after the session never appears on it, so a past session reads the same as it did then.
- News with no 8-K (an analyst note, a press story, a rumour, a competitor's results) is not counted: a zero is 'no company filing', not 'no news'. rollup.filing_activity@v1.earnings_in_5d adds the report windows, and feature.news_free_5d combines both.
- An Item 2.02 results release is an 8-K, so an earnings week shows at least one filing.

Sources: Form 8-K, SEC investor bulletin: https://www.sec.gov/files/form8-k.pdf

### `rollup.filing_activity@v1.earnings_in_5d`

**How to read it.** True when an earnings report's reaction window, the session before the report to the session after it, touches the last 5 sessions: the company reported in them, or on the session just before the first of them (its next-day reaction then falls inside). False is a week with no report; null when no report is known for the company at all.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no earnings in the week | `eq false` | hard | - | or feature.news_free_5d eq true, which also checks the 8-K filings |

**When the reading lies**

- Null means no report date is known for the name (an ETF, or a company the calendar never listed), not 'no report': a hard 'eq false' drops it.
- Only reports known on the session count; a report date the calendar published later is not in a past session's reading.
- The window follows the dates of the earnings calendar (rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date; its rows, not the 8-K results releases): a report the calendar missed shows in rollup.filing_activity@v1.filing_count_5d as an 8-K instead.

Sources: Bernard and Thomas (1989), Post-earnings-announcement drift: the E-1 to E+1 window

### `rollup.dividend_months@v1.div_month_hits_3y`

**How to read it.** Out of the last 3 years, in how many the name had an ex-dividend date in the calendar month after the session's month: 3 is a name that pays in that month every year (a quarterly payer's regular month), 0 a name that never did. Special distributions are left out.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pays in the coming month, every year | `gte 3` | soft | 1 | or rollup.dividend_months@v1.expected_div_month_flag eq true for at least one year |

**When the reading lies**

- Null for a name with fewer than 240 bars in the last 252 sessions and no hit: too new, or the corporate-actions history may not reach it, to call it a non-payer of that month. A payer with a hit has a value whatever its age; the count then covers only the years it has.
- It reads the past only: a new dividend the company announced or a changed schedule is not here until its ex-date passes. rollup.dividend_schedule@v1.next_ex_date holds the declared date, but only 30 days ahead.
- The month is the one after the session's month, not the next 30 days: on the 28th it is four days away, on the 1st a month away. A weekly or monthly payer hits every month.
- A payer's ex-date moves by a few days and can cross a month end: a hit of 2 of 3 can be a date that slipped.

Sources: Hartzmark and Solomon (2013), The dividend month premium, Journal of Financial Economics

### `rollup.dividend_months@v1.expected_div_month_flag`

**How to read it.** True when the name had an ex-dividend date in the calendar month after the session's month in at least one of the last 3 years: a dividend is expected in the coming month, the case the dividend-month premium (stocks earning more in the months they are expected to pay) is about. False is a month the name has not paid in within 3 years; null when its history cannot say.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a dividend is expected next month | `eq true` | hard | - | the month is the one after the session's, as known on the session |

**When the reading lies**

- Null for a name too new, or without bars covering the history (fewer than 240 of the last 252 sessions) and with no hit; false only for a name whose history was long enough to see.
- Expected from the pattern, not announced: a payer that cut or skipped its dividend still reads true, and one that began paying reads false until a year has passed. Check rollup.dividend_schedule@v1.dividend_status.
- True is common (any quarterly payer is true for a third of the months, a monthly payer for all): rank by rollup.dividend_months@v1.div_month_hits_3y rather than treat it as rare.

Sources: Hartzmark and Solomon (2013), The dividend month premium, Journal of Financial Economics

### `feature.news_free_5d`

**How to read it.** True when the company filed no 8-K (rollup.filing_activity@v1.filing_count_5d is 0) and no earnings report's reaction window touched the last 5 sessions (rollup.filing_activity@v1.earnings_in_5d is false), as known on the session: nothing identifiable explains a move in the week. False when either happened; null when it cannot be told (no filing stored for the name, or no earnings report known) and neither term already says false.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no 8-K and no earnings in the week | `eq true` | hard | - | null (unknown) names drop out |

**When the reading lies**

- It is the absence of company filings and report dates, not of news: a press story, an analyst call, a rumour, a peer's results or a macro release still moves a name in a 'news free' week.
- Null is common before the filings backfill, on a night the filings step did not store the session, and for names with no stored filing; a hard 'eq true' keeps only names where the absence is known.
- A past session is read as it was: a filing accepted after it, or a report date published later, never changes it.

Sources: Gervais, Kaniel and Mingelgrin (2001), The high-volume return premium, Journal of Finance

### `rollup.max_return@v1.max_ret_21d`

**How to read it.** The best single day of the last 21 sessions, close to close, as a fraction: 0.10 is a name that gained 10% in one day within the month. Below about 0.03 is a quiet month for a large cap; 0.10 and above is a lottery-like day; 0.20 and above is a name traded as a lottery ticket. Negative means every day lost, so a heavy decline.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| avoid lottery-like names | `lte 0.05` | soft | 0.02 | a filter for avoid-lists; the decile rank is the signal in the literature |

**When the reading lies**

- A name with one huge news day (a takeover bid, an FDA decision, earnings) tops the list without being a lottery stock; check rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date and rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move.
- Low-priced and thin names post 10% days on noise; gate on rollup.price_stats@v2.close and rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d.
- Signed: a 15% crash day does not count here (rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move takes the largest absolute move).
- Null after a gap in the last 22 closes or a shorter history.

Sources: Bali, Cakici and Whitelaw (2011), Maxing out: stocks as lotteries and the cross-section of expected returns, Journal of Financial Economics

### `rollup.earnings_neutral_reversal@v1.ret_ex_earnings_20d`

**How to read it.** The last 20 sessions' return with the daily returns of every earnings reaction window left out (the close before a report to the close after it): the month's move that earnings did not make. Equal to rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_20d when no report fell in the window. Read as the short-term reversal input: a large negative value is a name that fell on no earnings news, a candidate for a bounce.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a month-long fall that earnings did not cause | `lte -0.1` | soft | 0.03 | a reversal candidate; compare rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_20d |

**When the reading lies**

- Null when a close is missing in the 21, the history is shorter, or no earnings report is known for the name at all (the calendar cannot say it did not report); a name the calendar missed reads as if it had none.
- It removes the report's own two returns, not the days around it: drift after the window (rollup.earnings_reaction@v1.sessions_since_reaction) is still in.
- Other news (a deal, a downgrade, guidance outside the report) is still in the return: rollup.filing_activity@v1.filing_count_5d shows 8-Ks of the last week only.
- Only reports known on the session are removed, so a past session is read as it was.

Sources: Jegadeesh (1990), Evidence of predictable behavior of security returns; Chan (2003), Stock price reaction to news and no-news; Savor (2012), Stock returns after major price shocks

### `rollup.earnings_neutral_reversal@v1.earnings_days_excluded`

**How to read it.** How many of the last 20 daily returns an earnings reaction window left out of rollup.earnings_neutral_reversal@v1.ret_ex_earnings_20d: 0 means no report fell in the window, 2 is one report with both its reaction days, 1 a report on the session itself (its next day has not happened) or on a non-session day.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|

**When the reading lies**

- More than 2 means two reports in a month (a correction or a rare double date): treat the return with care.

Sources: Bernard and Thomas (1989), Post-earnings-announcement drift: the E-1 to E+1 window

### `rollup.episode_behaviour@v1.beta_252d`

**How to read it.** The slope of the name's daily returns on SPY's over the trailing 252 sessions: 1 moves with the market, 1.5 moves half again as much (a 5% market fall is a 7.5% fall here), 0.5 half as much, below 0 against it. Under 0.8 is defensive (utilities, staples, health care), 0.8 to 1.2 average, above 1.2 high beta (technology, discretionary, financials, small caps). A position's market risk is its weight times its beta.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| defensive (low beta) | `lte 0.8` | soft | 0.1 | with rollup.episode_behaviour@v1.corr_252d gte 0.3 so the low beta is a steady name, not noise |
| high beta (a market play with leverage) | `gte 1.3` | soft | 0.1 | - |
| market-like | `between [0.8, 1.2]` | soft | 0.1 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Beta is average co-movement, not downside behaviour: a low-beta name can fall as much as the market in a panic when correlations go to one. The episode drawdowns (dd_covid_2020 and the others) are the direct measure of that.
- Trailing a year: a name that changed character (a merger, a new business) carries the old beta for months.
- Null for young listings (under 200 overlapping returns) and when SPY's bars are missing.

Sources: Sharpe (1964) and the CAPM; beta bands as practitioners use them: https://www.tastylive.com/concepts-strategies/beta

### `rollup.episode_behaviour@v1.corr_252d`

**How to read it.** The correlation of the name's daily returns with SPY's over the trailing 252 sessions, -1 to 1: above 0.7 the name is mostly a market bet, 0.3 to 0.7 ordinary, under 0.3 it has a life of its own (gold miners, biotech, special situations), negative is rare and usually an inverse fund. Beta says how much, correlation says how reliably.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| moves with the market | `gte 0.7` | soft | 0.1 | - |
| its own story (low correlation) | `lte 0.3` | soft | 0.1 | exclude inverse funds with instrument.is_inverse eq false |

**When the reading lies**

- A low beta with a low correlation is noise, not defence: the regression explains little. Defensive names have a low beta with a decent correlation.
- Correlations rise toward 1 in a crash, whatever the trailing year said; diversification by low correlation is weakest when it is needed.
- Null for young listings and when SPY's bars are missing.

Sources: Standard portfolio theory (Markowitz); the correlation-in-crashes observation (Longin and Solnik 2001)

### `rollup.episode_behaviour@v1.dd_covid_2020`

### `rollup.episode_behaviour@v1.recovery_sessions_covid_2020`

**How to read it.** Sessions from the Covid trough (2020-03-23) until the close first regained the pre-episode high: 0 means the trough itself was at or above it, about 100 is the index's own recovery (SPY regained its high in August 2020), 250 is a year. Resilience in one number: how long holders waited to be whole.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| recovered as fast as the index or faster | `lte 120` | soft | 30 | - |
| recovered at all within the horizon | `not_null` | soft | 0 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Null means not recovered within 400 sessions of the trough, or the session is before the trough: 'never came back' and 'too early to say' look the same, and a hard criterion rejects both.
- A fast recovery of a shallow drawdown is unremarkable; read with rollup.episode_behaviour@v1.dd_covid_2020.

Sources: docs/market-regime-plan.md; S&P 500 recovery times after bear markets (StockCharts, Bear markets and drawdowns)

### `rollup.episode_behaviour@v1.recovery_sessions_hikes_2022`

**How to read it.** Sessions from the 2022 trough (2022-10-12) until the close first regained the pre-episode high (the early-2022 peak). SPY took about 300 sessions (January 2024); many growth names took longer or have not, which is what a null here means.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| recovered faster than the index | `lte 250` | soft | 50 | - |
| recovered within the horizon | `not_null` | soft | 0 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Null is 'not within 400 sessions' as much as 'not yet': for this episode the horizon has passed, so null reads as 'did not recover within about 19 months'.

Sources: docs/market-regime-plan.md

### `rollup.episode_behaviour@v1.recovery_sessions_tariffs_2025`

**How to read it.** Sessions from the tariff-shock trough (2025-04-08) until the close first regained the pre-episode high. SPY needed about 30 sessions; a liquid high-beta name often recovered in a similar time, a name that had something else wrong took much longer.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| recovered with the index | `lte 45` | soft | 15 | - |
| recovered within the horizon so far | `not_null` | soft | 0 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- The horizon (400 sessions after the trough) runs into late 2026: a null can still be 'not yet'.

Sources: docs/market-regime-plan.md
