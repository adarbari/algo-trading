### `rollup.analyst_ratings@v1.rating_strong_buy`

**How to read it.** How many analysts rate the stock a strong buy in the newest month the vendor lists. A count of 10 is ten analysts. Compare it with rating_count for the share, not the count alone.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| several analysts rate it a strong buy | `gte 3` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Analysts rate on different dates, and the vendor does not date each rating. A count mixes views from last week and from a year ago.
- A few analysts make a thin consensus. Read rollup.analyst_ratings@v1.rating_count beside the counts.
- Ratings follow the price: analysts upgrade after a rise and downgrade after a fall. A strong rating can be the lag of a rally.
- Null with ratings_status NOT_COVERED or NOT_FETCHED reads as not covered by our analyst data. The vendor does not answer for every name, and the nightly rotation has not asked every name yet.
- Null with ratings_status STALE: the newest snapshot is over 45 days old, so no value is served.

Sources: Finnhub analyst recommendation trends, stored nightly in analyst/estimate_snapshots (docs/adr/0068-analyst-data-vendors.md)

### `rollup.analyst_ratings@v1.rating_score`

**How to read it.** The mean analyst rating on a scale of 1 to 5. A 5 is every analyst at strong buy, 3 is hold, 1 is strong sell. Most covered stocks sit between 3.5 and 4.5, since analysts seldom publish a sell.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| most analysts rate it a buy | `gte 4.0` | hard | - | - |
| analysts neutral or better | `gte 3.0` | soft | 0.3 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Analysts lean to buy, so a score of 3.0 is already a cautious consensus. Compare the score with other names, not with the midpoint of the scale.
- A score from a handful of analysts moves with each of them. Read rollup.analyst_ratings@v1.rating_count beside it.
- The score says what analysts rate, not what the stock will do. A high score after a rally is often the lag of the price.
- Null when no analyst rates the name, and with ratings_status STALE, NOT_COVERED or NOT_FETCHED. A hard criterion rejects those names.

Sources: Finnhub analyst recommendation trends, stored nightly in analyst/estimate_snapshots (docs/adr/0068-analyst-data-vendors.md)

### `rollup.analyst_ratings@v1.ratings_status`

**How to read it.** Whether the analyst ratings of the name can be used. OK: a snapshot at most 45 days old. STALE: older, so no value is served. NOT_COVERED: the vendor has no ratings for the name. NOT_FETCHED: we have not asked yet.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ratings are current | `eq "OK"` | hard | - | - |
| leave out stale snapshots | `ne "STALE"` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- NOT_COVERED and NOT_FETCHED read the same to a trader, as not covered. The admin cause tells them apart, and NOT_FETCHED ends as the nightly rotation reaches the name.
- STALE is not the old value: a snapshot past 45 days serves nothing, so a vendor outage shows as STALE rather than as old counts.
- OK does not say the score has a 90-day change: rollup.analyst_ratings@v1.rating_score_change_90d is null until our history is that long.

Sources: Finnhub analyst recommendation trends, stored nightly in analyst/estimate_snapshots (docs/adr/0068-analyst-data-vendors.md)

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| analysts raising this year's EPS estimate | `gte 2` | hard | - | with rollup.analyst_revisions@v1.revisions_status eq OK and rollup.analyst_estimates@v1.eps_fy1_analysts gte 5 |

**When the reading lies**

- Our history starts on 2026-10-19, so this is null until about 2026-11-18. Read rollup.analyst_revisions@v1.revisions_status for whether the windows exist yet.
- The comparison is for the same fiscal year, matched on its year end. At the year roll a year the old snapshots never listed has no past value, so the field reads null, not a jump.
- A small old estimate makes a big percentage: the field is null when the old EPS is under five cents in size. Read it beside rollup.analyst_estimates@v1.eps_fy1.
- One analyst moving a thin estimate shifts it a lot. Read rollup.analyst_estimates@v1.eps_fy1_analysts, and prefer 5 or more.
- Null with revisions_status NOT_COVERED or NOT_FETCHED reads as not covered by our analyst data. The vendor does not answer for every name, and the nightly rotation has not asked every name yet.

### `rollup.analyst_revisions@v1.revisions_status`

**How to read it.** Whether the estimate revisions of the name can be measured. OK: a snapshot of the same fiscal year exists for 30, 60 and 90 days back. BUILDING: an earlier one exists, but not for every window. NO_PRIOR: none holds this year. STALE, NOT_COVERED and NOT_FETCHED are the estimates' own status.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| only names whose revisions over every window can be measured | `eq "OK"` | hard | - | - |
| leave out stale snapshots | `ne "STALE"` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- OK says the comparisons exist, not that the estimate moved: most names read a revision near 0. Read the revision fields for the size.
- BUILDING lasts until 2027-01-17 for every name, since our history starts on 2026-10-19. A night the rotation missed can also hold a covered name there.
- NO_PRIOR also covers a year that the older snapshots never listed, as at the fiscal year roll, and a name whose first estimate year is unknown.
- Null with revisions_status NOT_COVERED or NOT_FETCHED reads as not covered by our analyst data. The vendor does not answer for every name, and the nightly rotation has not asked every name yet.
- Null with revisions_status STALE: the newest snapshot is over 21 days old, so no estimate and no revision is served.

Sources: Financial Modeling Prep analyst estimates, stored nightly in analyst/estimate_snapshots (docs/adr/0068-analyst-data-vendors.md)

### `feature.forward_pe`

**How to read it.** Close divided by the analysts' average EPS estimate for the current fiscal year (FY1): 15 means the price is fifteen times this year's expected earnings. Lower is cheaper on expected profit.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cheap on expected earnings | `lte 15` | soft | 3 | with feature.forward_eps_growth gte 0 so a low multiple is not a profit expected to shrink |
| a forward multiple exists | `gt 0` | hard | - | drops names with no estimates, a forecast loss or an ADR. On a night without the analyst table it rejects every row, though the run reads complete |

**When the reading lies**

- At the fiscal year roll FY1 moves when the annual report becomes public, so the ratio jumps once. That is a change of year, not of the estimate: check rollup.analyst_estimates@v1.fy1_period_end.
- Null with estimates_status NOT_COVERED or NOT_FETCHED reads as not covered by our analyst data. The free vendor plan covers few names, so a hard criterion keeps only a covered subset.
- A consensus of one or two analysts is an opinion, not a consensus: check rollup.analyst_estimates@v1.eps_fy1_analysts.
- Analysts' EPS usually leaves out one-off items that the reported EPS keeps, so this ratio and feature.pe_ratio are measured differently. Compare them as a rough guide only.
- Null for a forecast loss and for an ADR, whose EPS is per ordinary share. A hard criterion drops both.

Sources: Damodaran, Investment Valuation: the price earnings ratio, trailing and forward; Financial Modeling Prep analyst estimates, stored nightly in analyst/estimate_snapshots (docs/adr/0068-analyst-data-vendors.md)

### `feature.forward_eps_growth`

**How to read it.** The analysts' FY1 EPS estimate divided by the diluted EPS of the last twelve months, minus 1: 0.20 is 20% more earnings per share. Below 0 is a profit expected to shrink, below -1 a forecast loss.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| earnings expected to rise | `gte 0.1` | soft | 0.05 | with feature.forward_pe lte 30 so the rise is not already in the price |

**When the reading lies**

- At the fiscal year roll FY1 moves when the annual report becomes public, so the figure jumps once. That is a change of year, not of the estimate: check rollup.analyst_estimates@v1.fy1_period_end.
- Analysts' EPS usually leaves out one-off items that the reported EPS keeps, so a one-off can make the figure look high or low. Check feature.eps_cagr_3y for the reported trend.
- Null when the last twelve months were a loss: a growth rate off a loss is not shown, so a hard criterion drops turnarounds. Null for an ADR and for a stale reported EPS.
- Null with estimates_status NOT_COVERED or NOT_FETCHED reads as not covered by our analyst data. The free vendor plan covers few names, so a hard criterion keeps only a covered subset.

Sources: Damodaran, Investment Valuation: expected growth in earnings and analyst estimates; Financial Modeling Prep analyst estimates, stored nightly in analyst/estimate_snapshots (docs/adr/0068-analyst-data-vendors.md)

### `feature.forward_revenue_growth`

**How to read it.** The analysts' FY1 revenue estimate divided by the revenue of the last twelve months, minus 1: 0.10 is 10% more sales. Below 0 is revenue expected to shrink.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sales expected to rise | `gte 0.08` | soft | 0.04 | with feature.forward_eps_growth gte 0 so the sales rise reaches profit |

**When the reading lies**

- At the fiscal year roll FY1 moves when the annual report becomes public, so the figure jumps once. That is a change of year, not of the estimate: check rollup.analyst_estimates@v1.fy1_period_end.
- Null for an ADR, because a foreign filer's revenue is in its own currency. Null when the reported revenue is stale or not positive.
- Null with estimates_status NOT_COVERED or NOT_FETCHED reads as not covered by our analyst data. The free vendor plan covers few names, so a hard criterion keeps only a covered subset.
- A consensus of one or two analysts is an opinion, not a consensus: check rollup.analyst_estimates@v1.revenue_fy1_analysts.

Sources: Damodaran, Investment Valuation: expected growth in revenues and analyst estimates; Financial Modeling Prep analyst estimates, stored nightly in analyst/estimate_snapshots (docs/adr/0068-analyst-data-vendors.md)

### `feature.target_upside`

**How to read it.** The average analyst price target divided by the close, minus 1: 0.15 means the average analyst puts the price 15% above today's close. Negative means the close is above the target.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| room to the analysts' target | `gte 0.15` | soft | 0.05 | with feature.forward_eps_growth gte 0 so the room is not on a shrinking profit |

**When the reading lies**

- Targets are the vendor's current ones on the day it was asked and carry no date of their own: check rollup.analyst_targets@v1.targets_age_days.
- Targets usually look twelve months ahead and move after the price does, so a large upside is often a recent fall. It is not a forecast of the price.
- Null with targets_status NOT_COVERED or NOT_FETCHED reads as not covered by our analyst data. The free vendor plan covers few names, so a hard criterion keeps only a covered subset.
- A wide gap between the highest and the lowest target means analysts disagree: check rollup.analyst_targets@v1.target_high and rollup.analyst_targets@v1.target_low.

Sources: Brav and Lehavy (2003), An empirical analysis of analysts' target prices, Journal of Finance; Financial Modeling Prep price-target consensus, stored nightly in analyst/estimate_snapshots (docs/adr/0068-analyst-data-vendors.md)

### `feature.ev_ebit`

**How to read it.** Enterprise value divided by operating income over the last twelve months: 12 means the whole business, debt included, costs twelve years of operating profit before interest and tax. Low is cheap: under 8 is cheap against most of the market, over 25 expensive. It sees through debt and depreciation, unlike a price-to-earnings.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cheap against operating profit | `lt 10` | soft | 2 | illustrative screen, not a published cut-off: Loughran and Wellman sort names into groups by the multiple and set no level, and 10 is a round figure for the cheap end; a loss-making company has no multiple, so a hard rule drops it |

**When the reading lies**

- Null for a loss: a company with no positive EBIT has no multiple, so a hard 'lt 10' drops every unprofitable name. A bank, broker or insurer has no EBIT either (see feature.ev_ebitda for the same gap).
- A cyclical company at a profit peak looks cheap right before earnings fall. Compare it with its own history as well as with other names.
- A stale income statement gives null (rollup.income_statement@v2.is_status STALE). Enterprise value uses today's price against a statement up to four months old.

Sources: Loughran and Wellman (2011), New evidence on the relation between the enterprise multiple and average stock returns, JFQA 46

### `rollup.earnings_date_shift@v1.earnings_shift_known_date`

**How to read it.** The session the next report date was first stored in a calendar known by the session: when the market could first have seen that the date had moved. A date that moved a week later and first appeared on the 3rd is known from the 3rd, even if the move was decided earlier. It is set only for a date at least 7 days from the same weekday a year ago.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| names whose report date moved by a week or more | `not_null` | hard | - | null means the date did not move, or no year-ago report to compare with |

**When the reading lies**

- The history starts with the first stored calendar snapshot. A date first stored then says nothing about when it became known (vendor backfills carry no time), so it is never an event.
- Dates are the calendar's scheduled ones; the Nasdaq calendar does not confirm a date, so a move can be an estimate being revised, not a decision.
- Null with no next date, no report a year ago to compare with, or a shift under 7 days.

Sources: Site convention over the stored earnings calendars (ADR 0050)

### `rollup.earnings_date_shift@v1.sessions_since_earnings_shift`

**How to read it.** Exchange sessions from the session the shifted report date became known up to the session: 0 means it became known today; 5 is a week ago. It measures how fresh the news of the move is; the event reads best in the first sessions after it.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a report date that moved in the last five sessions | `lte 5` | hard | - | the news is freshest in the first sessions after the move |

**When the reading lies**

- Null without a shift of at least 7 days (rollup.earnings_date_shift@v1.earnings_shift_direction null), so a hard criterion drops every name with a normal date.
- A later date resets the count if the date moves again, so a name whose date keeps changing shows small values for the wrong reason; check rollup.earnings_date_shift@v1.earnings_shift_known_date.
- The date is scheduled, not confirmed; the report can still move. With few shifted names in a window the evidence is thin.

Sources: Site convention over the stored earnings calendars (ADR 0050)
