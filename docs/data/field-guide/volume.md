### `rollup.volume@v1.session_volume`

**How to read it.** The shares traded on the session, split-adjusted as of the session. 0 means no trades (known, not missing). Its size has no universal scale: a typical S&P 500 stock trades 1 to 10 million shares a day, a mega cap 20 to 100 million, a small Nasdaq name 100,000 to 1 million, and under about 100,000 shares a day a name is thin. Read it against the name's own normal: today over rollup.volume@v1.adv_shares_20d near 1 is an ordinary day, 1.5 a busy one, 3 or more an event (rollup.momentum@v1.rel_volume and rollup.volume@v1.volume_z_20d do that comparison for you). For dollars traded use rollup.volume@v1.dollar_volume.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| traded today (not a stale quote) | `gt 0` | hard | - | a name with no trades today has volume 0, which is known, not null |

**When the reading lies**

- Earnings days trade 3 to 10 times the normal volume by nature; check rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date before reading a big number as unusual.
- Quarterly expiry Fridays, index rebalances and the Russell reconstitution (late June) inflate volume with no news behind it; a big number with a flat close (rollup.momentum@v1.ret_5d near 0) is usually one of these.
- A split multiplies the share count: the stored value is adjusted as of the session, but a share threshold set before a split means half as much after a 2-for-1. Prefer rollup.volume@v1.dollar_volume or rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d for liquidity gates.

Sources: Volume (finance), Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volume_(finance)

### `rollup.volume@v1.dollar_volume`

**How to read it.** Close x shares traded on the session, in dollars: one day of rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d. Low to high: under about $1M a day a name is illiquid (a few orders move the price); $1M to $5M is thin, fine only for small orders; $5M to $50M is tradable for most individual positions; $50M and above is liquid; large caps trade $500M to several billion and the largest names $10B or more. A normal day sits within about 0.7 to 1.5 times the name's 20-session average; outside that band something happened.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| traded a lot of dollars today | `gte 50000000` | soft | 10000000 | for a liquidity gate use rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d gte the same value |

**When the reading lies**

- One day is noisy: an index event or an earnings day can be 5 times normal. Gate liquidity on rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d, the 20-session average, and use this for 'today'.
- Low-priced names can show a large share count and still a small dollar figure; gate on rollup.price_stats@v2.close as well.

Sources: Market liquidity and average daily dollar volume: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_liquidity

### `rollup.volume@v1.adv_shares_20d`

**How to read it.** The average shares traded per session over the last 20 sessions (about a month), the session included. Low to high: under 100,000 shares a day is thin, 100,000 to 1 million moderate, 1 to 10 million typical for an S&P 500 stock, above 10 million heavily traded. It is a share count, so it is a position-sizing figure (an order under 1% of it moves the price little) more than a liquidity grade: 500,000 shares a day is liquid for a $100 stock ($50M) and thin for a $2 one ($1M). For a dollar grade use rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| liquid in shares | `gte 500000` | hard | - | pair with rollup.price_stats@v2.close; a dollar gate is rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d |

**When the reading lies**

- An earnings day or an index event inside the window lifts the average for 20 sessions; check rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date.
- Share counts change at a split: the value is adjusted as of the session, but a threshold in shares means a different dollar amount before and after; prefer rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d for a gate that survives splits.
- Null after a gap in the last 20 sessions or a shorter history (a new listing); a hard criterion then rejects the name.

Sources: Average daily trading volume, Investopedia: https://www.investopedia.com/terms/a/averagedailytradingvolume.asp

### `rollup.volume@v1.volume_ratio_5d_20d`

**How to read it.** The average volume of the last 5 sessions over the average of the last 20 (both including the session): 1 is a normal week, below 0.7 a quiet one, below 0.6 volume is drying up (the contraction that often precedes a breakout in a base), above 1.3 volume is expanding and above 2 the week held an event.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| volume drying up | `lte 0.6` | soft | 0.1 | the contraction of a base; pair with feature.range_20d_pct low and feature.trend_state eq UPTREND |
| volume expanding | `gte 1.3` | soft | 0.2 | participation is rising; check rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date for an event inside the week |

**When the reading lies**

- Holiday weeks (Thanksgiving, the last week of December) are quiet across the market; a low ratio then is the calendar, not the stock. Compare with the market or wait a week.
- An earnings day 6 to 20 sessions ago inflates the 20-session base, so the ratio reads 'drying up' when the week is merely normal; check rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date.
- An earnings day or an index event in the last 5 sessions lifts the ratio with no trend behind it; a high ratio with a flat close (rollup.momentum@v1.ret_5d near 0) is usually one of these.
- Null after a gap in the last 20 sessions, a shorter history, or no volume at all over the 20.

Sources: Volume contraction before breakouts (O'Neil, How to Make Money in Stocks: volume drying up in a base); Volume analysis, StockCharts ChartSchool: https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/overlays/volume-by-price

### `rollup.volume@v1.volume_z_20d`

**How to read it.** How unusual the session's volume is, in standard deviations of the 20 sessions before it: 0 is an average day, 1 to 2 a busy one, 3 and above an event-sized day (earnings, news, an index event or a capitulation), negative a quiet one. Same base as rollup.momentum@v1.rel_volume, but scaled by how much the volume usually varies, so a steady name's spike scores higher than an erratic name's.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| volume climax | `gte 3` | soft | 0.5 | or feature.volume_climax eq true; check rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date |
| a quiet session | `lte -1` | soft | 0.3 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Earnings days are routinely 3 or more; check rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date before reading a high z-score as news.
- Expiry Fridays and index rebalances print high z-scores with no news; a high value with a flat close (rollup.momentum@v1.ret_5d near 0) is usually one of these.
- Volume is skewed (big days are far bigger than quiet days are small), so z-scores above 3 are commoner than a normal curve suggests; rank by it rather than read it as a probability.
- On thin names a single block trade is a 5-sigma day; gate on rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d.
- Null after a gap in the last 21 sessions, a shorter history, or when the 20 sessions before all had the same volume (no spread to scale by).

Sources: Standard score, Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_score; Abnormal trading volume around events: Campbell, Grossman and Wang (1993), Trading volume and serial correlation in stock returns

### `rollup.volume@v1.up_volume_share_20d`

**How to read it.** The share of the last 20 sessions' volume that traded on up days (a close above the previous close), 0 to 1: 0.5 is balanced, 0.6 and above is accumulation (buyers did the heavy trading), 0.4 and below distribution. A session closing unchanged counts in the total only. It is the 20-session form of on-balance volume's idea: volume follows the side in control.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| accumulation | `gte 0.6` | soft | 0.05 | or feature.volume_bias eq ACCUMULATION; pair with feature.trend_state eq UPTREND |
| distribution | `lte 0.4` | soft | 0.05 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- One heavy day can be most of the window: an earnings gap up on 10 times normal volume pushes the share above 0.6 on its own. Check rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date and rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move.
- It counts closes, not how far they moved: twenty small up days and one large down day read as accumulation. Pair with rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_20d.
- Null after a gap in the last 21 sessions (the first session needs the close before it), a shorter history, or no volume at all.

Sources: On-balance volume (Granville), Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On-balance_volume; Accumulation / distribution, StockCharts ChartSchool: https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/technical-indicators-and-overlays/technical-indicators/accumulation-distribution-line

### `rollup.volume@v1.cmf_20d`

**How to read it.** Chaikin money flow over 20 sessions, -1 to 1: where each close sat in its day's range (+1 at the high, -1 at the low), weighted by volume. Above 0 buyers closed the heavy days near the highs; above 0.1 is a usual 'money flowing in' reading, above 0.25 strong; below -0.1 money flowing out. Read with rollup.volume@v1.up_volume_share_20d: the share counts up and down closes, CMF where in the range they landed.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| money flowing in | `gte 0.1` | soft | 0.05 | pair with rollup.volume@v1.up_volume_share_20d gte 0.5 |
| money flowing out | `lte -0.1` | soft | 0.05 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- CMF ignores gaps: a stock gapping down 10% and closing at the day's high scores +1 for that day. On a gap day (rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move large, rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date recent) read rollup.volume@v1.up_volume_share_20d instead.
- A day with high equal to low (a halt, a thin name with one print) counts as 0; thin names need a gate on rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d.
- Null after a gap in the last 20 sessions, a shorter history, or no volume at all.

Sources: Chaikin money flow, StockCharts ChartSchool: https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/technical-indicators-and-overlays/technical-indicators/chaikin-money-flow-cmf; Chaikin Analytics, money flow: https://www.chaikinanalytics.com/

### `feature.volume_dry_up`

**How to read it.** True when the last 5 sessions traded under 60% of the 20-session average volume (rollup.volume@v1.volume_ratio_5d_20d below 0.6): the quiet before a breakout in a base, when sellers have stopped. False is a normal or busy week (most names, most weeks: the ratio usually sits between 0.8 and 1.2); null when the ratio is unknown. The threshold is params.max_ratio; 0.5 is a stricter dry-up, 0.7 a looser one, and the ratio itself (rollup.volume@v1.volume_ratio_5d_20d) ranks how dry.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| volume drying up | `eq true` | hard | - | with feature.trend_state eq UPTREND; the breakout is then feature.breakout_20d |

**When the reading lies**

- Holiday weeks are quiet for everyone; a dry-up then says nothing about the stock.
- A heavy earnings day 6 to 20 sessions ago inflates the base and fakes a dry-up; check rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date.
- A dry-up in a downtrend is a stock nobody wants, not a base; gate on feature.trend_state eq UPTREND.

Sources: O'Neil, How to Make Money in Stocks: volume drying up near the lows of a base

### `feature.volume_climax`

**How to read it.** True when the session's volume is 3 or more standard deviations above the 20 sessions before it (rollup.volume@v1.volume_z_20d at least 3): an event-sized day. False is everything from a quiet day to a busy one (a z-score under 3; 1 to 2 is a busy but ordinary day); null when the z-score is unknown. After a long decline a climax with a close off the lows can mark capitulation; after a long rise, exhaustion. Most climaxes are simply news, and a typical name has a few a year (earnings alone gives four).

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| volume climax | `eq true` | hard | - | pair with the sign of rollup.momentum@v1.ret_5d to tell capitulation from a blow-off |

**When the reading lies**

- Earnings days are climaxes by nature; check rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date before reading one as capitulation.
- Expiry Fridays and index rebalances print climaxes with no news; a flat close (rollup.momentum@v1.ret_5d near 0) points to one.
- On thin names a single block trade is a climax; gate on rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d.

Sources: Selling climax and buying climax (Wyckoff method): https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-analysis/wyckoff-analysis-articles/the-wyckoff-method-a-tutorial

### `feature.volume_bias`

**How to read it.** Which side did the heavy trading over the last 20 sessions, from rollup.volume@v1.up_volume_share_20d (the share of volume on up days, 0 to 1): ACCUMULATION when 60% or more of the volume traded on up days (buyers in control), DISTRIBUTION at 40% or less (sellers in control), NEUTRAL between, which is the usual state (most names most months sit between 0.4 and 0.6). Null when the share is unknown. The bounds are params.accumulation_at and params.distribution_at; the share itself ranks how strong the bias is.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| accumulation | `eq "ACCUMULATION"` | hard | - | with feature.trend_state eq UPTREND |
| distribution | `eq "DISTRIBUTION"` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- One earnings gap on heavy volume can decide the label on its own; check rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date and rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move.
- It counts up and down closes, not how far they moved; confirm with rollup.volume@v1.cmf_20d and rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_20d.

Sources: Accumulation / distribution, StockCharts ChartSchool: https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/technical-indicators-and-overlays/technical-indicators/accumulation-distribution-line
