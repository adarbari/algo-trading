### `rollup.iv30@v1.iv30`

**How to read it.** Our 30-calendar-day at-the-money implied volatility as a fraction (ADR 0021): the forward ATM vols of the two expiries around 30 days, each from the two strikes around the forward with calls and puts averaged, interpolated in total variance. The number rollup.iv_history@v2.iv30 stores and feature.iv_rank ranks. 0.20 is a calm large cap, 0.30 to 0.50 an ordinary single stock, above 0.80 a biotech, a meme or an event.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| high implied volatility (ours, absolute) | `gte 0.4` | soft | 0.05 | prefer feature.iv_rank gte 0.5 (relative to the name's own year) |
| low implied volatility (ours, absolute) | `lte 0.25` | soft | 0.05 | with feature.atr_pct gte 0.005 |

**When the reading lies**

- A report inside 30 days inflates it: the earnings move is priced in and drops out after the report (20% to 60% of it in a session). Check rollup.earnings@v1.days_to_earnings.
- Built from after-close quotes that pass the quality filters; on a thin chain few quotes survive (rollup.iv30@v1.n_quotes_used) and one wide quote moves the number. SINGLE_EXPIRY (rollup.iv30@v1.iv30_status) means one expiry only, flat vol.
- The feed's IV30 (rollup.iv30@v1.iv30_cboe) and IBKR's (rollup.ibkr_iv@v1.iv30_ibkr) use other methods; they differ by a few points on liquid names and by more on thin ones, which is why feature.vrp_iv30 takes the lower.

Sources: ADR 0021 (our IV30); https://optionalpha.com/learn/iv-crush for the earnings behaviour

### `rollup.iv30@v1.iv30_cboe`

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| high implied volatility (the feed's) | `gte 0.4` | soft | 0.05 | prefer feature.iv_rank |

**When the reading lies**

- A fraction here, percentage points in the chain group: 'IV over 40%' is gte 0.40 on this field.
- Null when the feed gives no IV30 (no underlying quote, a thin chain); ours may still have one, and vice versa.

Sources: Cboe's IV30 definition

### `rollup.iv30@v1.iv30_status`

**How to read it.** Why our IV30 has a value or not: OK (two expiries), SINGLE_EXPIRY (one usable expiry, flat vol, still a value), or the first failing step: NO_SPOT, NO_CHAIN, NO_EXPIRY (none 7 to 90 days out), NO_QUOTES, WIDE_SPREADS (every ATM quote wider than 35% of mid), ILLIQUID (no quote met the open-interest or volume floor), IV_FAILED (the inversion did not converge).

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| an IV30 from two expiries | `eq "OK"` | soft | 0 | the near miss is SINGLE_EXPIRY |

**When the reading lies**

- WIDE_SPREADS and ILLIQUID are facts about the name's options, not the run: an IV that is null for that reason is a name whose options are not worth pricing. NO_CHAIN and NO_SPOT can be the day's fetch.
- SINGLE_EXPIRY values are less reliable near 30 days when the one expiry is far from it.

Sources: ADR 0021; features/rollups/options/iv30.py

### `rollup.skew@v1.skew_status`

**How to read it.** Whether the 30-day skew has a value: OK (two expiries around 30 days), SINGLE_EXPIRY (one usable expiry, its vols unchanged, still a value), or the first failing step: NO_SPOT, NO_CHAIN, NO_EXPIRY (none 7 to 90 days out), NO_ATM (no ATM vol from the two-sided, tight quotes) or NO_WING (an ATM vol, but the 25-delta strikes are not bracketed on a side).

### `rollup.skew@v1.skew`

**How to read it.** The normalised 25-delta skew at 30 days: (put vol at delta -0.25 minus call vol at +0.25) over the ATM vol. Positive means puts are richer than calls, the normal shape for stocks: about 0.10 to 0.20 is ordinary, above about 0.25 puts are rich (downside protection is expensive: collar and put-spread sellers like it, a put buyer pays up), near 0 flat, negative call-rich (a takeover, a squeeze, a meme name where the upside is what is bid). Dividing by ATM vol makes a 20-vol and an 80-vol name comparable.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| puts rich (a steep put skew) | `gte 0.25` | soft | 0.05 | with rollup.skew@v1.skew_status eq OK; prefer rollup.skew_history@v1.skew_rank_252d gte 0.8 once a rank exists |
| call-rich skew (calls priced above puts) | `lte -0.05` | soft | 0.05 | rare; check for a pending takeover or a squeeze (rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move) |

**When the reading lies**

- The level of a skew is partly structural: index ETFs sit high, single names lower, and a name's own history matters more than the cross-section; rollup.skew_history@v1.skew_rank_252d says where it is for the name, but is null for the first 60 sessions of stored chains.
- Our delta at each contract's own IV, from after-close mids of tight quotes; one stale wing quote moves it. Read rollup.skew@v1.skew_status and compare rollup.skew@v1.ne_skew, which should agree in sign.
- An earnings report inside 30 days lifts the ATM vol and the wings differently, so the ratio can move with no change in sentiment; check rollup.earnings@v1.days_to_earnings.
- Null when the status is not OK or SINGLE_EXPIRY.

Sources: Risk reversal and skew: https://www.investopedia.com/terms/r/risk-reversal.asp; Normalising the risk reversal by ATM vol: Sinclair (2013), Volatility Trading

### `rollup.skew_history@v1.skew_rank_252d`

**How to read it.** Skew rank, 0 to 1: where today's 30-day skew sits between the year's lowest and highest. Above 0.8 is a stretched put premium for the name (puts are as rich as they have been this year), below 0.2 the flattest. The year the rank needs is built from stored chains, which start on 2026-10-02: every name reads null until it has 60 sessions with a skew (about the end of 2026), and a full year around October 2027.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a stretched put premium for the name | `gte 0.8` | soft | 0.1 | with rollup.skew_history@v1.skew_rank_status in PROVISIONAL or FULL |

**When the reading lies**

- One spike sets the year's maximum and squashes later readings, as for IV rank; rollup.skew_history@v1.skew_percentile_252d is the robust cross-check.
- A short window is a short sample: PROVISIONAL (rollup.skew_history@v1.skew_rank_status) means under a year of skews, so a rank near 1 can be an ordinary day.
- Null while skew_rank_status is UNKNOWN, with no skew today, or when every skew in the window is equal.

Sources: https://flashalpha.com/articles/iv-rank-vs-iv-percentile-difference-which-matters (the same rank construction)

### `rollup.skew_history@v1.skew_percentile_252d`

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|

**When the reading lies**

- Null while the rank status is UNKNOWN (fewer than 60 sessions with a skew), with no skew today, or without an earlier skew.

Sources: https://flashalpha.com/articles/iv-rank-vs-iv-percentile-difference-which-matters

### `rollup.skew_history@v1.history_days`

**How to read it.** Sessions of the 252-session window with a 30-day skew, today included: 60 is the floor for a rank, 252 a full year. Chains are stored from 2026-10-02, so counts are short for every name until a year has passed.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| enough history for a skew rank | `gte 60` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- A session with no skew (no chain, or a chain with no 25-delta wing) is a gap, not filled.

Sources: Site convention (docs/data/positioning.md OP3)

### `rollup.skew_history@v1.skew_rank_status`

**How to read it.** How complete the skew history is for the rank: UNKNOWN below 60 sessions with a skew (no rank), PROVISIONAL below 252, FULL from 252. Every name is UNKNOWN until about the end of 2026.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a rank over a full year | `eq "FULL"` | soft | 0 | the near miss is PROVISIONAL |

**When the reading lies**

- PROVISIONAL over a short window makes an ordinary skew rank near 1; read rollup.skew@v1.skew with it.

Sources: Site convention (docs/data/positioning.md OP3)

### `rollup.iv_term@v1.iv_term_status`

**How to read it.** Whether the term points have values: OK (both), or the first failing step: NO_SPOT, NO_CHAIN, NO_NEXT (no ATM vol at the next expiry) or NO_90D (no pair of expiries 30 to 180 days out around 90 days with ATM vols). The side that exists keeps its value.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| both term points priced | `eq "OK"` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- A single listed expiry in the 30 to 180 day window is not a 90-day vol: NO_90D, where rollup.iv30@v1.iv30_status would say SINGLE_EXPIRY.

Sources: Site convention (features/rollups/positioning/iv_term.py)

### `rollup.iv_term@v1.iv_next`

**How to read it.** The at-the-money vol at the next expiry (the first listed one at least one day out, weeklies included), as a fraction: iv30's method on that single expiry. Above rollup.iv30@v1.iv30 when an event sits in the nearest expiry; feature.term_ratio_next_30 is that comparison.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a high near-term vol | `gte 0.6` | soft | 0.1 | with feature.term_ratio_next_30 gte 1.3 to know it is an event, not the name's level |

**When the reading lies**

- A few days to expiry make it noisy, and pin risk bids the at-the-money options; read rollup.skew@v1.ne_dte for how many days out it is.
- Null when the status is NO_SPOT, NO_CHAIN or NO_NEXT.

Sources: Site convention (docs/data/positioning.md)

### `rollup.iv_term@v1.iv_90d`

**How to read it.** Our constant 90-day at-the-money vol as a fraction: the two expiries around 90 days among 30 to 180 days out (standard monthlies first), each priced as in rollup.iv30@v1.iv30, interpolated in total variance. The back of the term structure: the vol the market charges for a quarter.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a high long-dated vol | `gte 0.4` | soft | 0.05 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Needs both expiries of the pair with ATM vols: a thin back month makes it null (NO_90D) and the term ratio with it.
- A name's back vol sits near its long-run level; a front vol far above it is stress or an event, far below it is calm.

Sources: Site convention (docs/data/positioning.md)

### `feature.term_ratio_30_90`

**How to read it.** The front of the vol term structure over the back: our 30-day ATM vol over the 90-day one. 1 is flat; above 1 is backwardation (the front richer than the back: an event or stress is priced near, calendars sell the front), above about 1.1 a clear one; below 1 is contango, the normal shape, below about 0.9 steep (calendars buy the front cheaply).

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| backwardation (the front richer than the back) | `gte 1.1` | soft | 0.05 | with rollup.earnings@v1.days_to_earnings gt 30 to rule out a report |
| steep contango (the front cheap against the back) | `lte 0.9` | soft | 0.05 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- An earnings date inside 30 days (rollup.earnings@v1.days_to_earnings) lifts the 30-day vol and the ratio with it, with no stress in the market; feature.term_ratio_next_30 isolates the front expiry.
- The two vols come from different expiries and spot rules (the 30-day one reads the feed's price, the 90-day one the close), a small error against a 0.1 threshold.
- Null when either vol is null; a single usable expiry is not a 90-day vol.

Sources: Term structure of implied volatility and calendar spreads: Natenberg, Option Volatility and Pricing

### `feature.term_ratio_next_30`

**How to read it.** The front-expiry premium: the ATM vol at the next expiry over the 30-day vol. 1 is no premium; above 1.2 an event (earnings, a ruling) is priced into the nearest expiry, above about 1.3 clearly so. After the event the premium drops out.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| an event priced into the nearest expiry | `gte 1.3` | soft | 0.1 | with rollup.skew@v1.ne_dte gte 7 |

**When the reading lies**

- A next expiry a few days out is noisy and pin-bid: read rollup.skew@v1.ne_dte and floor it near 7 days.
- A name with weeklies has a different next expiry from one with monthlies only, so ratios of the two are not like for like.
- Null when either vol is null.

Sources: Event vol in the front expiry: https://www.tastylive.com/concepts-strategies/implied-volatility-earnings
