# Field guide: put wing, walls and expiries

### `rollup.put_wing@v1.wing_status`

**How to read it.** Whether the best put was found: OK (its delta is inside 0.08 to 0.15), OUTSIDE_BAND (a best put exists between 0.05 and 0.35 delta but outside the band; rollup.put_wing@v1.delta_band_distance says how far), or the first failing step: NO_SPOT, NO_CHAIN (no puts), NO_EXPIRY (none 30 to 60 days out), NO_STRIKE (no put with our delta in 0.05 to 0.35).

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a best put in the 8 to 15 delta band | `eq "OK"` | soft | 0 | the near miss is OUTSIDE_BAND; score rollup.put_wing@v1.delta_band_distance instead of gating |

**When the reading lies**

- OUTSIDE_BAND is not a failure: the VRP scanner scores the distance rather than rejecting (owner, 2026-10-04). A hard 'eq OK' is stricter than the preset.
- NO_STRIKE on a liquid name usually means a high-priced stock whose strike ladder skips the band, or every far put lacked a two-sided quote (rollup.put_wing@v1.n_unpriced).

Sources: docs/screeners/vrp-scanner.md (owner decisions 2026-10-04)

### `rollup.put_wing@v1.target_expiry`

**How to read it.** The expiry of the best put: the one nearest 45 calendar days among those 30 to 60 days out, standard monthlies first (open interest concentrates there; a weekly nearer 45 days is usually thin). A date to show with the strike; rollup.put_wing@v1.target_dte is the days.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| has a wing expiry | `not_null` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Null when no expiry sits 30 to 60 days out (a name with monthlies only can have a gap right after an expiry).
- Different from rollup.option_liquidity@v1.target_expiry (near 35 days) and rollup.nearest_expiry@v1.expiry_date (the front one).

Sources: docs/screeners/vrp-scanner.md

### `rollup.put_wing@v1.target_dte`

**How to read it.** Calendar days to the best put's expiry, 30 to 60 by construction and near 45: the premium-selling sweet spot where time decay is fast and gamma still moderate. Nearer 30 the position decays faster but a move hurts more; nearer 60 it is slower and calmer.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| around 45 days to expiry | `between [35, 55]` | soft | 5 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- The VRP scanner holds to expiry, so a report before it is the risk to check: feature.earnings_before_expiry is for the nearest expiry, not this one; compare rollup.earnings@v1.days_to_earnings (sessions) with this (calendar days, roughly 1.4 sessions per 2 days).

Sources: tastylive on 45 days to expiry; docs/screeners/vrp-scanner.md

### `rollup.put_wing@v1.n_unpriced`

**How to read it.** Puts at the target expiry that have no delta of ours (no two-sided quote, or the implied-volatility inversion failed): they can never be the best put. A few far wings are normal; a large share of the expiry means the chain was quoted one-sided after the close and the wing columns rest on few strikes.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the expiry's puts were priced | `lte 10` | soft | 10 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Compare with rollup.put_wing@v1.n_strikes: many unpriced and few band strikes says 'quote problem', not 'no market'.

Sources: Site convention (features/rollups/options/put_wing.py)

### `rollup.put_wing@v1.n_strikes`

**How to read it.** How many strikes at the target expiry have a put with our delta inside 0.08 to 0.15: the choice available in the band. 0 means no put in the band (the best put is outside it), 1 or 2 is normal for a strike ladder in $5 steps, 4 or more is a finely struck, usually liquid, name.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a strike inside the band | `gte 1` | soft | 1 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- 0 with wing_status OUTSIDE_BAND is a ladder gap, common on high-priced stocks; the best put is then the nearest outside the band and its distance is scored.

Sources: Site convention

### `rollup.put_wing@v1.wing_oi`

**How to read it.** Open interest summed over the puts inside the 8 to 15 delta band at the target expiry: the standing depth of the wing a short put is sold into. Under 500 is thin, a few thousand is tradable, tens of thousands is a popular wing. 0 when no put is in the band.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a wing with depth | `gte 2000` | soft | 0.5 x the threshold, LIQUIDITY_RISK | - |

**When the reading lies**

- A single strike can hold all of it (one institutional hedge); rollup.put_wing@v1.best_put_oi is the chosen strike alone, and the two together say whether the depth is spread across the band.
- Standing positions, not today's activity: rollup.put_wing@v1.wing_volume is the day's trades.

Sources: https://www.tastylive.com/concepts-strategies/options-liquidity

### `rollup.put_wing@v1.wing_volume`

**How to read it.** Contracts traded on the session across the puts in the 8 to 15 delta band at the target expiry: whether the wing trades today. A few hundred is active for one expiry's wing; 0 on a quiet day is common even on liquid names.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the wing trades | `gte 200` | soft | 0.5 x the threshold, LIQUIDITY_RISK | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Daily and noisy; read with rollup.put_wing@v1.wing_oi.

Sources: https://www.tastylive.com/concepts-strategies/options-liquidity

### `rollup.put_wing@v1.wing_spread_pct`

**How to read it.** The median bid-ask spread as a share of mid across the puts in the 8 to 15 delta band, on the stored after-close quotes. Far wings quote wide in percent because their mids are small: 0.15 is good for a wing, 0.30 is usual on a mid-cap, above 0.50 the band is not really made.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a wing that is made (scored) | `lt 0.2` | score | 0.2 | full points under 20% of mid, none at 40% |

**When the reading lies**

- A $0.05 spread on a $0.20 put is 25%: a wide percentage can be a nickel. Read rollup.put_wing@v1.best_put_mid with it.
- After-close quotes; judge live. Null when no put is in the band.

Sources: https://www.thetaedge.ai/blog/assess-liquidity-trading-options

### `rollup.put_wing@v1.best_put_strike`

**How to read it.** The strike of the best put: among puts with our delta between 0.05 and 0.35, the one nearest the 8 to 15 delta band, then the highest cash-secured return on capital, then the higher open interest, then the lower strike. With the expiry it identifies the contract to quote live; it is a level, shown next to the close and support (rollup.swing_levels@v1.swing_low, rollup.oi_walls@v1.put_wall).

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| has a candidate put | `not_null` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- A strike above the put wall or above swing support is a wing that sits inside the zone where the stock has recently stopped falling; a strike below both has those levels as a cushion.
- Null with wing_status NO_STRIKE or no target expiry.

Sources: docs/screeners/vrp-scanner.md

### `rollup.put_wing@v1.best_put_delta`

**How to read it.** Our delta of the best put, negative, between -0.35 and -0.05: -0.10 is a put with roughly a 10% chance of finishing in the money, the VRP scanner's 8 to 15 delta target (-0.08 to -0.15). Nearer -0.05 the premium is tiny; nearer -0.35 the put is a stock position in waiting.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| in the 8 to 15 delta band | `between [-0.15, -0.08]` | soft | 0.05 | or score rollup.put_wing@v1.delta_band_distance lte 0 with tolerance 0.20, as the preset does |

**When the reading lies**

- Ours (ADR 0021), from the stored mid with the Treasury curve and the dividend yield; the feed's delta can differ by a few hundredths. rollup.put_wing@v1.delta_band_distance is the band check in one number.
- A delta computed on an after-close quote on a name that moved after the close is already stale by that move; confirm live.

Sources: docs/screeners/vrp-scanner.md; Hull on delta as a rough probability

### `rollup.put_wing@v1.best_put_iv`

**How to read it.** The implied volatility of the best put from its mid, as a fraction: 0.45 is 45% a year. A far put's IV is above the at-the-money IV (the skew: puts are bid for protection), so it reads higher than rollup.iv_history@v2.iv30 by 5 to 15 points on an ordinary stock. The premium the VRP scanner collects is this number against realised movement.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a rich wing (sell premium) | `gte 0.4` | soft | 0.05 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Skew is sector- and regime-dependent: a wing IV 20 points over the ATM IV says protection is expensive (a crowded or feared name), not that the stock will move that much.
- A report before expiry inflates it like every IV; check rollup.earnings@v1.days_to_earnings.

Sources: Carr and Wu (2009); practitioner notes on put skew

### `rollup.put_wing@v1.best_put_mid`

**How to read it.** The best put's mid price, (bid + ask) / 2, in dollars per share: the premium a seller can expect near the middle of the market; times 100 per contract. Under $0.20 the spread is most of it; $0.50 to $2.00 is the usual wing premium on a $50 to $200 stock.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a premium worth the spread | `gte 0.3` | soft | 0.1, LIQUIDITY_RISK | - |

**When the reading lies**

- The mid of an after-close quote; the live mid can be far from it. The return on capital (rollup.put_wing@v1.best_put_roc) is the comparable number across prices.

Sources: docs/screeners/vrp-scanner.md

### `rollup.put_wing@v1.best_put_roc`

**How to read it.** The best put's cash-secured return on capital: premium over the strike (mid / strike), as a fraction for the expiry's term. 0.01 is 1% for about 45 days (roughly 8% a year); 0.02 is rich and usually means a high-volatility name; under 0.005 the trade does not pay for its risk.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a worthwhile return on capital | `gte 0.01` | soft | 0.003 | - |
| rank by return on capital | `gte 0.015` | score | 0.01 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- A high ROC is a high IV: it is the compensation for the name's risk, not free money. Pair with feature.vrp_iv_hv_ratio so the IV is high against what the stock actually does.
- Per term, not annualised: compare only across similar rollup.put_wing@v1.target_dte.

Sources: docs/screeners/vrp-scanner.md (the put wing's ROC ordering)

### `rollup.oi_walls@v1.wall_status`

**How to read it.** Whether the open-interest walls were found: OK (a call wall at or above spot and a put wall at or below), PARTIAL (one side only), NO_OI (no open interest on either side within 60 days), or the first failing step (NO_SPOT, NO_CHAIN, NO_EXPIRY: nothing listed 1 to 60 days out).

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| both walls known | `eq "OK"` | soft | 0 | the near miss is PARTIAL |

**When the reading lies**

- PARTIAL is common on a name at a 52-week extreme: at a high there is little call interest above spot, at a low little put interest below.

Sources: docs/data/swing.md (oi_walls@v1)

### `rollup.oi_walls@v1.call_wall`

**How to read it.** The strike at or above spot with the most call open interest, summed over expiries 1 to 60 days out: where upside positioning is concentrated. Dealer hedging around a heavy call strike tends to lean against the price as it approaches, so the call wall reads as soft resistance and a common pinning level into expiry. Compare it with the close: a wall 3% above is near, 15% above is out of play.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| has a call wall | `not_null` | hard | - | read it against the close and feature.dist_to_resistance |

**When the reading lies**

- Open interest does not say who holds it: a strike where customers face customers produces no hedging flow. Treat the wall as an estimate of a level, not a barrier (docs/data/positioning.md).
- Round strikes attract interest regardless of positioning; a wall exactly at a round number is partly a ladder artefact.
- After a large move the wall can sit well above or below spot until positions roll; rollup.oi_walls@v1.call_wall_oi says how much interest backs it.

Sources: SpotGamma, call wall and put wall explained: https://spotgamma.com/call-wall-put-wall-explained/; docs/data/positioning.md

### `rollup.oi_walls@v1.call_wall_oi`

**How to read it.** Call open interest at the call wall, summed over expiries 1 to 60 days out: how much positioning backs the level. A few thousand contracts is a modest wall on a mid-cap; tens of thousands is a level the market watches; against the chain total (feature.option_chain_oi) it says how concentrated the upside positioning is.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a wall with weight | `gte 5000` | soft | 0.5 x the threshold | - |

**When the reading lies**

- A large number with a chain total not much larger is one expiry's interest: the wall vanishes at that expiry.

Sources: https://spotgamma.com/call-wall-put-wall-explained/

### `rollup.oi_walls@v1.put_wall`

**How to read it.** The strike at or below spot with the most put open interest, summed over expiries 1 to 60 days out: where downside protection is concentrated. Hedging around a heavy put strike tends to cushion the price as it approaches, so the put wall reads as soft support. Compare it with the close and with swing support (rollup.swing_levels@v1.swing_low); a short put struck below the wall has it as a cushion.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| has a put wall | `not_null` | hard | - | read it against the close and feature.dist_to_support |

**When the reading lies**

- An estimate of a level, not a barrier: nothing here says whose gamma it is (docs/data/positioning.md).
- Round strikes and monthlies attract interest by themselves; a wall at a round number is partly a ladder artefact.

Sources: https://spotgamma.com/call-wall-put-wall-explained/; docs/data/positioning.md

### `rollup.oi_walls@v1.put_wall_oi`

**How to read it.** Put open interest at the put wall, summed over expiries 1 to 60 days out: how much protection sits at the level. Tens of thousands is a watched level on a large cap; against feature.option_chain_oi it says how concentrated the downside positioning is.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a wall with weight | `gte 5000` | soft | 0.5 x the threshold | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Concentrated in one expiry, it disappears at that expiry; a rolled hedge moves the wall overnight.

Sources: https://spotgamma.com/call-wall-put-wall-explained/

### `rollup.nearest_expiry@v1.expiry_date`

**How to read it.** The nearest listed option expiry on or after the session, from the session's own stored chain (an expiry on the session counts: a 0-DTE trades until the close). The Ideas page's expiry column; a date to show, with rollup.nearest_expiry@v1.dte for the days.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| has a listed expiry | `not_null` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Null when every stored expiry is before the session (a stale chain): the session's chain run says why (rollup.option_liquidity@v1.liq_status).
- The nearest expiry is rarely the one to trade: the short-strike groups use expiries near 35 and 45 days.

Sources: Site convention (features/rollups/options/nearest_expiry.py)

### `rollup.nearest_expiry@v1.dte`

**How to read it.** Calendar days to the nearest listed expiry: 0 expires today, 1 to 7 is a weekly, up to about 30 a monthly-only name. For an event play the front expiry is the one that prices the event; for everything else the number says how finely the name's expiries are spaced.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a weekly expiry available | `lte 7` | soft | 3 | - |
| no expiry inside a week (avoid 0-DTE pinning days) | `gt 7` | soft | 3 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- A front expiry with earnings before it (feature.earnings_before_expiry) carries the event; a short option there is an earnings trade whether or not it was meant as one.

Sources: Site convention

### `rollup.nearest_expiry@v1.sessions_to_expiry`

**How to read it.** Exchange sessions after the session up to the nearest expiry (0: expires today), the same count as rollup.earnings@v1.days_to_earnings uses, so the two compare directly: earnings sessions below expiry sessions means the report lands before the front expiry (feature.earnings_before_expiry).

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a front expiry at least a week out | `gte 5` | soft | 2 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Sessions, not calendar days: a holiday week makes 5 calendar days 3 sessions.

Sources: Site convention
