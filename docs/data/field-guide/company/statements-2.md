### `feature.revenue_cagr_3y`

**How to read it.** Compound annual growth of revenue over three years: the TTM against the TTM about three years earlier, as a yearly rate. 0.10 is 10% a year, 0.20 and above is a fast grower, negative is a shrinking business.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| steady growth over three years | `gte 0.1` | soft | 0.03 | with feature.revenue_growth_yoy gt 0 so the old growth has not stopped |

**When the reading lies**

- Null when the base 3 years ago is zero or negative, or the current one is, and for a company with under three years of filed history.
- Acquisitions inflate reported growth and divestitures deflate it; the figure is the reported one, not organic. A jump after a deal is not demand.
- The two ends are TTMs, so a seasonal company is compared like with like, but a cycle peak or trough end point can mislead. Check feature.revenue_growth_yoy for the recent pace.
- Null when the statement it reads is STALE: its newest period ended over 480 days before the session. Old figures are shown in the rollup but never used in a ratio.

Sources: SEC EDGAR company facts, the statement lines of instruments/statements (docs/adr/0067-sec-financial-statements-table.md); CFA Institute curriculum, compound annual growth rate as the standard multi-year growth measure

### `feature.cash_op_profitability`

**How to read it.** Cash operating profit on each dollar of assets: operating income less the accruals in earnings (net income minus operating cash flow), over total assets, 0.15 is 15 cents a year. Ball, Gerakos, Linnainmaa and Nikolaev find it subsumes the profitability and accruals premiums. Above 0.15 is a strongly cash-profitable business, 0.05 to 0.15 is ordinary, and below 0 is a business that loses cash at the operating level.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a cash-profitable business | `gte 0.1` | soft | 0.3 x the threshold | - |
| a positive cash operating profit | `gt 0` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Built from what is stored. The paper adds up line by line accrual changes (receivables, inventory, payables and others) and adds R&D back. This uses the whole gap between net income and operating cash flow. So it also adds back stock-based pay, impairments, deferred tax and non-operating cash items, and it keeps R&D in operating income. Total assets are the latest balance sheet, not tied to the TTM date.
- Null for banks, brokers and insurers (EBIT is null by design) and for a stale statement. Null too when the income statement, net income and operating cash flow do not end on the same quarter. A missing value is a gap, not a pass.
- Profitable is not cheap: pair with feature.composite_value_yield. A name after a big one-off (an impairment in operating income, a legal settlement in cash flow) can swing a quarter's worth of the value.
- Statement figures enter from the session after SEC acceptance of the filing and can be four months old.

Sources: Ball, Gerakos, Linnainmaa and Nikolaev (2016), Journal of Financial Economics 121(1), Accruals, cash flows, and operating profitability in the cross section of stock returns
