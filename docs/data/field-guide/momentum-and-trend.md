### `rollup.momentum@v1.rsi_14`

**How to read it.** Wilder's RSI over 14 sessions, 0 to 100: the share of recent movement that was up. 50 is balance; 70 and above means the last two weeks were mostly gains (overbought), 30 and below mostly losses (oversold). In a steady uptrend RSI lives between 40 and 80 and rarely visits 30; in a downtrend between 20 and 60 (Cardwell's ranges), so 'overbought' in a bull range is strength, not a top.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| oversold bounce in an uptrend | `lt 30` | soft | 5 | with feature.trend_state eq UPTREND (or feature.pct_vs_sma_200 gt 0) as the gate; alone it finds falling knives |
| momentum strength (a score, never the only gate) | `gte 50` | score | 20 | ranks the stronger names; the gate is rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_20d gt 0 |
| not chasing an overbought name | `lte 70` | soft | 5 | a filter on entries, not a short signal; in a strong uptrend expect many names above 70 |

**When the reading lies**

- A pending takeover pins the price just under the offer: RSI prints 80+ on the announcement day and then stays high for weeks with no trend behind it. Check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move (one big day) with feature.atr_pct near 0 since; see the 'pending takeover' situation.
- In a strong trend RSI stays above 70 for weeks; 'overbought' alone is not a sell signal (Cardwell). Pair an overbought rule with feature.pct_vs_sma_20 or feature.dist_to_resistance before reading it as stretched.
- RSI is 100 when the 14-session run had no down close at all, which happens on thin, low-priced names that barely trade. Gate on rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d and rollup.price_stats@v2.close first.
- Oversold in a downtrend keeps falling (a falling knife): an RSI below 30 is a bounce candidate only with feature.trend_state UPTREND or feature.pct_vs_sma_200 above 0.
- Null after a gap in the last 15 sessions or a shorter history; a hard criterion then rejects the name.

Sources: Wilder (1978), New Concepts in Technical Trading Systems: the 70 / 30 convention; Cardwell's RSI range rules (bull 40-80, bear 20-60): https://nexusfi.com/a/concepts/cardwell-rsi-framework; Relative strength index, Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_strength_index

### `rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_20d`

**How to read it.** The return over the last 20 sessions (about a month) as a fraction: 0.05 is up 5%, -0.10 is down 10%. Above 0 the month was up; beyond about 0.10 or -0.10 the move was large for a typical large cap (ATR of 1.5% a day compounds to a monthly range of that order). One-month returns tend to reverse slightly rather than continue (the short-term reversal), so this is a trend confirmation, not the academic momentum signal: that one is the 12-month return skipping the last month, which rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_60d comes nearer to.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| upward momentum (the gate) | `gt 0` | hard | - | confirm with feature.pct_vs_sma_20 gt 0; rank by ret_20d descending |
| a strong month | `gte 0.1` | soft | 0.03 | check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move is not most of it |
| a weak month (mean-reversion candidates) | `lte -0.1` | soft | 0.03 | with feature.trend_state eq UPTREND to buy a dip rather than a decline |

**When the reading lies**

- One day can be the whole month: an earnings gap or a takeover jump of 15% leaves ret_20d at 0.15 with nineteen flat sessions around it. Check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move; when it is most of ret_20d the 'momentum' is one event.
- After a large up month the next month leans slightly the other way (Jegadeesh 1990): a high ret_20d is weak evidence of continuation. Rank by it, do not gate hard on a large value.
- A null means a missing bar in the window (a halt, a listing gap) and rejects a hard criterion.

Sources: Jegadeesh (1990), Evidence of predictable behavior of security returns: the one-month reversal; Jegadeesh and Titman (1993) and the 12-1 convention: https://alphaarchitect.com/skip-month-mystery/

### `rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_60d`

**How to read it.** The return over the last 60 sessions (about a quarter) as a fraction. Positive means the quarter was up. Of the stored returns this is the closest to the academic momentum formation window (6 to 12 months, skipping the latest month), where past winners kept winning on average; it is still shorter than that evidence, so read it as 'the quarter was up', not as a proven factor.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a longer run of momentum | `gt 0` | hard | - | use instead of ret_20d when the sentence says a longer trend; rank descending |
| a strong quarter | `gte 0.2` | soft | 0.05 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- A single event (earnings, a deal, an index inclusion) can be most of a quarter's return; check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move and rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date.
- Null after any missing bar in the last 61 sessions.

Sources: Jegadeesh and Titman (1993), Returns to buying winners and selling losers; Momentum formation windows and the skip month: https://alphaarchitect.com/skip-month-mystery/

### `rollup.momentum@v1.ret_5d`

**How to read it.** The return over the last 5 sessions (a week) as a fraction. Short windows mean-revert: a week that was sharply up or down is more likely to give some back than to continue, so this is a timing field (buy the dip in an uptrend, do not chase a spike), not a momentum field.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a dip to buy inside an uptrend | `lte -0.03` | soft | 0.01 | with feature.trend_state eq UPTREND; the dip is the entry, the trend is the reason |
| not chasing a spike | `lte 0.08` | soft | 0.02 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- A week dominated by one gap day is an event, not a drift; check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move.
- Thin names swing 5% in a week on noise; gate on rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d.

Sources: Jegadeesh (1990) and Lehmann (1990): one-week to one-month contrarian profits

### `feature.pct_from_high_52w`

**How to read it.** How far the close sits under its 52-week high, as a fraction at or below 0: -0.05 is 5% below the high, 0 is at it. Nearness to the 52-week high predicts returns better than past returns do (George and Hwang 2004: investors anchor on the high and under-react to news near it), and those profits do not reverse later. Within 10% of the high is the catalogue's own 'near' band (feature.near_52w HIGH).

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| near the 52-week high (the anchoring effect) | `gte -0.1` | soft | 0.05 | or feature.near_52w eq HIGH; rank by pct_from_high_52w descending (nearest first) |
| at a new high | `gte -0.02` | soft | 0.02 | - |
| well off the high (beaten down) | `lte -0.3` | soft | 0.05 | say why it is interesting with another field; a discount alone is not a reason |

**When the reading lies**

- A pending takeover puts the close at its high and keeps it there; the anchoring story does not apply. See the 'pending takeover' situation.
- Null for a name with fewer than 240 bars in the last 252 sessions (a listing under a year old, a long halt): a hard criterion rejects it. Check instrument.listed_on.
- A name 50% below its high is 'cheap' only in price; nothing here says why it fell. Pair a deep-discount rule with feature.trend_state or rollup.financials@v2.financials_status.

Sources: George and Hwang (2004), The 52-week high and momentum investing, Journal of Finance: https://alphaarchitect.com/the-secret-to-momentum-is-the-52-week-high/; Quantpedia, 52-weeks high effect in stocks: https://quantpedia.com/strategies/52-weeks-high-effect-in-stocks

### `feature.pct_from_low_52w`

**How to read it.** How far the close sits above its 52-week low, as a fraction at or above 0: 0.10 is 10% above the low, 0 is at it. Near the low is where 'beaten down' names are; nearness to the low has weaker evidence than nearness to the high and the names there are often there for a reason.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| near the 52-week low | `lte 0.1` | soft | 0.05 | or feature.near_52w eq LOW; 0.20 for 'off the lows'; add a turn signal (ret_20d gt 0) |

**When the reading lies**

- At the low with feature.trend_state DOWNTREND the trend is down; a bounce rule needs a turn (rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_20d gt 0, feature.pct_vs_sma_20 gt 0) or it buys the decline.
- A dividend cut, a failed deal or a going-concern notice puts a name at its low; check rollup.financials@v2.financials_status and instrument.financial_status.
- Null under 240 bars in the last 252 sessions (recent listings).

Sources: George and Hwang (2004): the low has less predictive power than the high

### `feature.near_52w`

**How to read it.** Where the close sits in its 52-week range: HIGH within 10% of the high, LOW within 10% of the low, BOTH when the whole range is under about 20% (a quiet name: a utility, a bond fund, a pinned takeover target), NONE in between.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| near the high | `eq "HIGH"` | hard | - | - |
| near the low | `eq "LOW"` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- BOTH is a narrow range, not 'near both extremes': it marks low-volatility names and pinned takeover targets. Exclude it from momentum screens.
- Null when the 52-week high or low is unknown (under 240 bars).

Sources: George and Hwang (2004) for HIGH; BOTH is a site convention

### `feature.pct_vs_sma_20`

**How to read it.** Close over its 20-session average minus 1: 0.03 is 3% above the one-month average, negative is below. Above 0 the short-term trend is up. Beyond about 0.08 to 0.10 above, a large cap is stretched for a month and tends to come back to the average; a pullback to it is the 'buy the dip' entry (feature.pullback_to_sma20 measures that in ATRs).

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| short-term trend up (confirmation) | `gt 0` | soft | 0.02 | the confirmation of rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_20d gt 0 |
| not stretched above the month's average | `lte 0.08` | soft | 0.02 | for entries; a breakout screen wants the opposite |

**When the reading lies**

- A gap day (earnings, a deal) jumps the close far above the average in one session: a 'stretched' reading right after a gap is the event, not an overextension. Check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move.
- Leveraged and inverse funds reset daily and drift from their index; moving-average readings describe the fund's path, not the market's. Exclude with instrument.is_leveraged eq false.
- Null after a gap in the last 20 sessions.

Sources: Brock, Lakonishok and LeBaron (1992), Simple technical trading rules: buy signals after a close above the average earned more than sell signals

### `feature.pct_vs_sma_50`

**How to read it.** Close over its 50-session average minus 1: the intermediate trend. Above 0 the name trades above its roughly quarterly average; the 50-day average was the best performing single rule in Brock, Lakonishok and LeBaron's long Dow test. More than about 0.15 above is stretched for a large cap.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| above the 50-day (a loose uptrend) | `gt 0` | soft | 0.02 | - |
| not stretched above the 50-day | `lte 0.15` | soft | 0.03 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Gaps and takeovers as for the 20-day average: one event, not a trend. Check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move.
- Leveraged and inverse funds: exclude with instrument.is_leveraged eq false.
- Null after a gap in the last 50 sessions or a shorter history.

Sources: Brock, Lakonishok and LeBaron (1992): https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v47y1992i5p1731-64.html

### `feature.pct_vs_sma_200`

**How to read it.** Close over its 200-session average minus 1: the long-term trend, the daily version of Faber's 10-month moving average rule. Above 0 is the classic bull-market condition: returns after a close above the long average were higher and calmer than after a close below it. More than about 0.25 above the 200-day is far from its long average and historically gave some back; more than 0.25 below is a name in a long decline.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| long-term uptrend (the bull-market filter) | `gt 0` | hard | - | or feature.trend_state eq UPTREND for the stricter close > SMA50 > SMA200 |
| not far above the long average | `lte 0.25` | soft | 0.05 | - |
| in a long decline (contrarian or avoid) | `lte -0.25` | soft | 0.05 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- The rule's edge was found on indices and diversified portfolios; on a single stock a close above the 200-day is a trend filter, not a return forecast.
- Null for names with under 200 sessions of history (listings under ten months old) or a gap in the window; a hard criterion rejects them. Check instrument.listed_on.
- Leveraged and inverse funds: exclude with instrument.is_leveraged eq false and instrument.is_inverse eq false.

Sources: Faber (2007), A quantitative approach to tactical asset allocation (the 10-month SMA): https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=1115; Brock, Lakonishok and LeBaron (1992)

### `feature.trend_state`

**How to read it.** UPTREND when close > SMA50 > SMA200 (price above a rising structure), DOWNTREND when close < SMA50 < SMA200, MIXED otherwise. MIXED is a large bucket: a pullback below the 50-day inside an uptrend, a bounce inside a downtrend, and every crossover in progress.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| in an uptrend | `eq "UPTREND"` | hard | - | do not combine with 'near the 52-week low'; those rarely coexist |
| in a downtrend | `eq "DOWNTREND"` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- A name can be UPTREND the day after a 30% takeover jump and stay so for months without moving again; see the 'pending takeover' situation (feature.atr_pct near 0).
- Null under 200 sessions of history; a hard UPTREND criterion silently drops every recent listing. Check instrument.listed_on when that matters.
- Leveraged and inverse funds: exclude with instrument.is_leveraged eq false.

Sources: Faber (2007) and Brock, Lakonishok and LeBaron (1992) for the moving-average evidence; the alignment rule is a practitioner convention

### `feature.pullback_to_sma20`

**How to read it.** True when the name is in an uptrend (feature.trend_state UPTREND) and the close is within one ATR of its 20-session average, above or below it: the dip-in-an-uptrend entry. The ATR scales 'near' to the name's own volatility, so a 1% move on a utility and a 4% move on a chip maker both count.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| buy the dip in an uptrend | `eq true` | hard | - | with feature.atr_pct gte 0.01 and rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d as gates |

**When the reading lies**

- A pinned takeover target converges onto its average with a tiny ATR, so 'within one ATR' becomes trivially true in an UPTREND: a false pullback. Require feature.atr_pct gte 0.01.
- True on the day of a sharp drop through the average as much as on a gentle dip; add rollup.momentum@v1.ret_5d gte -0.06 to keep out breakdowns.
- Null when the trend, the average or the ATR is unknown.

Sources: Practitioner convention (pullback-to-moving-average entries); the Turtle rules' ATR-scaled distances

### `feature.breakout_20d`

**How to read it.** True when the close is above the highest high of the previous 20 sessions and the day's volume is at least 1.5 times its 20-session average: a Donchian-style breakout with volume confirmation (1.5 to 2 times average is the usual bar, above 2 is strong participation). Breakouts fail often in ranging markets (Raschke and Connors built the 'Turtle Soup' strategy on fading them), so volume and a trend filter matter.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| breaking out on volume | `eq true` | hard | - | with feature.trend_state eq UPTREND or feature.pct_vs_sma_200 gt 0 as the trend filter |

**When the reading lies**

- The announcement day of a takeover is a textbook breakout on volume, and the last move the name will make. Check feature.atr_pct in the sessions after; see the 'pending takeover' situation.
- An earnings gap is a breakout on volume too; whether it continues is the post-earnings drift question, not a technical one. Check rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date.
- Index events (quarterly expiry Fridays, the Russell reconstitution in late June, S&P changes) put huge volume on a flat close; the price condition still has to hold, but treat a breakout on such a day with suspicion.
- Null when the prior high or the volume ratio is unknown (a gap in the last 21 sessions).

Sources: Volume confirmation of breakouts (1.5x to 2x the 20-day average): https://www.luxalgo.com/blog/how-volume-confirms-breakouts-in-trading/; Turtle rules (20-day Donchian entries) and their failure rate; Raschke and Connors, Street Smarts (Turtle Soup)

### `rollup.momentum@v1.rel_volume`

**How to read it.** The session's volume over the average of the 20 sessions before it: 1 is normal, 2 is twice normal, 0.5 is a quiet day. Above 1.5 is the usual confirmation that a move has participation behind it; above 2 is strong; above 5 is an event. Below 0.7 on an up day is a move nobody joined.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| heavy volume today | `gte 1.5` | soft | 0.3 | - |
| an unusual volume event | `gte 3` | soft | 0.5 | check rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date; most 3x days are earnings or index events |

**When the reading lies**

- Quarterly expiry Fridays, the Russell reconstitution (late June) and index inclusions multiply volume without any news; so do secondary offerings and block trades. High volume with a flat close (rollup.momentum@v1.ret_5d near 0) is usually one of these.
- Earnings days are 3 to 10 times normal volume by nature; check rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date before reading a spike as unusual.
- On thin names a single institutional order is a 5x day; gate on rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d.
- The base is the 20 sessions before today; after a very heavy week the base is inflated and today's ratio understates activity.

Sources: Volume confirmation thresholds: https://www.luxalgo.com/blog/how-volume-confirms-breakouts-in-trading/

### `rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move`

**How to read it.** The largest single-day close-to-close move, up or down, in the last 20 sessions, as a fraction: 0.12 is a 12% day. Below about 0.04 the month had no shock; 0.08 and above is a news day for a large cap (earnings, guidance, a deal, an FDA decision). After earnings a large move tends to continue for days to weeks (the post-earnings announcement drift); a large move with no earnings behind it more often partly reverses.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no shock in the last month (a calm name) | `lte 0.05` | soft | 0.02 | a filter for premium-selling and pullback screens |
| had a big one-day move | `gte 0.08` | soft | 0.02 | check rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date to know whether it was earnings |

**When the reading lies**

- It says a move happened, not why or which way: pair with rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date (an earnings move) and the sign of rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_20d.
- A move of 0.20 or more followed by a dead-flat tape is the takeover signature; see the 'pending takeover' situation.
- Low-priced and thin names post 10% days on noise; gate on rollup.price_stats@v2.close and rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d.
- The window is 20 sessions: a shock 21 sessions ago has dropped out of this field but still sits inside rollup.price_stats@v2.hv30.

Sources: Ball and Brown (1968); Bernard and Thomas (1989): the post-earnings announcement drift; Short-term reversal after non-news moves: Jegadeesh (1990)

### `feature.dist_to_resistance`

**How to read it.** How far the nearest confirmed swing high above the close is, as a fraction of price: 0.05 is 5% of room before the last place sellers showed up. Under about 0.02 the name is at resistance; above about 0.10 there is room to run. A swing high is confirmed five sessions after it forms, so the level lags.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| room to run | `gte 0.1` | soft | 0.03 | soft, so a name at a new high (null) is listed rather than rejected; or use pct_from_high_52w |
| near resistance (a sell-call or fade level) | `lte 0.03` | soft | 0.01 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Null at a one-year high (no swing high above the close): a hard 'room to run' criterion rejects exactly the names breaking out. Use feature.pct_from_high_52w gte -0.02 for those.
- The nearest swing high can be a minor one; compare feature.dist_to_resistance_atr: under 1 ATR away the level is in today's noise.
- A level from eleven months ago has less meaning than one from last month; rollup.swing_levels@v1.swing_high_date says when it formed.

Sources: Practitioner convention (swing-point support and resistance)

### `feature.dist_to_support`

**How to read it.** How far the nearest confirmed swing low below the close is, as a fraction of price: 0.03 is 3% above the last place buyers showed up. Under about 0.03 the name is at support (a stop goes just below it); above about 0.15 a stop at support is a wide one.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| near support | `lte 0.03` | soft | 0.01 | with feature.trend_state eq UPTREND; support in a downtrend breaks |

**When the reading lies**

- Null at a one-year low (no swing low below): a 'near support' criterion then rejects the names that just broke support, which may be what you want.
- A level more than a few ATRs away is not 'support' for a swing trade; feature.dist_to_support_atr under 2 is the usual working range.

Sources: Practitioner convention; ATR-scaled stops (Turtle rules)

### `feature.dist_52w`

**How to read it.** The distance to the nearer 52-week extreme as a fraction of it: 0 at the high or the low, 0.10 when the close is 10% from whichever is closer. It is the one-number form of 'near a 52-week extreme' (the VRP scanner's gate); feature.near_52w says which side (HIGH, LOW, BOTH).

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| within 10% of a 52-week extreme (the VRP gate) | `lte 0.1` | hard | - | read feature.near_52w for the side |
| near an extreme, listing near misses | `lte 0.1` | soft | 0.05 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- It does not say which extreme: a name 5% under its high and one 5% above its low both read 0.05, and they are opposite setups. Read feature.near_52w, or feature.pct_from_high_52w and feature.pct_from_low_52w, before acting on it.
- A pinned takeover target sits at its high with a distance near 0 for months (the 'pending takeover' situation).
- Null under 240 bars in the last 252 sessions (recent listings).

Sources: George and Hwang (2004) for the high; the VRP scanner's spec (docs/screeners/vrp-scanner.md) for the 10% gate

### `feature.bb_pct_b`

**How to read it.** %B: where the close sits between the Bollinger bands, 0 at the lower band, 0.5 at the 20-session mean, 1 at the upper band, below 0 or above 1 outside them. Low to high: under 0 is a two-sigma dip against the last month (the mean-reversion long in an uptrend), 0.2 to 0.8 is inside the normal range, above 1 the close has broken out of the band (strength in a trend, exhaustion after a long run). Bollinger's own rules: buy strength when %B exceeds 1 with volume, buy weakness when it is below 0 with a trend behind it.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a dip to the lower band in an uptrend | `lte 0.0` | soft | 0.1 | with feature.trend_state eq UPTREND as the gate; alone it finds downtrends |
| breaking out above the upper band | `gte 1.0` | soft | 0.05 | with rollup.momentum@v1.rel_volume gte 1.5 for participation; a score, not a gate, in a trend |

**When the reading lies**

- In a strong trend %B sits above 0.8 for weeks (the band walk, rollup.bands@v2.band_walk): a high %B is not overbought in itself. Pair with rollup.momentum@v1.rsi_14 or feature.stretch_sma20_atr before reading it as stretched.
- Below 0 in a downtrend is where the price lives, not a dip: gate a mean-reversion long on feature.trend_state eq UPTREND or feature.pct_vs_sma_200 gt 0.
- The bands widen for 20 sessions after a shock day, so %B reads 'inside' while the price is far from where it was. Check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move.
- Null when the bands are unknown (a gap among the last 20 sessions) or coincide (20 equal closes).

Sources: Bollinger (2001), Bollinger on Bollinger Bands: %b; Bollinger Bands, Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bollinger_Bands

### `feature.kc_position`

**How to read it.** Where the close sits in the Keltner channel: 0 at the lower line (EMA minus 2 ATR), 0.5 at the 20-session EMA, 1 at the upper line, outside 0..1 beyond them. Because the channel is built from the daily range, 1 means 'two average days above the EMA': a close above it is a strong move for this name whatever its price. Between 0.3 and 0.7 the name is near its short-term mean.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pulled back to the channel midline in an uptrend | `between [0.35, 0.65]` | soft | 0.1 | with feature.trend_state eq UPTREND; feature.pullback_to_sma20 is the same idea against the SMA |

**When the reading lies**

- A collapsed ATR (a pending takeover, a halted name) makes the channel a hair wide and every close 'outside'. Check feature.atr_pct; see the 'pending takeover' situation.
- The channel lags a gap by two weeks (the ATR catches up), so the day after a gap the position is far outside and means 'gapped', not 'trending'. Check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move.
- Null with fewer than 20 consecutive bars ending on the session, or an ATR of 0.

Sources: Keltner channel, Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keltner_channel

### `feature.price_z_20d`

**How to read it.** The price z-score: how many standard deviations of the last 20 closes the close sits from their mean. 0 is at the mean, +2 on the upper Bollinger band, -2 on the lower; beyond 2.5 either way is rare (a few sessions a year on a mean-reverting name) and beyond 3 is almost always a one-day shock. It is %B on a different scale (%B 0 is -2, %B 1 is +2); use it when a rule wants sigmas, and feature.stretch_sma20_atr when it wants average daily ranges.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a mean-reversion long | `lte -2.0` | soft | 0.5 | with feature.trend_state eq UPTREND; feature.bb_pct_b lte 0 is the same criterion |
| stretched above the mean | `gte 2.0` | soft | 0.5 | a filter against chasing; in a strong trend expect many names here |

**When the reading lies**

- A clean trend inflates the deviation (the closes spread out along the move), so a trending name shows a moderate z-score while sitting far from its mean in ATRs. Check feature.stretch_sma20_atr as well.
- A high z-score in an uptrend is strength more often than a top: pair with rollup.momentum@v1.rsi_14 and feature.pct_vs_sma_50 before fading it.
- Null when sma_20 or close_std_20 is unknown (a gap among the last 20 sessions) or the closes never moved.

Sources: Standard score, Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_score; Bollinger (2001), Bollinger on Bollinger Bands: bands as +-2 standard deviations

### `feature.stretch_sma20_atr`

**How to read it.** How far the close sits from its 20-session mean, measured in Wilder ATR(14): +1 is one average day's range above it, -1 below. Low to high: within -1 to +1 is at the mean (feature.pullback_to_sma20 is this band in an uptrend), 2 to 3 is extended, beyond 4 is a climax or a gap, the zone where a long is late and a pullback likely. The ATR scale makes it comparable across names: 3 ATR is 3 ATR on a utility and on a biotech.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| not overextended (entry filter) | `lte 2.0` | soft | 0.5 | with feature.trend_state eq UPTREND; feature.pullback_to_sma20 is the within-1-ATR version |
| a climax run (fade or trim) | `gte 4.0` | soft | 1.0 | a score; confirm with rollup.momentum@v1.rsi_14 gte 75 and feature.volume_climax |

**When the reading lies**

- A gap on news puts the close many ATRs from the mean in one day; the ATR then grows and the stretch shrinks without the price moving. Check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move and rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date.
- A pending takeover shrinks the ATR towards zero, so the stretch explodes on a flat price. Check feature.atr_pct; see the 'pending takeover' situation.
- In a strong uptrend 2 to 3 ATR above the mean persists for weeks; fade it only with rollup.momentum@v1.rsi_14 above 75 or feature.bb_pct_b above 1.
- Null when sma_20 or atr_14 is unknown (a gap among the last 20 sessions, fewer than 15 consecutive bars) or the ATR is 0.

Sources: Wilder (1978), New Concepts in Technical Trading Systems: ATR; Raschke and Connors (1995), Street Smarts: 'stretch' entries measured in ATR

### `feature.stretch_sma50_atr`

**How to read it.** How far the close sits from its 50-session mean, in Wilder ATR(14): the intermediate-term version of feature.stretch_sma20_atr. Within -2 to +2 is near the mean (where a trend-following entry on the 50-day lives), 4 to 6 is a strong run, beyond 8 an extended one that has usually gone a quarter without a rest.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pulled back to the 50-day in an uptrend | `between [-1.5, 1.5]` | soft | 0.5 | with feature.trend_state eq UPTREND (close above SMA50 above SMA200) |

**When the reading lies**

- A gap moves the stretch many ATRs in a day; the ATR then grows and the stretch shrinks without the price moving. Check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move.
- A pending takeover shrinks the ATR, so the stretch explodes on a flat price; see the 'pending takeover' situation.
- Null when sma_50 or atr_14 is unknown (a gap among the last 50 sessions, fewer than 15 consecutive bars) or the ATR is 0.

Sources: Wilder (1978), New Concepts in Technical Trading Systems: ATR

### `feature.donchian_pos_20d`

**How to read it.** Where the close sits in the 20-session high-low channel, 0 at the channel low, 1 at the channel high. 1 means the close is at a 20-session high (today's high included), the Turtle entry; 0 at a 20-session low. Between 0.4 and 0.6 the name is mid-range. Unlike %B it uses highs and lows and has no statistical scale, so it reads the same on every name.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| at a 20-session high (breakout) | `gte 0.95` | soft | 0.05 | with rollup.momentum@v1.rel_volume gte 1.5; feature.breakout_20d is the volume-confirmed flag |
| at a 20-session low | `lte 0.05` | soft | 0.05 | with feature.trend_state eq UPTREND for a dip; alone it lists breakdowns |

**When the reading lies**

- A single spike high 19 sessions ago keeps the channel top far above the price for one more day, so the position reads low on a name that is quietly rising; feature.range_20d_pct says how wide the channel is.
- A close at the channel high in a downtrend is a rally to resistance: gate a breakout on feature.trend_state or feature.pct_vs_sma_200, and on volume (feature.breakout_20d).
- Null when high_20d or low_20d is unknown (a gap among the last 20 sessions) or the channel has no width.

Sources: Donchian channels and the Turtle rules: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Donchian; Faith (2007), Way of the Turtle: the 20-day breakout entry

### `rollup.bands@v2.band_walk`

**How to read it.** How many consecutive sessions the close has sat outside the Bollinger bands: positive counts closes above the upper band, negative below the lower, 0 inside. A walk of 3 or more up the band is the signature of a strong trend (Bollinger: 'a tag of the upper band is not a sell signal'); a walk down the lower band is a persistent sell-off. Most walks end within a week; 10 and above is a run that usually ends in a climax.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| walking the upper band (strong trend) | `gte 3` | soft | 1 | with rollup.momentum@v1.rel_volume for participation; a score, the gate is feature.trend_state eq UPTREND |
| a persistent sell-off | `lte -3` | soft | 1 | a capitulation candidate with feature.volume_climax; not a long entry on its own |

**When the reading lies**

- A walk starts with a shock day as often as with a trend: a one-day earnings gap sits outside the band for a session or two while the bands catch up. Check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move and rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date.
- A thin name with equal closes has bands of zero width, so any tick is outside them. Gate on rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d.
- Null when today's bands are unknown (a gap among the last 20 sessions); the count stops at the first session without bands.

Sources: Bollinger (2001), Bollinger on Bollinger Bands: walking the bands

### `rollup.trend_stats@v2.ret_120d`

**How to read it.** The close over the close 120 sessions (about six months) earlier, minus one: 0.25 is up 25%. The six-month return is the classic intermediate momentum horizon (Jegadeesh and Titman's 6-month formation); above about 0.20 a name is a momentum leader in an ordinary year, below -0.20 a laggard. Compare with the market's six-month return (market.market_trend@v2.spx_ret_252d is the year) before reading it as stock-specific.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| six-month momentum | `gte 0.15` | soft | 0.05 | gate ret_120d gt 0; score the size; pair with rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_20d to avoid names already reversing |

**When the reading lies**

- A single gap (an earnings surprise, a takeover) can be the whole return: check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move and rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date before reading it as a trend.
- Six months ago may sit just before or after a crash: the same stock reads +40% or -10% depending on the day. Read with rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_60d and rollup.trend_stats@v2.ret_252d.
- Null after a gap in the last 121 sessions or a shorter history (a listing under six months old).

Sources: Jegadeesh and Titman (1993), Returns to Buying Winners and Selling Losers; Momentum (finance), Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Momentum_(finance)

### `rollup.trend_stats@v2.ret_252d`

**How to read it.** The close over the close 252 sessions (one year) earlier, minus one: the plain one-year return, 0.30 is up 30%. In an average year the S&P 500 returns about 10% with a spread of about 15 points across its members, so above 0.30 is a leader and below -0.20 a laggard. For the momentum factor as the literature defines it, which skips the last month, use rollup.trend_stats@v2.mom_12_1.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| one-year winner | `gte 0.3` | soft | 0.1 | a score; relative strength against the market is rollup.trend_stats@v2.ret_252d minus market.market_trend@v2.spx_ret_252d |

**When the reading lies**

- The last month's reversal is inside this number: a stock up 50% on the year and down 20% this month reads +20%. rollup.trend_stats@v2.mom_12_1 leaves the month out.
- The year-ago base day moves daily: a crash or a rally one year ago rolling out of the window changes the return with no move today. Check rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_60d for the recent trend.
- Null after a gap in the last 253 sessions or a shorter history (a listing under a year old; rollup.price_history@v1.range_status says SINCE_LISTING).

Sources: Momentum (finance), Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Momentum_(finance)

### `rollup.trend_stats@v2.mom_12_1`

**How to read it.** The 12-1 momentum: the return from 12 months ago to one month ago (the close 21 sessions back over the close 252 sessions back, minus one), the definition the momentum factor literature uses because the most recent month tends to reverse. 0.20 means the name was up 20% over the eleven months ending a month ago. Positive is the gate; the top decile of the universe (about 0.40 and above in a normal year) is where the factor's return has come from, and the bottom decile is where it has been short.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| momentum factor long | `gte 0.2` | soft | 0.1 | gate mom_12_1 gt 0; score the size; add rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_20d gt -0.10 to avoid the names already breaking |

**When the reading lies**

- It ignores the last month on purpose, so a name that collapsed in the last three weeks still reads as a winner; pair with rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_20d or feature.trend_state to catch the turn.
- Momentum crashes: after a bear market the losers rally hardest (2009, 2020), and a pure 12-1 screen is on the wrong side for months. Check market.market_trend@v2.spx_drawdown_252d.
- Null after a gap in the last 253 sessions or a shorter history.

Sources: Jegadeesh and Titman (1993), Returns to Buying Winners and Selling Losers; Carhart (1997), On Persistence in Mutual Fund Performance: the 12-1 momentum factor; Daniel and Moskowitz (2016), Momentum Crashes

### `rollup.trend_stats@v2.close_streak`

**How to read it.** How many consecutive sessions the close has risen (positive) or fallen (negative), ending today; 0 when today's close is unchanged. Streaks of 3 to 5 are common (a coin flip gives five in a row one time in sixteen); 7 and above is rare and, in the short-term mean-reversion literature, a reversal setup rather than a trend: Connors' rules buy after several down closes in an uptrend and sell after several up closes.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a pullback of several down closes in an uptrend | `lte -3` | soft | 1 | with feature.trend_state eq UPTREND as the gate; the short-term mean-reversion entry |
| a long run of up closes (not chasing) | `lte 5` | soft | 1 | a filter against buying the sixth up day; use with feature.stretch_sma20_atr |

**When the reading lies**

- A streak says nothing about size: five up closes of 0.1% each is noise. Read it with rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_20d or feature.stretch_sma20_atr for the distance covered.
- A takeover pins the price, so the streak alternates around zero for months; a halted name has no streak. See the 'pending takeover' situation.
- Null when the session before has no bar (a gap); the count stops at the first gap.

Sources: Connors and Alvarez (2009), Short Term Trading Strategies That Work: consecutive-close setups

### `rollup.trend_stats@v2.sma20_streak`

**How to read it.** How many consecutive sessions the close has held above (positive) or below (negative) its 20-session mean, ending today. 1 to 3 is a fresh cross, often a whipsaw; 10 and above is a trend that has held for two weeks; 40 and above is a run that has not visited its mean for two months. A negative count of the same size is the mirror in a downtrend. The 20-session mean is the short-term trend line most pullback strategies use, so the streak dates the current leg.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| an established short-term uptrend | `gte 10` | soft | 3 | with feature.pct_vs_sma_20 gt 0.02 so the trend still has distance; the mirror lte -10 for a downtrend |
| a fresh cross above the 20-day | `between [1, 3]` | soft | 1 | confirm with rollup.momentum@v1.rel_volume gte 1.3 on the cross; alone it is a whipsaw list |

**When the reading lies**

- A fresh cross (a streak of 1 or 2) reverses as often as not; a gate on the crossing alone is noise. Require 3 or more, or pair with rollup.momentum@v1.rel_volume on the cross day.
- A long streak above the mean with a shrinking distance (feature.pct_vs_sma_20 near 0) is a trend losing speed, not strength; read both.
- Null when today's 20-session mean is unknown (a gap among the last 20 sessions); the count stops at the first session without a mean.

Sources: Moving average crossovers, Investopedia: https://www.investopedia.com/terms/c/crossover.asp

### `rollup.bands@v2.ema20_slope_5d`

**How to read it.** How much the 20-session EMA moved over the last 5 sessions, as a fraction: 0.02 means the fast average is 2% higher than a week ago (rising), -0.02 falling. Near 0 (within about 0.005) the short-term trend is flat. A rising 20 EMA with the close above it is the simplest statement of a short-term uptrend; the slope turning negative while the close is still above the average is the early deterioration signal.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a rising short-term trend | `gt 0.0` | hard | - | with rollup.price_stats@v2.close above rollup.bands@v2.ema_20 (feature.pct_vs_ema_20 gt 0) |

**When the reading lies**

- The average lags: after a gap the slope reads the gap for a week. Check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move.
- Scale it by the name's volatility: 0.02 a week is a crawl on a high-beta name and a sprint on a utility. Compare with feature.atr_pct, or use feature.stretch_sma20_atr for the position in ATRs.

Sources: Moving average slope as a trend filter (Kaufman, Trading Systems and Methods)

Sources: Moving average slope as a trend filter (Kaufman, Trading Systems and Methods)

### `rollup.trend_stats@v2.mom_accel_5d`

**How to read it.** Momentum acceleration: this week's 5-session return minus last week's, in return points. +0.03 means this week ran 3 points ahead of last week (accelerating); -0.03 it slowed by 3 points (deteriorating). | Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |

Sources: Faber (2007), A Quantitative Approach to Tactical Asset Allocation

### `feature.ema_stack`

**How to read it.** The EMA alignment: BULL when EMA20 > EMA50 > EMA200 (the averages stacked in order, the shape of an established uptrend), BEAR when EMA20 < EMA50 < EMA200, else MIXED (a crossing in progress or a range). feature.trend_state is the same idea on the close and the SMAs (close > SMA50 > SMA200), so BULL here with UPTREND there is full alignment.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| an aligned uptrend | `eq "BULL"` | hard | - | the gate of the pullback and continuation screens; add feature.trend_state eq UPTREND for the close as well |
| an aligned downtrend (short setups) | `eq "BEAR"` | hard | - | with feature.breakdown_20d for the trigger |

**When the reading lies**

- Alignment lags: a stack stays BULL for weeks after a top. Pair with rollup.bands@v2.ema20_slope_5d or feature.pct_vs_ema_20 for the turn.
- Null when an EMA is unknown (fewer than 200 consecutive bars).

Sources: Moving average ribbons and alignment (Guppy, Trend Trading)

### `rollup.retest@v1.retest_state`

**How to read it.** What the latest 20-session breakout of the last 60 sessions did since. RETESTING: today's low came back to within half an ATR above the breakout level and the close held it (the entry traders wait for). HELD: an earlier session did that and no close since went below the level. FRESH: price has not come back to the level. FAILED: some close since the breakout is below its level. NONE: no breakout in the window. NO_ATR: the ATR is unknown, so the retest tolerance is too.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a breakout that came back and held | `in ["RETESTING", "HELD"]` | hard | - | feature.breakout_retest_held is the HELD half |
| buying the retest today | `eq "RETESTING"` | hard | - | with feature.trend_state eq UPTREND |
| a breakout that has not failed | `ne "FAILED"` | soft | 0 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- FAILED includes a single close below the level on any day since the breakout, even if price recovered the next day; rollup.retest@v1.failed_breakouts_252d is how often the name's breakouts failed within 20 sessions.
- RETESTING is today's bar only: a low in the band with a weak close at or above the level is still RETESTING, so check the close against feature.dist_to_support_atr or the session's range.
- The breakout is the close above the prior 20-session high, with no volume condition; a thin-volume breakout fails more often (rollup.momentum@v1.rel_volume).
- NONE and NO_ATR are not failures: a hard 'eq HELD' rejects them, which is usually intended.

Sources: docs/data/swing.md; Bulkowski, Encyclopedia of Chart Patterns (pullbacks and throwbacks to a breakout); Edwards and Magee, Technical Analysis of Stock Trends

### `feature.pct_vs_sma_150`

**How to read it.** Close over the 150-session (30-week) average minus one: 0.08 is 8% above it. Above 0 is the first line of Minervini's trend template (close above the 150 and 200-day averages, the 150 above the 200, the 50 above both) and Weinstein's stage-2 condition; well above it (0.25 and more) the name is extended from its seven-month base.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| above the 30-week average (trend template) | `gt 0.0` | hard | - | with feature.pct_vs_sma_200 gt 0 and feature.ema_stack eq BULL for the rest of the template |

**When the reading lies**

- Null when sma_150 is unknown (a gap among the last 150 sessions, or a listing under seven months old).

Sources: Minervini (2013), Trade Like a Stock Market Wizard: the trend template

### `rollup.trend_stats@v2.trend_r2_90d`

**How to read it.** How well a straight line fits the log price over the last 90 sessions, 0 to 1: the R-squared of the regression of log close on time. Above 0.8 the trend has been smooth (few deep pullbacks: the kind a trend follower can hold); 0.4 to 0.8 a trend with noise; below 0.3 no trend worth the name, whatever the return. It says nothing about direction: read it with rollup.trend_stats@v2.reg_slope_90d_ann (the pace) or feature.clenow_momentum_90d (both at once).

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a smooth trend | `gte 0.7` | soft | 0.1 | with rollup.trend_stats@v2.reg_slope_90d_ann gt 0 for a smooth uptrend |

**When the reading lies**

- One gap (an earnings jump) in the window breaks the line: a name up 30% in a day and flat otherwise has a low R-squared and a high slope. Check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move.
- A smooth decline fits a line as well as a smooth rise; the sign is in the slope.
- Null after a gap in the last 90 sessions, a shorter history, or a close that never moved.

Sources: Clenow (2015), Stocks on the Move: the exponential regression slope x R-squared ranking

### `rollup.trend_stats@v2.reg_slope_90d_ann`

**How to read it.** The pace of the last 90 sessions' trend as a yearly rate: the slope of the regression line through the log close, annualised, so 0.40 means the trend, continued for a year, would return 40%. It is the return the trend implies, not the return made (a single jump adds little to the slope); Clenow ranks names by this times rollup.trend_stats@v2.trend_r2_90d (feature.clenow_momentum_90d). Above about 0.3 is a strong trend in an ordinary year, above 1.0 a parabolic one.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a strong trend pace | `gte 0.3` | soft | 0.1 | with rollup.trend_stats@v2.trend_r2_90d gte 0.6; the combined score is feature.clenow_momentum_90d |

**When the reading lies**

- A slope with a low R-squared is a line through noise: gate on rollup.trend_stats@v2.trend_r2_90d gte 0.5 before reading the pace.
- It describes the last 90 sessions and turns slowly; a name that broke down two weeks ago still shows a positive slope. Check rollup.trend_stats@v2.ret_10d.
- Null after a gap in the last 90 sessions or a shorter history.

Sources: Clenow (2015), Stocks on the Move

### `feature.clenow_momentum_90d`

**How to read it.** Clenow's momentum score: the annualised regression slope over 90 sessions times its R-squared, so a smooth 40%-a-year trend scores about 0.36 while a jerky one with the same slope scores half that. Rank the universe by it and buy the top decile (Clenow's rule, with the 100-day average and a 15% gap filter); above about 0.25 is a candidate in an ordinary year, negative is a downtrend.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| momentum ranking (smooth and fast) | `gte 0.25` | score | 0.1 | the gate is feature.pct_vs_sma_200 gt 0 (or sma_100 in Clenow's book) and market.market_trend@v2.spx_close_vs_sma200 gt 0 |

**When the reading lies**

- A one-day gap inside the window lowers the R-squared more than it raises the slope, so gap-driven names rank low; that is the intended filter, but check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move when a strong name is missing.
- Null when either input is unknown (a gap among the last 90 sessions, or a flat close).

Sources: Clenow (2015), Stocks on the Move: the ranking formula and the 90-day window

### `rollup.trend_stats@v2.high_100d`

**How to read it.** The highest daily high of the last 100 sessions (about five months), in dollars per share, today's high included. With the 20 and 50-session highs it dates the current advance: the close at all three is a fresh leg, at the 20 but not the 100 a rally inside a longer range. Compare with the close as a share: rollup.price_stats@v2.close / high_100d - 1.

**The criterion per intent**

**When the reading lies**

### `rollup.trend_stats@v2.prior_high_50d`

**How to read it.** The highest daily high of the 50 sessions before today (today excluded), in dollars per share: the level a 50-session breakout close has to clear. feature.breakout_50d is the rule (close above it on 1.5x volume); rollup.momentum@v1.prior_high_20d is the 20-session version.
