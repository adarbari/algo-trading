### `rollup.reverse_splits@v1.sessions_since_reverse_split`

**How to read it.** Exchange sessions from the ex-date of the latest reverse split up to the session. 0 means it took effect today. 5 is a week ago. The read goes back one year.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a reverse split in the last 20 sessions | `lte 20` | hard | - | illustrative: about a month, while the move is still news; no published cut-off |

**When the reading lies**

- Null means no reverse split was stored in the last 252 sessions, not that none happened. Funds reverse-split often, so screen operating companies only.

Sources: Desai and Jain (1997), Long-Run Common Stock Returns Following Stock Splits and Reverse Splits, Journal of Business

### `rollup.reverse_splits@v1.last_reverse_split_date`

**How to read it.** The ex-date of the latest reverse split: the first session at the new share price. Only splits with an ex-date on or before the session count, so a declared one is never read early.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| names that reverse-split within a year | `not_null` | hard | - | null means none stored in the window, not that none happened |

**When the reading lies**

- Null when no reverse split was stored in the last 252 sessions. A vendor can give a date a day off the real ex-date.

Sources: Site convention over the stored splits (ADR 0075)

### `rollup.reverse_splits@v1.reverse_split_ratio`

**How to read it.** New shares per old share of the latest reverse split. 0.1 means one new share for ten old, a 1-for-10. It is always below 1. A forward split has a ratio above 1 and is not read here.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a split of one for ten or deeper | `lte 0.1` | hard | - | illustrative: deep ratios are the weakest names; no published cut-off |

**When the reading lies**

- The ratio is the stored one. A vendor row that records a reverse split as a forward ratio is not read as one. Null when no reverse split was stored in the window.

Sources: Desai and Jain (1997), Long-Run Common Stock Returns Following Stock Splits and Reverse Splits, Journal of Business

### `rollup.reverse_splits@v1.reverse_splits_1y`

**How to read it.** How many reverse splits the name had in the last 252 sessions. 1 is a single split. 2 or more marks a company that had to do it again after a long decline.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| names that reverse-split more than once in a year | `gte 2` | hard | - | illustrative screen for repeat splitters; names with none drop out |

**When the reading lies**

- Counts the splits stored. A vendor with a shallow history can miss an early one. Null when the name had none in the window.

Sources: Site convention over the stored splits (ADR 0075)
