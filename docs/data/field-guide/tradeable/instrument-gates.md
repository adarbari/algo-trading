### `instrument.instrument_id`

**How to read it.** The instrument's own id. Identity, not a measure: used to gate a screen to a hand list of names (the listed business development companies of the bdc_price_to_nav screener), so hard, never soft or scored. The list is resolved from tickers once, through the symbol resolver, and keyed by id so a renamed company stays itself.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| one of these instruments | `in ["EQ:EXAMPLE1", "EQ:EXAMPLE2"]` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- A list by id is survivor-only: a company merged away or delisted before the list was made is missing from any history built on it. A newly listed name is missing until it is added.
- A list says nothing about what a name is; check the company before trusting an id copied from another list.

Sources: Site convention (docs/data/instruments.md; ADR 0018, FIGI ids)

### `rollup.factor_betas@v1.beta_size`

**How to read it.** How much a name moves with small companies against the market, over the last 252 sessions: the slope on IWM minus SPY, fitted together with the other factors. 0 is neutral; 0.5 means the name gains about half a percent when small caps beat the market by one percent; negative leans large. It is a loading, not a prediction.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a small-cap tilt | `gte 0.3` | soft | 0.1 | illustrative screen, not a published cut-off: 0.3 is a clear lean on a one-year fit; a loading against IWM minus SPY, not a size rank |

**When the reading lies**

- Unknown before about 2014. MTUM and VLUE, the momentum and value proxies, start in 2013, and the fit needs 200 sessions of every factor. factor_status says NO_FACTOR_HISTORY then. The value is never a market-only fit.
- Also null with SHORT_HISTORY: under 200 sessions of the name's own returns (a recent listing). Read rollup.factor_betas@v1.factor_obs for how many the fit used; under about 250 is noisy.
- The factors are ETFs, not the academic long-short portfolios. A loading of 0.3 is relative to those funds only, so rank exposures across names and never compare with a paper's number.

Sources: Fama and French (1993), Common risk factors in the returns on stocks and bonds, JFE 33(1); ETF proxies are a site choice

### `rollup.factor_betas@v1.beta_mom`

**How to read it.** How much a name moves with the momentum factor, over the last 252 sessions: the slope on MTUM minus SPY, fitted with the other factors. Positive means the name behaves like recent winners, negative like recent losers; 0 is neutral. A loading of 0.5 means half a percent for each percent the momentum fund beats the market.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a name that trades like recent winners | `gte 0.3` | soft | 0.1 | illustrative screen, not a published cut-off: 0.3 is a clear lean on a one-year fit; a loading against MTUM minus SPY |

**When the reading lies**

- Unknown before about 2014 (NO_FACTOR_HISTORY in rollup.factor_betas@v1.factor_status): MTUM started in 2013.
- Momentum funds rotate. MTUM's holdings change twice a year, so a loading fitted across a rebalance mixes two portfolios; read it as a one-year average.
- Null with SHORT_HISTORY; check rollup.factor_betas@v1.factor_obs.

Sources: Carhart (1997), On persistence in mutual fund performance, JF 52(1); the MTUM proxy is a site choice

### `rollup.factor_betas@v1.beta_sector`

**How to read it.** How much a name moves with its own sector against the market, over the last 252 sessions: the slope on its sector SPDR minus SPY, from the four-factor fit. 1 moves one for one with the sector's edge over the market; near 0 means the name ignores its sector. Use it to see whether a trade is a stock call or a sector call.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a name that moves with its sector | `gte 0.5` | soft | 0.2 | illustrative screen, not a published cut-off: half the sector ETF move marks a name that follows its sector; check factor_status for NO_SECTOR |

**When the reading lies**

- Null with NO_SECTOR when the session has no sector ETF for the name (rollup.relative_strength@v1.sector_etf is null: no company snapshot yet, or an ETF). The other betas are still set then.
- A sector ETF holds the name itself. A megacap's sector loading is therefore partly its own return.
- Unknown before about 2014 and with SHORT_HISTORY, as the other betas.

Sources: Site convention over the select-sector SPDR ETFs (rollup.relative_strength@v1.sector_etf)

### `rollup.factor_betas@v1.factor_obs`

**How to read it.** The sessions of the 252-session window in which the name and every factor all had a return: the observations the regression used. 252 is a full year; 200 is the minimum for any beta. A new listing or a name with trading halts has fewer.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a regression on a full year of sessions | `gte 250` | hard | - | illustrative screen: 250 is about a full year of sessions; below it the fit has fewer observations than the window and wider error |

**When the reading lies**

- Under about 250 the betas have wide error bands; a loading near 0.2 from 205 sessions cannot be told from 0. Treat the betas of a name with under 250 sessions as a rough guide.
- It counts sessions, not information: a long halt followed by a jump contributes few real observations.

Sources: Site convention (the regression window of rollup.factor_betas@v1)

### `rollup.factor_betas@v1.factor_status`

**How to read it.** Whether the factor loadings of the session could be fitted. OK: every beta is set. NO_SECTOR: all but beta_sector, as no sector ETF is known for the name. NO_FACTOR_HISTORY: a factor ETF has fewer than 200 returns in the window, which holds for every name before about 2014. SHORT_HISTORY: the name has fewer than 200 sessions with a return and every factor.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| names with every factor loading set | `eq "OK"` | hard | - | NO_FACTOR_HISTORY holds for every name before about 2014 |

**When the reading lies**

- NO_FACTOR_HISTORY is a limit of the data, not of the name: the same name is OK from about 2014 on. A backtest before then has no betas, and a rule on them rejects every name there.
- SHORT_HISTORY ends by itself once the name has 200 sessions; nothing needs fixing. A rule that must hold on new listings should not use the betas.

Sources: Site convention (the fit's minimum of 200 observations)

### `rollup.cef_nav@v1.nav_per_share`

**How to read it.** The latest net asset value per share of a closed-end fund known by the session: what the assets behind one share are worth, as the fund struck it. A fund at 10.00 NAV and 9.00 close trades at a 10% discount. It is the figure the discount is measured against.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| funds with a stored NAV | `not_null` | hard | - | personal-licence data; the discount is in rollup.cef_nav@v1.cef_discount_to_nav |

**When the reading lies**

- It is as struck, not split-adjusted; after a reverse split it will not match the adjusted close. rollup.cef_nav@v1.cef_discount_to_nav adjusts for splits before comparing.
- It is struck once a day after the close and shows from the session after its date, so on the session it is usually a day old (rollup.cef_nav@v1.nav_date). A fund holding foreign or illiquid assets prices its NAV stale at the close.
- The data is for personal use only (Tiingo): rule screens for the owner's own use only. Null with no NAV stored or none in the last 252 sessions.

Sources: Tiingo, Nasdaq Mutual Fund Quotation Service NAV series (personal licence)

### `rollup.cef_nav@v1.nav_date`

**How to read it.** The date the fund struck the NAV in rollup.cef_nav@v1.nav_per_share. The session minus this date is the NAV's age: 1 session is normal for a fund updated daily; 5 is the limit, since the discount is null beyond it.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| funds with a stored NAV to date the discount by | `not_null` | hard | - | personal-licence data; the discount is null when the NAV is over 5 sessions old |

**When the reading lies**

- NAV dates lag prices. The discount pairs the NAV with the close of the same date, not the session's close, so a day of price move never counts as discount. Don't compare nav_per_share against today's close by hand.
- A NAV restated later replaces the first one from the session that learned of it; a past session never sees the restatement.
- Null with no NAV stored or none dated in the last 252 sessions. Personal-licence data.

Sources: Tiingo, Nasdaq Mutual Fund Quotation Service NAV series (personal licence)

### `rollup.etf_flows@v1.etf_shares_outstanding`

**How to read it.** The issuer's latest count of shares outstanding for an iShares fund known by the session. A fund creates shares when money comes in and redeems them when it goes out, so the count is its size in shares. A count of 500,000,000 up from 480,000,000 a month earlier is 4% net creation.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| iShares funds with a recent share count | `not_null` | hard | - | other issuers' funds have no count |

**When the reading lies**

- iShares files only: SPDR, Vanguard and every other issuer's funds have none, and the stored history starts about 2026-10-17, so no change exists before a month later.
- It is the issuer's date, which can be a few days before the session (rollup.etf_flows@v1.etf_shares_asof); a count more than 30 days old is not shown.
- A share count is not dollars: a price change moves assets but not the count. For the flow see rollup.etf_flows@v1.etf_shares_chg_21d. Null for a fund without a recent count.

Sources: iShares fund files (issuer's daily shares outstanding), edge build D4

### `rollup.etf_flows@v1.etf_shares_asof`

**How to read it.** The issuer's date of the share count in rollup.etf_flows@v1.etf_shares_outstanding. A fund listed daily has a date one or two sessions old; one the issuer lists weekly can be up to 7 days old. The session minus this date is the age of the count.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| funds with a recent share count to date the flow by | `not_null` | hard | - | the 21-session change needs a count within 7 days of the session |

**When the reading lies**

- The 21-session change is null when this date is more than 7 days before the session. Check it before reading a flow as quiet: an old date means no data, not no flow.
- A count the issuer restated is seen from the session that fetched it, never earlier.
- Null for a fund with no count in the last 30 days.

Sources: iShares fund files (issuer's daily shares outstanding), edge build D4

### `rollup.share_classes@v1.class_count`

**How to read it.** How many share classes of the issuer the reference lists: 2 for GOOG and GOOGL or for BRK.A and BRK.B, 3 or more for a company with several. It only counts classes; rollup.share_classes@v1.sibling_status says whether the classes make a clean pair.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| issuers with more than one share class | `gte 2` | hard | - | two or more classes is the definition of a pair candidate; then read sibling_status, as three or more is AMBIGUOUS |

**When the reading lies**

- Null for an issuer with one listed class or without an issuer or class id in the reference, so null is the common case, not a gap.
- Three or more classes, or one class listed under several ids, is AMBIGUOUS in rollup.share_classes@v1.sibling_status: no pair is guessed, so spread rules skip the name.
- It counts the classes in the snapshot the session sees; a class added or dropped shows from that snapshot, never from today's reference.

Sources: Site convention over the instrument reference (issuer CIK and share-class FIGI)

### `rollup.cohort_pct_price@v1.mom_12_1_pct_universe`

**How to read it.** Where the 12-1 momentum (the 12-month return without the last month) ranks among the universe's stocks: 0 the weakest, 1 the strongest, 0.9 a top-decile year. A rank is comparable across market regimes where the raw return is not.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a top-decile year of momentum | `gte 0.9` | soft | 0.05 | with rollup.cohort_pct_price@v1.mom_12_1_pct_sector gte 0.8 so the lead is also within its own sector |

**When the reading lies**

- A rank hides magnitude: in a flat year the top decile may have gained little. Read rollup.trend_stats@v2.mom_12_1 for the size.
- Only STOCKS of the universe snapshot are ranked, so an ETF, fund or trust is null.
- Every name is null when fewer than 200 stocks have a known value, or when fewer than 90% of the universe's stocks have a price_stats row (a partial day).
- The strongest year-long winners are the most extended names: add feature.stretch_sma50_atr lte 4 to avoid chasing.

Sources: Jegadeesh and Titman (1993), Returns to buying winners and selling losers, Journal of Finance

### `rollup.cohort_pct_options@v1.iv_rank_252d_pct_universe`

**How to read it.** Where the 252-session IV rank ranks among the universe's stocks: 0 the calmest options, 1 the richest. It shows which names have expensive options next to the market, where the raw IV rank only says where a name sits against its own year.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rich options among the universe's stocks | `gte 0.8` | soft | 0.05 | with the raw figure so the size is read too |

**When the reading lies**

- Only STOCKS of the universe snapshot are ranked, so an ETF, fund or trust is null. Null too when fewer than 200 stocks have a known value.
- It ranks our own IV rank (from our open IV30), never the blended rank that may carry an IBKR value, so it stays open to every viewer.
- A name with too little IV history, or a thin chain, has no IV rank and is not counted in its cohort.

Sources: Natenberg (1994), Option Volatility and Pricing: volatility rank

### `rollup.short_interest@v1.short_position`

**How to read it.** Shares sold short at the latest settlement date whose report was public by the session (FINRA, all markets). 2,000,000 against a share count of 40 million is 5% of the company short. The figure is a stock, not a flow: it says how large the bet is, not whether it is growing.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| names with a public short-interest report | `not_null` | hard | - | rule screens only: FINRA data is not for ML use |

**When the reading lies**

- It is old by design: FINRA publishes on the 8th business day after the settlement date, so the figure is about two weeks old when it first shows. A squeeze can have come and gone; rollup.short_interest@v1.short_interest_date says which day it describes.
- Reports come twice a month, so the figure holds still for about 10 sessions and then jumps; don't read a day-to-day change into it.
- Null when no report became public in the last 30 sessions: unknown, never 'no shorts'. FINRA data is not for ML use here: rule screens only, no learned scorer reads it.
- It counts short positions, not borrow cost or the float; read feature.short_interest_ratio and rollup.short_interest@v1.days_to_cover for the crowding.

Sources: FINRA, Equity short interest data and publication schedule: https://www.finra.org/finra-data/browse-catalog/equity-short-interest

### `rollup.short_interest@v1.short_interest_date`

**How to read it.** The settlement date the latest public short-interest report describes. It is about two weeks before the report was public (T+8 business days). A session late in the month shows the figure of the 15th or the month end before it. The age of the short data is the session minus this date.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| names with a public short-interest report | `not_null` | hard | - | compare the date with the session to see how old the figure is; rule screens only |

**When the reading lies**

- Check it before trusting the short fields: a date more than 20 sessions old means FINRA's next report is missing for the name, and the figures are stale.
- It is the date of the figure, not of its publication; a screen at the session sees only what was public then, never a report that came out later.
- Null when no report was public in the last 30 sessions (unknown, never 'no shorts'). FINRA data is not for ML use here.

Sources: FINRA, Equity short interest data and publication schedule: https://www.finra.org/finra-data/browse-catalog/equity-short-interest

### `instrument.status`

**How to read it.** Whether the listing is live: ACTIVE for a name in the current reference snapshot, DELISTED for one that left it (instrument.delisted_on says when). A screen almost always gates on ACTIVE; the drafting examples do. It is identity, not a measure, so it is hard, never soft or scored.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| listed today (always) | `eq "ACTIVE"` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- DELISTED names keep their history: a backtest or a results table can show one with stored features. The gate is what keeps them out of today's picks.
- A name that was acquired is DELISTED only after the deal closes; before that it is ACTIVE and pinned (see the 'pending takeover' situation).

Sources: Site convention (docs/data/instruments.md)

### `instrument.security_type`

**How to read it.** What kind of listing it is: COMMON_STOCK, ADR (a foreign company's US receipt) or ETF, among others. 'Stocks' is COMMON_STOCK and ADR; 'ETFs' is ETF. A company screen (P/E, revenue growth, market cap) wants stocks only: funds have no financials and ADRs have no P/E here.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| stocks | `in ["COMMON_STOCK", "ADR"]` | hard | - | - |
| ETFs | `in ["ETF"]` | hard | - | - |
| stocks and funds (options screens) | `in ["COMMON_STOCK", "ADR", "ETF"]` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- An ADR passes a 'stocks' gate but feature.pe_ratio is null for it (the ADR ratio is not stored) and its filings are foreign; add rollup.financials@v3.is_adr eq false when the screen reads earnings.
- ETFs include leveraged and inverse funds; a fund screen that wants plain index exposure adds instrument.is_leveraged eq false and instrument.is_inverse eq false.

Sources: Site convention (docs/data/instruments.md; the vendor's type wins over the name rules)

### `instrument.optionable`

**How to read it.** Whether listed options exist on the name. True is the gate for any options screen; it says nothing about how tradable those options are, which is feature.option_tier and the chain's open interest and volume.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| has listed options | `eq true` | hard | - | add feature.option_tier in [A, B] for tradable ones |

**When the reading lies**

- Optionable with feature.option_tier D is a chain nobody trades: gate on the tier as well for a trade, not only on the flag.
- A newly optionable name has no IV history: feature.iv_rank is null for its first 60 sessions.

Sources: Site convention (the reference snapshot's optionable flag)

### `instrument.is_leveraged`

**How to read it.** True for a fund that targets a multiple of its index's daily return (instrument.leverage gives the multiple). Such funds reset daily and drift from the index over time (volatility decay), so trend, momentum and volatility readings describe the fund's path, not the market's.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no leveraged funds | `eq false` | hard | - | pair with instrument.is_inverse eq false |

**When the reading lies**

- A stock screen that does not exclude these finds 2x and 3x funds at the top of every momentum and volatility list; see the 'leveraged or inverse fund' situation.

Sources: StockCharts, Why technical analysis does not work for leveraged ETFs: https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-finalbar-2024-06-why-technical-analysis-does-no-911/

### `instrument.is_inverse`

**How to read it.** True for a fund that targets the opposite of its index's daily return. An inverse fund in a 'downtrend' is a rising market; every directional reading on it is reversed and decays like a leveraged one.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no inverse funds | `eq false` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Exclude from stock and trend screens with the leveraged flag; screen them deliberately or not at all (the 'leveraged or inverse fund' situation).

Sources: StockCharts, Why technical analysis does not work for leveraged ETFs: https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-finalbar-2024-06-why-technical-analysis-does-no-911/

### `instrument.in_sp500`

**How to read it.** Whether the name is an S&P 500 constituent in the reference snapshot. Membership is a liquidity and quality filter in one flag (the index requires profitability and float), and index flows make members trade differently: additions get bid, deletions sold, and every member moves with the ETF rebalances.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S&P 500 members only | `eq true` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Membership changes quarterly and on corporate events; a name added last week has the flag and none of the history as a member.
- Not a size cut-off: the smallest members are around $10B and some $50B companies are not in (foreign domiciles, dual-class rules). feature.market_cap is the size.
- Null when the reference does not know; false for every ETF.

Sources: S&P Dow Jones Indices, S&P 500 eligibility criteria

### `instrument.sector`

**How to read it.** The company's sector as derived from its SEC industry code (SIC): the broad group (energy, financials, health care, technology and so on). A screen uses it to compare like with like, because every valuation and volatility threshold is sector-dependent: a P/E of 12 is rich for a bank and cheap for software, an IV of 0.40 is calm for a biotech.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| one sector | `eq "Technology"` | hard | - | the values are the catalogue's sector labels; use in [..] for several |

**When the reading lies**

- SIC-derived, so the labels follow the SEC's old industry codes rather than GICS: a platform company can sit under 'services', a conglomerate under its original business. instrument.industry is the finer SIC description.
- Null for funds (no SIC) and companies without a filing; a hard sector criterion drops every ETF.

Sources: SEC SIC codes (docs/data/instruments.md); sector-relative valuation as a practitioner convention

### `instrument.industry`

**How to read it.** The company's industry as the SEC's SIC description gives it (a few hundred labels: 'pharmaceutical preparations', 'semiconductors and related devices', 'state commercial banks'). Finer than instrument.sector: the peer group a company is valued and traded against.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| one industry | `in ["Pharmaceutical Preparations"]` | hard | - | the values are the catalogue's industry labels, spelled exactly |

**When the reading lies**

- SIC descriptions are decades old and self-reported at registration: many technology companies are 'services-prepackaged software' and a company that changed business keeps its original code.
- Null for funds and companies without a filing.

Sources: SEC SIC codes (docs/data/instruments.md)

### `instrument.is_etf`

**How to read it.** True for an exchange-traded fund (and the trusts and notes the reference classes with them). A fund has no earnings, revenue or market cap here, its on-screen volume understates its liquidity (creation and redemption), and its 'sector' is null; the company fields do not apply and a company screen should exclude it.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| funds only | `eq true` | hard | - | - |
| no funds (a company screen) | `eq false` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Includes leveraged and inverse funds; instrument.is_leveraged and instrument.is_inverse single those out.
- instrument.security_type in [ETF] is the same gate in the three-way form; this flag is the one-bit version.

Sources: Site convention (docs/data/instruments.md)

### `rollup.relative_strength@v1.rs_spy_63d`

**How to read it.** Relative strength against SPY over a quarter (63 sessions): (1 + the name's return) / (1 + SPY's return) - 1. 0 is level with the market, 0.10 is ten points ahead of it, -0.10 ten points behind; the value is a ratio of growth factors, so it is the same whatever the market did (a name up 5% in a market down 5% reads +0.105). Above 0 for the last quarter is the usual meaning of 'outperforming SPY' (O'Neil, Mansfield: buy leaders, not laggards).

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| outperforming SPY over the quarter | `gt 0.0` | soft | 0.02 | feature.rs_spy_positive is the same gate as a flag; add rollup.relative_strength@v1.rs_spy_trend_20d gt 0 to want it improving |
| a clear market leader | `gte 0.1` | soft | 0.03 | ten points ahead of SPY; with rollup.relative_strength@v1.mom_pctile_63d gte 0.8 so the lead is also a top-quintile return |

**When the reading lies**

- A name that rose on one gap (a takeover bid, an earnings jump) reads as a leader for the whole quarter. Check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move and rollup.relative_strength@v1.rs_spy_trend_20d: a leader whose trend is falling has stopped leading.
- Against SPY every name in a rotating market looks strong or weak by its sector. Pair with rollup.relative_strength@v1.rs_sector_63d to separate stock strength from sector strength.
- Price return only (no dividends), split-adjusted: a high-yield name trails its total return by its yield.
- Null when the name or SPY has a gap in the 64 closes, or SPY is not in the symbol map; a hard criterion then rejects the name.

Sources: O'Neil (1988), How to Make Money in Stocks: relative price strength; Levy (1967), Relative strength as a criterion for investment selection, Journal of Finance; Mansfield relative strength: https://www.investopedia.com/terms/r/relativestrength.asp

### `rollup.relative_strength@v1.rs_spy_252d`

**How to read it.** Relative strength against SPY over a year (252 sessions): (1 + the name's return) / (1 + SPY's return) - 1. The long-run leadership reading: above 0 the name has beaten the market over the year, 0.30 is thirty points ahead. It moves slowly; a quarter that turned (rs_spy_63d) takes months to show here.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a year-long market leader | `gt 0.0` | soft | 0.05 | with rollup.relative_strength@v1.rs_spy_63d gt 0 so the leadership is current, not a year-old move |

**When the reading lies**

- A year back is one start date: a name that fell hard 252 sessions ago and recovered reads a big positive value that says 'bounce', not 'leader'. Check rollup.trend_stats@v2.mom_12_1 (skips the last month) and rollup.relative_strength@v1.rs_spy_63d.
- A recent listing or a gap in the year gives null, never a shorter window: a young name is invisible to a hard criterion on this field.
- Price return only (no dividends).

Sources: O'Neil (1988), How to Make Money in Stocks: the 12-month relative price strength rank; Jegadeesh and Titman (1993), Returns to buying winners and selling losers, Journal of Finance

### `rollup.relative_strength@v1.rs_line_high_252d`

**How to read it.** True when the relative-strength line (the close divided by SPY's close) stands at its highest of the last 252 sessions, today included: the name is outperforming at a new high, even if its own price is not yet at one. A rising line into a breakout is the classic leader tell (O'Neil's 'blue dot'); False means the line peaked earlier in the year.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the RS line at a new high | `eq true` | hard | - | a score input more than a gate: combine with feature.breakout_20d for a leader breaking out |

**When the reading lies**

- A flat line while SPY and the name rise together can still tie its own high on a day the market dips; one day proves little. Check rollup.relative_strength@v1.rs_spy_trend_20d for whether the line has been climbing.
- It looks back one year only: a name that peaked relative to the market 14 months ago can print True after a long sag. Check rollup.relative_strength@v1.rs_spy_252d.
- Null when the name or SPY has a gap in the last 252 sessions, or SPY is not in the symbol map.

Sources: O'Neil (1988), How to Make Money in Stocks: the relative strength line at a new high

### `rollup.relative_strength@v1.rs_spy_trend_20d`

**How to read it.** How relative strength against SPY changed over the last 20 sessions: rs_spy_63d today minus rs_spy_63d a month ago. Above 0 relative strength is improving (the name is gaining on the market), below 0 deteriorating; 0.03 means three points better than a month ago. It is the 'RS improving' of a screen, a change rather than a level.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| relative strength improving | `gt 0.0` | soft | 0.02 | feature.rs_improving is the same gate as a flag; pair with rollup.relative_strength@v1.rs_spy_63d gt 0 to exclude laggards that merely stopped falling |
| an upward trend soon against the market | `gt 0.0` | soft | 0.02 | a name gaining on SPY over the last month; a leader's trend can still be falling, so pair with rollup.relative_strength@v1.rs_spy_63d gt 0 |

**When the reading lies**

- A name that was far behind and merely stopped falling reads positive while still lagging. Pair with rollup.relative_strength@v1.rs_spy_63d gt 0 for improving and ahead.
- The two 63-session windows overlap by 43 sessions, so the change comes from the 20 newest and the 20 oldest sessions: read it as a month's drift, not a day's move.
- Null when either 63-session window has a gap (SPY's included): 84 closes must be whole.

Sources: Relative-strength momentum of the line's slope, a standard charting reading: https://school.stockcharts.com/doku.php?id=technical_indicators:price_relative

### `rollup.relative_strength@v1.ret_5d_pctile`

**How to read it.** Where the 5-session return ranks among the universe's stocks: the share of universe members (the stocks of the snapshot the session sees) whose return is strictly below the name's, among members with a known return. 0.9 means the name beat 90% of stocks this week, 0.1 only 10%. It is a rank, so it is comparable across market regimes; the best member reads slightly under 1.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a top-quintile week | `gte 0.8` | soft | 0.05 | with rollup.relative_strength@v1.mom_pctile_63d gte 0.7; alone it finds one-week spikes |

**When the reading lies**

- A week is noisy: a top-decile week is as often a spike that mean-reverts as a start. Pair with rollup.relative_strength@v1.mom_pctile_63d gte 0.7 for a name that is strong over the quarter too.
- ETFs are ranked against stocks but never count as members, so an ETF's rank says how it would sit among stocks.
- Null with no universe snapshot on or before the session, fewer than 90% of its members with a bar on the session (min_coverage), fewer than 200 members with a known return (min_members), or a gap in the name's own 6 closes.

Sources: Cross-sectional momentum ranks: Jegadeesh and Titman (1993), Journal of Finance

### `rollup.relative_strength@v1.mom_pctile_63d`

**How to read it.** Where the 63-session (a quarter) return ranks among the universe's stocks: the share of universe members whose return is strictly below the name's, among members with a known return. 0.9 is the top decile of the quarter, 0.5 the median stock, 0.1 the bottom decile. 'Top decile momentum' is this at or above 0.9.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| top decile momentum | `gte 0.9` | soft | 0.05 | with feature.pct_vs_sma_200 gt 0 so a bounce in a downtrend does not qualify |
| a top-quintile quarter | `gte 0.8` | soft | 0.05 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Ties share the lower rank, and the best member reads slightly under 1 (n - 1 of n are below it): gte 0.9 is a top decile, not 'above 90% of everything'.
- A rank hides magnitude: in a flat market the top decile may have gained 5%. Read rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_60d for the size.
- Top-decile quarterly winners are the most extended names: add feature.stretch_sma50_atr lte 4 to avoid chasing.
- Null with no universe snapshot on or before the session, fewer than 90% of its members with a bar on the session (min_coverage), fewer than 200 members with a known return (min_members), or a gap in the name's own 64 closes.

Sources: Jegadeesh and Titman (1993), Returns to buying winners and selling losers, Journal of Finance; O'Neil (1988), How to Make Money in Stocks: relative price strength rank (top 20%)

### `rollup.relative_strength@v1.mom_pctile_252d`

**How to read it.** Where the 252-session (a year) return ranks among the universe's stocks: the share of universe members whose return is strictly below the name's, among members with a known return. 0.9 is the top decile of the year, 0.5 the median. The classic momentum rank; it includes the last month, which mom_12_1 skips.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| top decile momentum over the year | `gte 0.9` | soft | 0.05 | with rollup.relative_strength@v1.mom_pctile_63d gte 0.7 so the year's leadership is still running |

**When the reading lies**

- It includes the most recent month, where short-term reversal works against it: a name at 0.95 after a spike this month may pull back. Check rollup.trend_stats@v2.mom_12_1 and rollup.relative_strength@v1.ret_5d_pctile.
- A rank over a year favours names that are already extended; add feature.pct_vs_sma_50 lte 0.2 when you do not want to chase.
- Null with no universe snapshot on or before the session, fewer than 90% of its members with a bar on the session (min_coverage), fewer than 200 members with a known return (min_members), or a gap in the name's own 253 closes (a recent listing).

Sources: Jegadeesh and Titman (1993), Returns to buying winners and selling losers, Journal of Finance; Fama and French (2012), Size, value and momentum in international stock returns

### `rollup.relative_strength@v1.sector_etf`

**How to read it.** The ticker of the sector ETF the name's sector maps to: Technology XLK, Health Care XLV, Financials XLF, Consumer Discretionary XLY, Consumer Staples XLP, Energy XLE, Industrials XLI, Materials XLB, Utilities XLU, Real Estate XLRE, Communication Services XLC. The sector is a coarse market sector derived from the SEC SIC code, not a GICS sector; it names the benchmark that sector_ret_63d, rs_sector_63d and sector_rank_63d compare against.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| technology names | `eq "XLK"` | hard | - | a sector filter; with rollup.relative_strength@v1.sector_rank_63d lte 3 for names in a leading sector |

**When the reading lies**

- The SIC code is the company's old filing category: a name that is now a software business filed under an industrial code reads Industrials. Treat the sector as a first approximation and check instrument.name or the SEC description for a name that matters.
- ETFs and funds listed as such in the universe have no sector here (null), so a sector screen drops them; gate on instrument.security_type for stocks anyway.

Sources: SPDR Select Sector ETFs: https://www.sectorspdrs.com; SEC SIC code list: https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/standard-industrial-classification-sic-code-list

### `rollup.relative_strength@v1.sector_ret_63d`

**How to read it.** The 63-session (a quarter) return of the name's sector ETF: how the sector has done. 0.08 is a sector up 8% in a quarter, below 0 a sector in decline. It is the benchmark for rs_sector_63d and the number the sector rank orders.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a rising sector | `gt 0.0` | soft | 0.02 | with rollup.relative_strength@v1.sector_rank_63d lte 5 so the sector is also above the middle of the eleven |

**When the reading lies**

- An ETF's price return leaves out dividends, which matter for Utilities and Real Estate: their sector return runs about a point per quarter below total return.
- A sector can rise because three mega-caps carry it (XLK, XLC): a strong sector return is not breadth. Check feature.sector_leader and the name's own rollup.relative_strength@v1.rs_sector_63d.
- Null when sector_etf is null, or the ETF is not in the symbol map or has a gap in its 64 closes.

Sources: SPDR Select Sector ETFs: https://www.sectorspdrs.com

### `rollup.relative_strength@v1.rs_sector_63d`

**How to read it.** Relative strength against the name's own sector ETF over a quarter: (1 + the name's return) / (1 + the ETF's return) - 1. Above 0 the name beat its sector, 0.10 ten points ahead; it separates stock strength from sector strength (a name up 20% in a sector up 20% reads 0).

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| beating its sector | `gt 0.0` | soft | 0.02 | with rollup.relative_strength@v1.rs_spy_63d gt 0 for a name that leads both its sector and the market |

**When the reading lies**

- The sector benchmark is the SIC heuristic's: a name classified into the wrong sector is measured against the wrong ETF. Check rollup.relative_strength@v1.sector_etf.
- A heavyweight is a large part of its own sector ETF, so its relative strength against it is damped toward 0.
- Null when sector_ret_63d is null or the name's own 64 closes have a gap.

Sources: Levy (1967), Relative strength as a criterion for investment selection, Journal of Finance
