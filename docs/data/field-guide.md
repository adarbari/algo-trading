# Field guide: how to read the catalogue's fields

Generated by `make features-doc` from `config/site/field_guide/` (one file per theme); edit those, not this page.
The same guide is shown by the Builder with each field and sent to the text model that drafts
a screen from a sentence (ADR 0041), so a threshold a person reads here is the threshold the
model proposes.

Every field is read the same way: **how to read it** (what low and high mean, in the field's
unit: a decimal field takes 0.10 for 10%), **the criterion per intent** (what a trader wants,
and the rule-screen criterion that expresses it: the operator, the value, the mode and the
near-miss band of [rules.md](../screeners/rules.md); `hard` rejects a miss, `soft` keeps a
near miss as a WATCH or a risk, `score` only ranks), and **when the reading lies**: the
situations in which the number is right and the conclusion is wrong, each with the field
that exposes it. The [situations](#situations-that-fool-several-thresholds) at the end are the
ones that distort many fields at once; a screen that ignores them finds the same false
positives whatever its thresholds. A null never passes a `hard` criterion (missing data never
passes); a field's own catalogue entry ([features.md](features.md)) says when it is null.

37 fields: [Events](#events) · [Fundamentals](#fundamentals) · [Liquidity](#liquidity) · [Momentum and trend](#momentum-and-trend) · [Volatility](#volatility).

## Events

### `rollup.earnings@v1.days_to_earnings`

**How to read it.** Exchange sessions until the next report: 0 reports today, 1 tomorrow, 10 is two weeks out. Options price the report from about ten sessions before (the IV ramp accelerates in the last two or three) and lose that premium within a session after it. 'Avoid earnings' for a short option held a month is days_to_earnings above the holding period; 'play earnings' is under 5.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no earnings in the next N sessions (short options) | `gt 10` | soft | 2, EVENT_RISK | N is the holding period; 25 for a 30-day short option held to expiry; or feature.earnings_before_expiry eq false |
| earnings this week (an event play) | `lte 5` | hard | - | add rollup.earnings@v1.date_confirmed eq true when the date must be right |

**When the reading lies**

- Null means no report date is known for the name, not 'no report soon': ETFs have none, and a company's next date is often unknown until it announces it (two to four weeks before). A hard 'gt 10' therefore rejects every ETF and every company that has not announced; use soft with EVENT_RISK.
- Unconfirmed dates move: rollup.earnings@v1.date_confirmed false (the Nasdaq calendar never confirms) means the date is an estimate from last year's pattern and can shift by days.
- rollup.earnings@v1.earnings_time says before the open (pre) or after the close (post): 'reports tomorrow, pre' means the move happens before you can act.

Sources: IV ramp and crush around earnings: https://flashalpha.com/articles/iv-crush-explained-earnings-volatility-collapse and https://optionalpha.com/learn/iv-crush

### `feature.earnings_before_expiry`

**How to read it.** True when the next report falls on or before the nearest listed expiry: a short option held to that expiry carries the earnings move. False is the clean case for premium selling at the nearest expiry; for a later expiry read rollup.earnings@v1.days_to_earnings against its days.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no earnings before the nearest expiry | `eq false` | soft | 0, EVENT_RISK | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Null when the report date or the nearest expiry is unknown (no date announced, no stored chain): a hard 'eq false' rejects those names; soft with EVENT_RISK keeps them listed.
- The nearest expiry can be a weekly a few days out; a 30-day short option needs days_to_earnings against its own expiry, not this flag.

Sources: Site convention over the earnings calendar and the stored chains

## Fundamentals

### `feature.market_cap`

**How to read it.** Shares outstanding times the close, in dollars. The usual US buckets: micro under $300M, small $300M to $2B, mid $2B to $10B, large $10B to $200B, mega above $200B. They are conventions, not rules: index providers draw the lines differently and names migrate across them as prices move.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| large cap | `gte 10000000000` | hard | - | - |
| mid cap | `between [2000000000, 10000000000]` | hard | - | - |
| small cap | `between [300000000, 2000000000]` | hard | - | under 300000000 is micro cap; pair with a liquidity gate |

**When the reading lies**

- The count is the company total and the price is this share class's, so a company with two listed classes shows the same cap on both only when they trade at the same price. Check rollup.fundamentals@v2.market_cap_status.
- Null for funds (no share count filed), for a stale count (rollup.fundamentals@v2.market_cap_status STALE) and for a company with no SEC filing (foreign private issuers); a hard size gate drops ETFs and many ADRs.
- Cap is size, not quality or liquidity: a $20B name can trade $5M a day (a controlled company) and a $500M name $50M (a meme). Pair with rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d.

Sources: Market-cap bucket conventions: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_capitalization and Morningstar's definitions

### `feature.pe_ratio`

**How to read it.** Close over diluted earnings per share of the trailing twelve months. The long-run market average is around 15 to 20; under 10 is cheap on its face, above 30 is priced for growth. It is only shown when the company earned money, when the figure is recent and when the shares are not an ADR, so a null is common and meaningful.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cheap on trailing earnings | `lt 15` | soft | 3 | with feature.revenue_growth_yoy gte 0 to keep out shrinking businesses; remember loss makers are null, not cheap |
| not priced for perfection | `lte 30` | score | 10 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Cyclicals look cheapest at the top of their cycle: an oil producer or a chip maker at a P/E of 6 on peak earnings becomes 18 when earnings normalise without the price moving. Pair a low P/E with feature.revenue_growth_yoy and instrument.sector, or compare against several years of earnings (not stored).
- A low P/E is often a value trap: the market has priced in falling earnings, a shrinking industry or heavy debt. A low P/E in feature.trend_state DOWNTREND is the market disagreeing with the ratio.
- One-offs inside the twelve months (an asset sale, a tax gain, a write-down) swing EPS and the ratio; nothing here separates them.
- Null for loss makers (EPS at or below 0), ADRs and stale filings: a hard 'pe_ratio lt 15' silently removes every unprofitable company and every ADR. If that is not the intent, use mode score, or add rollup.financials@v1.financials_status.
- Sector ranges differ by a factor of three (banks and energy low, software high); a single market-wide cut-off mostly selects sectors.

Sources: Problems with taking P/E at face value: https://anurimabanerjee.substack.com/p/problems-with-taking-pe-at-face-value; Cyclical peak earnings and value traps: https://moneyweek.com/10368/the-pitfalls-of-value-investing-53516; Graham and Dodd; Shiller's cyclically adjusted P/E for the multi-year averaging idea

### `feature.revenue_growth_yoy`

**How to read it.** Trailing-twelve-month revenue against the same twelve months a year earlier, as a fraction: 0.10 is 10% growth. Around 0.03 to 0.05 is nominal GDP-like; above 0.15 is a growth company; above 0.30 is rare outside early-stage names; negative is a shrinking business. It lags: the TTM ends at the last filed quarter, which can be four months old.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| growing revenue | `gte 0.1` | soft | 0.03 | - |
| fast growth | `gte 0.25` | soft | 0.05 | check for an acquisition when the jump is sudden |
| not shrinking | `gte 0` | soft | 0.03 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Acquisitions inflate reported growth and divestitures deflate it; companies report 'organic' growth for that reason and the catalogue has only the reported figure. A jump from 5% to 40% is usually a deal. Check instrument.sic or the company's filings before reading it as demand.
- A tiny year-ago base makes huge percentages (a company going from $10M to $30M is 200%); pair with feature.market_cap or rollup.financials@v1.revenue_ttm.
- Currency moves show up as growth or shrinkage for companies that earn abroad.
- Null with under two years of filings (recent IPOs, foreign filers, funds): a hard criterion rejects them. rollup.financials@v1.financials_status says why.

Sources: Organic versus reported revenue growth, as defined in issuers' filings (e.g. PepsiCo, Moody's 10-K definitions on sec.gov)

### `feature.div_yield`

**How to read it.** Dividends paid in the trailing twelve months over the close, as a fraction: 0.03 is a 3% yield. The S&P 500 yields about 0.013 to 0.02; 0.03 to 0.05 is a solid income name; above 0.08 is where the market is usually pricing a cut (a yield trap: the yield rose because the price fell on the news that the payout cannot last); above 0.10 is a warning, not an opportunity.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pays a meaningful dividend | `gte 0.03` | soft | 0.005 | - |
| not a yield trap | `lte 0.08` | soft | 0.01 | with feature.trend_state ne DOWNTREND |

**When the reading lies**

- A yield trap looks best right before the cut: a high yield with feature.trend_state DOWNTREND or rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_60d well below 0 is the market expecting the cut. Pair high-yield rules with a trend filter or with earnings coverage (rollup.financials@v1.eps_diluted_ttm above the dividend).
- A special dividend inflates the trailing twelve months for a year: rollup.dividends@v2.div_count_ttm above 4 for a quarterly payer is the sign.
- REITs, MLPs, BDCs and bond or covered-call ETFs yield 0.06 to 0.12 by structure; a market-wide 'above 5%' screen is mostly a list of those. Check instrument.sector and instrument.is_etf.
- Null for a non-payer with too little history to say so; 0 for a known non-payer. A hard 'gte 0.03' drops both, which is usually intended.

Sources: Yield traps and payout ratios (yields above 10% and payouts above 100% precede cuts): https://www.livewiremarkets.com/wires/how-to-avoid-dividend-traps and https://indexes.morningstar.com/insights/perspective/bltdb6d3e043f097825/not-all-dividend-stocks-are-safe-heres-how-to-avoid-dividend-traps

## Liquidity

### `rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d`

**How to read it.** Average dollars traded a day over 20 sessions (close times volume). The usual sizing rule is to hold no more than 1% to 2% of a day's volume, so the threshold follows from the position: a $50,000 position wants about $5M a day, $500,000 wants $50M. $5M is 'some volume', $50M is liquid, $100M and above is very liquid; under $1M a day is where spreads and slippage dominate any edge.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| liquid (a $500k position) | `gte 50000000` | soft | 0.2 x the threshold, LIQUIDITY_RISK | 'very liquid' 100000000; 'some volume' 5000000; or feature.liquidity_class in [HIGH, MEDIUM] |
| tradable at all (a small account) | `gte 5000000` | soft | 0.2 x the threshold, LIQUIDITY_RISK | - |

**When the reading lies**

- An ETF's on-screen volume understates its liquidity: market makers create and redeem shares, so a fund with thin volume can still absorb a large order if its holdings are liquid. For funds (instrument.is_etf eq true) a low ADV is not disqualifying by itself.
- One event day (earnings, an index inclusion) lifts a 20-day average for 20 sessions; a name just over a threshold may be thinner than it looks. Check rollup.momentum@v1.rel_volume history through rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move.
- Dollar volume, not shares: a $2 stock trading 10M shares is $20M a day, the same as a $200 stock trading 100k shares, and the $2 one has a far wider spread. Pair with rollup.price_stats@v2.close.

Sources: The 1% to 2% of ADV sizing rule: https://daytradingtoolkit.com/dictionary/liquidity; Position-level liquidity (days to exit, share of daily volume): https://www.alphatheory.com/blog/managing-portfolio-liquidity-position-level-calculation

### `rollup.price_stats@v2.close`

**How to read it.** The session's closing price in dollars. Under $5 is 'penny' territory (many brokers and funds exclude it; the tick is a large share of the price); under $10 spreads and ATR as a share of price are elevated; a price floor is almost always a hard gate.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a price floor | `gt 10` | hard | - | 5 for a looser floor; 'penny stocks' is close lt 5 |

**When the reading lies**

- A high price is not 'expensive' and a low price is not 'cheap'; valuation is feature.pe_ratio and size is feature.market_cap.
- Split-adjusted history: a stock that reverse-split 1:10 last month shows a continuous series and a 'high' price with the same thin market it had before.

Sources: Broker and index conventions (the $5 penny-stock line)

### `feature.liquidity_class`

**How to read it.** HIGH when every HIGH threshold holds ($100M a day, close at least $10, option tier A, chain open interest 50k and volume 5k), MEDIUM when every MEDIUM one does ($10M, $5, tier A or B, open interest 5k), else LOW; UNKNOWN when an input is missing. A composite for stock plus options liquidity in one word.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| liquid stock and options | `in ["HIGH", "MEDIUM"]` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- It bundles option liquidity: a very liquid stock with no options is LOW. For a stock-only screen use rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d instead.
- UNKNOWN, not LOW, when the chain run did not cover the session; a hard 'in [HIGH, MEDIUM]' rejects those names on a day the chains failed.

Sources: Site thresholds (config/site/features/liquidity.toml)

### `feature.option_tier`

**How to read it.** The worse of the put and call liquidity tiers at the target expiry, A (tight, deep) to D (no usable options). A and B are tradable for a retail account; C fills badly; D has no usable series. The tiers come from the near-the-money spread as a share of the mid, open interest and volume.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| tradable options | `in ["A", "B"]` | hard | - | add instrument.optionable eq true; A alone for 'tight spreads' |

**When the reading lies**

- Graded at the target expiry (30 to 45 days); a weekly or a far LEAPS series can be worse. For a specific expiry read rollup.option_liquidity@v1.put_spread_pct and call_spread_pct.
- Null when the session's chain run failed for the name (STALE_DATA, FETCH_ERROR), which is not the same as D; a hard criterion rejects it that day.

Sources: Options liquidity checklist (volume, open interest, spread, size): https://www.tastylive.com/concepts-strategies/options-liquidity; Spread as a share of the premium (a $0.20 spread on a $2 option is 10% round trip): https://www.thetaedge.ai/blog/assess-liquidity-trading-options

### `feature.option_chain_oi`

**How to read it.** Open interest summed over the chain: contracts outstanding. Thousands is a thin chain, tens of thousands is tradable, hundreds of thousands and above is an index-like chain. It measures depth for exiting a position; a strike you trade can still be empty, so it is a gate, not a guarantee.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a chain with depth | `gte 10000` | soft | 0.3 x the threshold, LIQUIDITY_RISK | 50000 for 'deep'; the site's HIGH class uses 50000, MEDIUM 5000 |

**When the reading lies**

- 0 when the session's chain run did not list the name (no options), null when the run failed; the two differ.
- Open interest piles up at a few strikes and expiries; the chain total says little about the strike you want. Check rollup.option_liquidity@v1.put_strike_oi for the target strike.

Sources: https://www.tastylive.com/concepts-strategies/options-liquidity

### `feature.option_chain_volume`

**How to read it.** Contracts traded across the chain on the session. Hundreds is quiet, a few thousand is active, tens of thousands is heavy. Volume is one day's activity, open interest the standing depth; both low means fills at the market maker's price.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| active options | `gte 1000` | soft | 0.3 x the threshold, LIQUIDITY_RISK | - |

**When the reading lies**

- An earnings week or an expiry Friday multiplies option volume; check rollup.earnings@v1.days_to_earnings before reading a spike as unusual activity.
- 0 when the run did not list the name, null when it failed.

Sources: https://www.tastylive.com/concepts-strategies/options-liquidity

## Momentum and trend

### `rollup.momentum@v1.rsi_14`

**How to read it.** Wilder's RSI over 14 sessions, 0 to 100: the share of recent movement that was up. 50 is balance; 70 and above means the last two weeks were mostly gains (overbought), 30 and below mostly losses (oversold). In a steady uptrend RSI lives between 40 and 80 and rarely visits 30; in a downtrend between 20 and 60 (Cardwell's ranges), so 'overbought' in a bull range is strength, not a top.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| oversold bounce in an uptrend | `lt 30` | soft | 5 | with feature.trend_state eq UPTREND (or feature.pct_vs_sma_200 gt 0) as the gate; alone it finds falling knives |
| momentum strength (a score, never the only gate) | `gte 50` | score | 20 | ranks the stronger names; the gate is rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_20d gt 0 |
| not chasing an overbought name | `lte 70` | soft | 5 | a filter on entries, not a short signal; in a strong uptrend expect many names above 70 |

**When the reading lies**

- A pending takeover pins the price just under the offer: RSI prints 80+ on the announcement day and then stays high for weeks with no trend behind it. Check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move (one big day) with feature.atr_pct near 0 since; see the 'pending takeover' situation.
- In a strong trend RSI stays above 70 for weeks; 'overbought' alone is not a sell signal (Cardwell). Pair an overbought rule with feature.pct_vs_sma_20 or feature.dist_to_resistance before reading it as stretched.
- RSI is 100 when the 14-session run had no down close at all, which happens on thin, low-priced names that barely trade. Gate on rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d and rollup.price_stats@v2.close first.
- Oversold in a downtrend keeps falling (a falling knife): an RSI below 30 is a bounce candidate only with feature.trend_state UPTREND or feature.pct_vs_sma_200 above 0.
- Null after a gap in the last 15 sessions or a shorter history; a hard criterion then rejects the name.

Sources: Wilder (1978), New Concepts in Technical Trading Systems: the 70 / 30 convention; Cardwell's RSI range rules (bull 40-80, bear 20-60): https://nexusfi.com/a/concepts/cardwell-rsi-framework; Relative strength index, Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_strength_index

### `rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_20d`

**How to read it.** The return over the last 20 sessions (about a month) as a fraction: 0.05 is up 5%, -0.10 is down 10%. Above 0 the month was up; beyond about 0.10 or -0.10 the move was large for a typical large cap (ATR of 1.5% a day compounds to a monthly range of that order). One-month returns tend to reverse slightly rather than continue (the short-term reversal), so this is a trend confirmation, not the academic momentum signal: that one is the 12-month return skipping the last month, which rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_60d comes nearer to.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| upward momentum (the gate) | `gt 0` | hard | - | confirm with feature.pct_vs_sma_20 gt 0; rank by ret_20d descending |
| a strong month | `gte 0.1` | soft | 0.03 | check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move is not most of it |
| a weak month (mean-reversion candidates) | `lte -0.1` | soft | 0.03 | with feature.trend_state eq UPTREND to buy a dip rather than a decline |

**When the reading lies**

- One day can be the whole month: an earnings gap or a takeover jump of 15% leaves ret_20d at 0.15 with nineteen flat sessions around it. Check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move; when it is most of ret_20d the 'momentum' is one event.
- After a large up month the next month leans slightly the other way (Jegadeesh 1990): a high ret_20d is weak evidence of continuation. Rank by it, do not gate hard on a large value.
- A null means a missing bar in the window (a halt, a listing gap) and rejects a hard criterion.

Sources: Jegadeesh (1990), Evidence of predictable behavior of security returns: the one-month reversal; Jegadeesh and Titman (1993) and the 12-1 convention: https://alphaarchitect.com/skip-month-mystery/

### `rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_60d`

**How to read it.** The return over the last 60 sessions (about a quarter) as a fraction. Positive means the quarter was up. Of the stored returns this is the closest to the academic momentum formation window (6 to 12 months, skipping the latest month), where past winners kept winning on average; it is still shorter than that evidence, so read it as 'the quarter was up', not as a proven factor.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a longer run of momentum | `gt 0` | hard | - | use instead of ret_20d when the sentence says a longer trend; rank descending |
| a strong quarter | `gte 0.2` | soft | 0.05 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- A single event (earnings, a deal, an index inclusion) can be most of a quarter's return; check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move and rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date.
- Null after any missing bar in the last 61 sessions.

Sources: Jegadeesh and Titman (1993), Returns to buying winners and selling losers; Momentum formation windows and the skip month: https://alphaarchitect.com/skip-month-mystery/

### `rollup.momentum@v1.ret_5d`

**How to read it.** The return over the last 5 sessions (a week) as a fraction. Short windows mean-revert: a week that was sharply up or down is more likely to give some back than to continue, so this is a timing field (buy the dip in an uptrend, do not chase a spike), not a momentum field.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a dip to buy inside an uptrend | `lte -0.03` | soft | 0.01 | with feature.trend_state eq UPTREND; the dip is the entry, the trend is the reason |
| not chasing a spike | `lte 0.08` | soft | 0.02 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- A week dominated by one gap day is an event, not a drift; check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move.
- Thin names swing 5% in a week on noise; gate on rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d.

Sources: Jegadeesh (1990) and Lehmann (1990): one-week to one-month contrarian profits

### `feature.pct_from_high_52w`

**How to read it.** How far the close sits under its 52-week high, as a fraction at or below 0: -0.05 is 5% below the high, 0 is at it. Nearness to the 52-week high predicts returns better than past returns do (George and Hwang 2004: investors anchor on the high and under-react to news near it), and those profits do not reverse later. Within 10% of the high is the catalogue's own 'near' band (feature.near_52w HIGH).

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| near the 52-week high (the anchoring effect) | `gte -0.1` | soft | 0.05 | or feature.near_52w eq HIGH; rank by pct_from_high_52w descending (nearest first) |
| at a new high | `gte -0.02` | soft | 0.02 | - |
| well off the high (beaten down) | `lte -0.3` | soft | 0.05 | say why it is interesting with another field; a discount alone is not a reason |

**When the reading lies**

- A pending takeover puts the close at its high and keeps it there; the anchoring story does not apply. See the 'pending takeover' situation.
- Null for a name with fewer than 240 bars in the last 252 sessions (a listing under a year old, a long halt): a hard criterion rejects it. Check instrument.listed_on.
- A name 50% below its high is 'cheap' only in price; nothing here says why it fell. Pair a deep-discount rule with feature.trend_state or rollup.financials@v1.financials_status.

Sources: George and Hwang (2004), The 52-week high and momentum investing, Journal of Finance: https://alphaarchitect.com/the-secret-to-momentum-is-the-52-week-high/; Quantpedia, 52-weeks high effect in stocks: https://quantpedia.com/strategies/52-weeks-high-effect-in-stocks

### `feature.pct_from_low_52w`

**How to read it.** How far the close sits above its 52-week low, as a fraction at or above 0: 0.10 is 10% above the low, 0 is at it. Near the low is where 'beaten down' names are; nearness to the low has weaker evidence than nearness to the high and the names there are often there for a reason.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| near the 52-week low | `lte 0.1` | soft | 0.05 | or feature.near_52w eq LOW; 0.20 for 'off the lows'; add a turn signal (ret_20d gt 0) |

**When the reading lies**

- At the low with feature.trend_state DOWNTREND the trend is down; a bounce rule needs a turn (rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_20d gt 0, feature.pct_vs_sma_20 gt 0) or it buys the decline.
- A dividend cut, a failed deal or a going-concern notice puts a name at its low; check rollup.financials@v1.financials_status and instrument.financial_status.
- Null under 240 bars in the last 252 sessions (recent listings).

Sources: George and Hwang (2004): the low has less predictive power than the high

### `feature.near_52w`

**How to read it.** Where the close sits in its 52-week range: HIGH within 10% of the high, LOW within 10% of the low, BOTH when the whole range is under about 20% (a quiet name: a utility, a bond fund, a pinned takeover target), NONE in between.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| near the high | `eq "HIGH"` | hard | - | - |
| near the low | `eq "LOW"` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- BOTH is a narrow range, not 'near both extremes': it marks low-volatility names and pinned takeover targets. Exclude it from momentum screens.
- Null when the 52-week high or low is unknown (under 240 bars).

Sources: George and Hwang (2004) for HIGH; BOTH is a site convention

### `feature.pct_vs_sma_20`

**How to read it.** Close over its 20-session average minus 1: 0.03 is 3% above the one-month average, negative is below. Above 0 the short-term trend is up. Beyond about 0.08 to 0.10 above, a large cap is stretched for a month and tends to come back to the average; a pullback to it is the 'buy the dip' entry (feature.pullback_to_sma20 measures that in ATRs).

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| short-term trend up (confirmation) | `gt 0` | soft | 0.02 | the confirmation of rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_20d gt 0 |
| not stretched above the month's average | `lte 0.08` | soft | 0.02 | for entries; a breakout screen wants the opposite |

**When the reading lies**

- A gap day (earnings, a deal) jumps the close far above the average in one session: a 'stretched' reading right after a gap is the event, not an overextension. Check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move.
- Leveraged and inverse funds reset daily and drift from their index; moving-average readings describe the fund's path, not the market's. Exclude with instrument.is_leveraged eq false.
- Null after a gap in the last 20 sessions.

Sources: Brock, Lakonishok and LeBaron (1992), Simple technical trading rules: buy signals after a close above the average earned more than sell signals

### `feature.pct_vs_sma_50`

**How to read it.** Close over its 50-session average minus 1: the intermediate trend. Above 0 the name trades above its roughly quarterly average; the 50-day average was the best performing single rule in Brock, Lakonishok and LeBaron's long Dow test. More than about 0.15 above is stretched for a large cap.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| above the 50-day (a loose uptrend) | `gt 0` | soft | 0.02 | - |
| not stretched above the 50-day | `lte 0.15` | soft | 0.03 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Gaps and takeovers as for the 20-day average: one event, not a trend. Check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move.
- Leveraged and inverse funds: exclude with instrument.is_leveraged eq false.
- Null after a gap in the last 50 sessions or a shorter history.

Sources: Brock, Lakonishok and LeBaron (1992): https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v47y1992i5p1731-64.html

### `feature.pct_vs_sma_200`

**How to read it.** Close over its 200-session average minus 1: the long-term trend, the daily version of Faber's 10-month moving average rule. Above 0 is the classic bull-market condition: returns after a close above the long average were higher and calmer than after a close below it. More than about 0.25 above the 200-day is far from its long average and historically gave some back; more than 0.25 below is a name in a long decline.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| long-term uptrend (the bull-market filter) | `gt 0` | hard | - | or feature.trend_state eq UPTREND for the stricter close > SMA50 > SMA200 |
| not far above the long average | `lte 0.25` | soft | 0.05 | - |
| in a long decline (contrarian or avoid) | `lte -0.25` | soft | 0.05 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- The rule's edge was found on indices and diversified portfolios; on a single stock a close above the 200-day is a trend filter, not a return forecast.
- Null for names with under 200 sessions of history (listings under ten months old) or a gap in the window; a hard criterion rejects them. Check instrument.listed_on.
- Leveraged and inverse funds: exclude with instrument.is_leveraged eq false and instrument.is_inverse eq false.

Sources: Faber (2007), A quantitative approach to tactical asset allocation (the 10-month SMA): https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=1115; Brock, Lakonishok and LeBaron (1992)

### `feature.trend_state`

**How to read it.** UPTREND when close > SMA50 > SMA200 (price above a rising structure), DOWNTREND when close < SMA50 < SMA200, MIXED otherwise. MIXED is a large bucket: a pullback below the 50-day inside an uptrend, a bounce inside a downtrend, and every crossover in progress.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| in an uptrend | `eq "UPTREND"` | hard | - | do not combine with 'near the 52-week low'; those rarely coexist |
| in a downtrend | `eq "DOWNTREND"` | hard | - | - |

**When the reading lies**

- A name can be UPTREND the day after a 30% takeover jump and stay so for months without moving again; see the 'pending takeover' situation (feature.atr_pct near 0).
- Null under 200 sessions of history; a hard UPTREND criterion silently drops every recent listing. Check instrument.listed_on when that matters.
- Leveraged and inverse funds: exclude with instrument.is_leveraged eq false.

Sources: Faber (2007) and Brock, Lakonishok and LeBaron (1992) for the moving-average evidence; the alignment rule is a practitioner convention

### `feature.pullback_to_sma20`

**How to read it.** True when the name is in an uptrend (feature.trend_state UPTREND) and the close is within one ATR of its 20-session average, above or below it: the dip-in-an-uptrend entry. The ATR scales 'near' to the name's own volatility, so a 1% move on a utility and a 4% move on a chip maker both count.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| buy the dip in an uptrend | `eq true` | hard | - | with feature.atr_pct gte 0.01 and rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d as gates |

**When the reading lies**

- A pinned takeover target converges onto its average with a tiny ATR, so 'within one ATR' becomes trivially true in an UPTREND: a false pullback. Require feature.atr_pct gte 0.01.
- True on the day of a sharp drop through the average as much as on a gentle dip; add rollup.momentum@v1.ret_5d gte -0.06 to keep out breakdowns.
- Null when the trend, the average or the ATR is unknown.

Sources: Practitioner convention (pullback-to-moving-average entries); the Turtle rules' ATR-scaled distances

### `feature.breakout_20d`

**How to read it.** True when the close is above the highest high of the previous 20 sessions and the day's volume is at least 1.5 times its 20-session average: a Donchian-style breakout with volume confirmation (1.5 to 2 times average is the usual bar, above 2 is strong participation). Breakouts fail often in ranging markets (Raschke and Connors built the 'Turtle Soup' strategy on fading them), so volume and a trend filter matter.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| breaking out on volume | `eq true` | hard | - | with feature.trend_state eq UPTREND or feature.pct_vs_sma_200 gt 0 as the trend filter |

**When the reading lies**

- The announcement day of a takeover is a textbook breakout on volume, and the last move the name will make. Check feature.atr_pct in the sessions after; see the 'pending takeover' situation.
- An earnings gap is a breakout on volume too; whether it continues is the post-earnings drift question, not a technical one. Check rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date.
- Index events (quarterly expiry Fridays, the Russell reconstitution in late June, S&P changes) put huge volume on a flat close; the price condition still has to hold, but treat a breakout on such a day with suspicion.
- Null when the prior high or the volume ratio is unknown (a gap in the last 21 sessions).

Sources: Volume confirmation of breakouts (1.5x to 2x the 20-day average): https://www.luxalgo.com/blog/how-volume-confirms-breakouts-in-trading/; Turtle rules (20-day Donchian entries) and their failure rate; Raschke and Connors, Street Smarts (Turtle Soup)

### `rollup.momentum@v1.rel_volume`

**How to read it.** The session's volume over the average of the 20 sessions before it: 1 is normal, 2 is twice normal, 0.5 is a quiet day. Above 1.5 is the usual confirmation that a move has participation behind it; above 2 is strong; above 5 is an event. Below 0.7 on an up day is a move nobody joined.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| heavy volume today | `gte 1.5` | soft | 0.3 | - |
| an unusual volume event | `gte 3` | soft | 0.5 | check rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date; most 3x days are earnings or index events |

**When the reading lies**

- Quarterly expiry Fridays, the Russell reconstitution (late June) and index inclusions multiply volume without any news; so do secondary offerings and block trades. High volume with a flat close (rollup.momentum@v1.ret_5d near 0) is usually one of these.
- Earnings days are 3 to 10 times normal volume by nature; check rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date before reading a spike as unusual.
- On thin names a single institutional order is a 5x day; gate on rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d.
- The base is the 20 sessions before today; after a very heavy week the base is inflated and today's ratio understates activity.

Sources: Volume confirmation thresholds: https://www.luxalgo.com/blog/how-volume-confirms-breakouts-in-trading/

### `rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move`

**How to read it.** The largest single-day close-to-close move, up or down, in the last 20 sessions, as a fraction: 0.12 is a 12% day. Below about 0.04 the month had no shock; 0.08 and above is a news day for a large cap (earnings, guidance, a deal, an FDA decision). After earnings a large move tends to continue for days to weeks (the post-earnings announcement drift); a large move with no earnings behind it more often partly reverses.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no shock in the last month (a calm name) | `lte 0.05` | soft | 0.02 | a filter for premium-selling and pullback screens |
| had a big one-day move | `gte 0.08` | soft | 0.02 | check rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date to know whether it was earnings |

**When the reading lies**

- It says a move happened, not why or which way: pair with rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date (an earnings move) and the sign of rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_20d.
- A move of 0.20 or more followed by a dead-flat tape is the takeover signature; see the 'pending takeover' situation.
- Low-priced and thin names post 10% days on noise; gate on rollup.price_stats@v2.close and rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d.
- The window is 20 sessions: a shock 21 sessions ago has dropped out of this field but still sits inside rollup.price_stats@v2.hv30.

Sources: Ball and Brown (1968); Bernard and Thomas (1989): the post-earnings announcement drift; Short-term reversal after non-news moves: Jegadeesh (1990)

### `feature.dist_to_resistance`

**How to read it.** How far the nearest confirmed swing high above the close is, as a fraction of price: 0.05 is 5% of room before the last place sellers showed up. Under about 0.02 the name is at resistance; above about 0.10 there is room to run. A swing high is confirmed five sessions after it forms, so the level lags.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| room to run | `gte 0.1` | soft | 0.03 | soft, so a name at a new high (null) is listed rather than rejected; or use pct_from_high_52w |
| near resistance (a sell-call or fade level) | `lte 0.03` | soft | 0.01 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- Null at a one-year high (no swing high above the close): a hard 'room to run' criterion rejects exactly the names breaking out. Use feature.pct_from_high_52w gte -0.02 for those.
- The nearest swing high can be a minor one; compare feature.dist_to_resistance_atr: under 1 ATR away the level is in today's noise.
- A level from eleven months ago has less meaning than one from last month; rollup.swing_levels@v1.swing_high_date says when it formed.

Sources: Practitioner convention (swing-point support and resistance)

### `feature.dist_to_support`

**How to read it.** How far the nearest confirmed swing low below the close is, as a fraction of price: 0.03 is 3% above the last place buyers showed up. Under about 0.03 the name is at support (a stop goes just below it); above about 0.15 a stop at support is a wide one.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| near support | `lte 0.03` | soft | 0.01 | with feature.trend_state eq UPTREND; support in a downtrend breaks |

**When the reading lies**

- Null at a one-year low (no swing low below): a 'near support' criterion then rejects the names that just broke support, which may be what you want.
- A level more than a few ATRs away is not 'support' for a swing trade; feature.dist_to_support_atr under 2 is the usual working range.

Sources: Practitioner convention; ATR-scaled stops (Turtle rules)

## Volatility

### `feature.atr_pct`

**How to read it.** Wilder's 14-day average true range as a share of price: the typical daily range. Under 0.015 is a calm large cap or a fund; 0.015 to 0.025 is a normal stock; 0.025 to 0.035 is a lively swing-trading range; 0.035 to 0.05 is volatile; above 0.05 is very volatile (small caps, biotech, crypto-linked names). A stop or a target is usually set in multiples of it (2 ATR is the Turtle stop).

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| moves enough to trade (swing range) | `gte 0.02` | soft | 0.005 | - |
| volatile | `gte 0.03` | soft | 0.005 | - |
| calm (premium selling, income) | `lte 0.02` | soft | 0.005 | with feature.atr_pct gte 0.005 so pinned takeover targets do not pass as calm |

**When the reading lies**

- Near 0 for weeks after a takeover announcement: the price is pinned. Under 0.005 on a stock that is not a bond fund is that signature; see the 'pending takeover' situation.
- One gap day raises the 14-day ATR for two weeks; a 'volatile' reading right after earnings is the event. Check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move and rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date.
- Low-priced names show a high ratio partly because the tick is a large share of the price; gate on rollup.price_stats@v2.close.
- Leveraged funds run at two or three times their index's range by construction; exclude with instrument.is_leveraged eq false unless that is the point.

Sources: ATR-percent bands used by swing traders (under 2.5% stable, 2.5 to 3.5% normal, 3.5 to 4.5% volatile, over 5% very volatile): https://oneoption.com/?p=59457; Wilder (1978) for the ATR; the Turtle rules for ATR-sized stops

### `feature.range_20d_pct`

**How to read it.** The height of the last 20 sessions' high-low channel as a share of price: 0.08 means the month's range was 8% of the price. Under about 0.06 for a stock is a tight consolidation (the volatility contraction that precedes many breakouts); above 0.25 is a wide, choppy month.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| tight consolidation (a breakout setup) | `lte 0.08` | soft | 0.02 | with feature.trend_state eq UPTREND and feature.atr_pct gte 0.01 |

**When the reading lies**

- A pinned takeover target has the tightest range on the board; a tight range with feature.atr_pct under 0.005 is a deal, not a setup.
- A tight range that includes a gap is two ranges; check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move.

Sources: Volatility contraction before breakouts (Minervini's VCP; Bollinger's squeeze) as practitioner conventions

### `rollup.price_stats@v2.hv20`

**How to read it.** Realised (historical) volatility over 20 sessions, annualised, as a fraction: 0.20 is 20% a year, the long-run average for a large-cap index is about 0.15 to 0.20, a typical single stock 0.25 to 0.40, a biotech or a meme name above 0.60. It is the denominator of every 'IV over HV' comparison (the 30-session version, hv30, is the one feature.iv_hv_ratio uses).

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a volatile name (realised) | `gte 0.4` | soft | 0.05 | - |
| a calm name (realised) | `lte 0.25` | soft | 0.05 | with rollup.price_stats@v2.hv20 gte 0.08 so pinned names do not pass |

**When the reading lies**

- Close-to-close volatility is dominated by one gap: a 15% earnings day alone puts hv20 near 0.50 for the next 20 sessions, then it drops out overnight. rollup.price_stats@v2.hv20_yz (Yang-Zhang, which weighs the overnight gap and the intraday range separately) is steadier; check rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date.
- Very low readings (under 0.10 on a stock) are a pinned takeover or a halted name, not a calm company.
- Null after any missing bar in the window.

Sources: Yang and Zhang (2000); why one gap day distorts close-to-close estimates: https://flashalpha.com/articles/yang-zhang-vs-close-to-close-realized-volatility

### `rollup.iv_history@v2.iv30`

**How to read it.** Our 30-day at-the-money implied volatility as a fraction: 0.30 is 30% a year. It is the market's price of the next month's movement. Levels are relative to the name: 0.20 is high for a utility and low for a chip maker, so compare it with the name's own history (feature.iv_rank, feature.iv_percentile) and with realised movement (feature.iv_hv_spread) rather than against one number. Options are 'expensive' when IV is high versus both.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| high implied volatility (absolute) | `gte 0.4` | soft | 0.05 | prefer feature.iv_rank gte 0.5 (relative to the name's own year) |
| low implied volatility (absolute) | `lte 0.25` | soft | 0.05 | with feature.atr_pct gte 0.005; prefer feature.iv_rank lte 0.3 |

**When the reading lies**

- A report inside the next 30 days inflates it: the earnings move is priced in and IV collapses right after the report (20% to 60% of it in a session). Check rollup.earnings@v1.days_to_earnings before calling options expensive.
- High IV with no earnings ahead usually means a known binary event the catalogue does not store (an FDA decision, a court ruling, a deal vote). It is not a free premium; treat it as EVENT_RISK.
- IV collapses after a takeover announcement (the price will not move): a very low iv30 with feature.atr_pct near 0 is a deal, not cheap options.
- Null when the session's chain gave no usable ATM quotes (rollup.iv30@v1.iv30_status says why).

Sources: IV behaviour around earnings: https://optionalpha.com/learn/iv-crush and https://flashalpha.com/articles/complete-guide-trading-earnings-volatility

### `feature.iv_rank`

**How to read it.** Where today's IV30 sits between the lowest and highest IV30 of the last 252 sessions, 0 to 1: 0.5 is halfway up the year's range, 0.8 is near the year's high. The premium-selling convention is above 0.5 (IV is high for this name), premium buying below 0.3. The source is IBKR's history where it has one, else ours (feature.iv_rank_source says which).

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| options expensive for this name (sell premium) | `gte 0.5` | soft | 0.1 | add rollup.earnings@v1.days_to_earnings gt 10 (soft, EVENT_RISK) and feature.iv_hv_spread gte 0.05 |
| options cheap for this name (buy premium) | `lte 0.3` | soft | 0.1 | with feature.atr_pct gte 0.01 so pinned names are out |

**When the reading lies**

- One spike (a market crash, one wild earnings) sets the year's maximum and squashes every later reading toward 0 for 252 sessions: a rank of 0.3 can be a normal IV after an abnormal year. feature.iv_percentile (the share of days below today) is not distorted by a single day; when the two disagree, trust the percentile.
- A report in the next ten sessions lifts the rank by itself; a high rank with rollup.earnings@v1.days_to_earnings under 10 is the earnings premium, which disappears after the report.
- After a takeover announcement IV collapses and the rank goes to 0 and stays there: 'cheap options' on a name that will not move. Check feature.atr_pct.
- Null under 60 sessions of IV history (new listings, newly optionable names) and when every IV in the window is equal; a hard criterion rejects them.

Sources: IV rank versus IV percentile, and the 50 / 30 convention: https://flashalpha.com/articles/iv-rank-vs-iv-percentile-difference-which-matters; tastylive on IV rank for premium selling

### `feature.iv_percentile`

**How to read it.** The share of the last 252 sessions whose IV30 was below today's, 0 to 1: 0.9 means IV was lower on 90% of days this year. It answers the same question as feature.iv_rank but counts days instead of measuring the span, so one extreme day cannot squash it; it is the more robust of the two for premium selling.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IV high for the name (robust) | `gte 0.7` | soft | 0.1 | - |
| IV low for the name (robust) | `lte 0.3` | soft | 0.1 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- The earnings and takeover caveats of feature.iv_rank apply unchanged.
- A percentile near 1 after a long calm stretch can be a modest IV (every earlier day was lower, but not by much); read the level too (rollup.iv_history@v2.iv30).

Sources: https://flashalpha.com/articles/iv-rank-vs-iv-percentile-difference-which-matters

### `feature.iv_hv_spread`

**How to read it.** IV30 minus HV30, in volatility points as a fraction: 0.05 means options price 5 points more movement than the stock delivered last month. Across markets implied volatility exceeds realised by about 3 to 4 points on average (the variance risk premium, Carr and Wu 2009), so a spread near 0.04 is just the normal insurance premium; 0.08 and above is a rich one, and a negative spread means the stock moved more than its options expected.

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a volatility premium worth selling | `gte 0.05` | soft | 0.02 | with feature.iv_hv_ratio gte 1.2 (both: a spread of 0.05 on a 0.10 HV is a ratio of 1.5, on a 0.60 HV it is noise) |
| options cheaper than the stock's movement (buy premium) | `lte 0` | soft | 0.02 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- A report inside 30 days inflates IV and the spread with it; the premium is the earnings move, not free. Check rollup.earnings@v1.days_to_earnings.
- A quiet month depresses HV30 and widens the spread; after a report drops out of the HV window the spread jumps with no change in IV. Check rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move and rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date.
- A report inside the HV window (the last 30 sessions) inflates HV and hides a real premium: the spread looks negative while the forward month is calm.
- Null without an IV for the session or with a gap in the last 31 bars.

Sources: Carr and Wu (2009), Variance risk premiums, Review of Financial Studies: implied exceeds realised by 3 to 4 points on average; IV minus HV as a premium-selling signal: https://www.optionsanalysissuite.com/screeners/highest-vrp

### `feature.iv_hv_ratio`

**How to read it.** IV30 over HV30: 1 means options price exactly last month's movement, 1.2 means 20% more, 0.8 means less. Around 1.1 to 1.2 is the normal premium; 1.5 and above is rich; under 1 the market expects calmer times than the last month delivered (often right after a shock).

**The criterion per intent**

| Intent | Criterion | Mode | Near-miss band | How to combine it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a volatility premium worth selling | `gte 1.2` | soft | 0.1 | with feature.iv_hv_spread gte 0.05 and rollup.price_stats@v2.hv30 gte 0.10 |
| options cheaper than realised (buy premium) | `lte 0.9` | soft | 0.1 | - |

**When the reading lies**

- The ratio explodes when HV30 is tiny: a pinned takeover target with HV 0.03 and IV 0.10 shows 3.3, the richest 'premium' on the board and the worst trade. Require rollup.price_stats@v2.hv30 gte 0.10 or feature.atr_pct gte 0.01, and read feature.iv_hv_spread alongside.
- A report inside the HV window compresses the ratio (HV inflated), a report ahead inflates it (IV inflated); check rollup.earnings@v1.days_to_earnings and last_earnings_date.
- The VRP scanner's version, feature.vrp_iv_hv_ratio, uses the lower of IBKR's and Cboe's IV30 (a conservative premium); this one uses our Cboe-derived IV30 only.

Sources: Carr and Wu (2009); practitioner IV/HV screens: https://www.optionsanalysissuite.com/documentation/iv-hv-history

## Situations that fool several thresholds

### Pending takeover

**Signs.** One day up 15% to 40% (rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move), then a dead tape: feature.atr_pct under 0.005, rollup.price_stats@v2.hv20 under 0.10, feature.range_20d_pct tiny, rollup.iv_history@v2.iv30 collapsed and feature.iv_rank near 0, the close a few percent under a round number (the offer) for weeks. The merger-arbitrage spread of 2% to 3% that then shrinks toward 0 is all the price will do until the deal closes or breaks.

**Affects.** `rollup.momentum@v1.rsi_14`, `rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_20d`, `rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_60d`, `feature.pct_from_high_52w`, `feature.near_52w`, `feature.trend_state`, `feature.breakout_20d`, `feature.pullback_to_sma20`, `feature.iv_rank`, `feature.iv_percentile`, `feature.iv_hv_ratio`, `feature.atr_pct`, `feature.range_20d_pct`, `rollup.price_stats@v2.hv20`

**What to do.** The catalogue has no deal flag yet (a planned feature). Until then every momentum, breakout, calm-name and cheap-options screen should carry feature.atr_pct gte 0.005 (soft) and treat a name whose one_day_move is most of its ret_20d as an event, not a trend.

### Earnings gap inside the window

**Signs.** rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date within the last 20 to 30 sessions and rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move of 0.05 or more.

**Affects.** `rollup.price_stats@v2.hv20`, `rollup.price_stats@v2.hv30`, `feature.iv_hv_spread`, `feature.iv_hv_ratio`, `rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_20d`, `rollup.momentum@v1.rel_volume`, `feature.breakout_20d`, `feature.atr_pct`, `rollup.momentum@v1.rsi_14`

**What to do.** Realised volatility and the ATR are inflated by one day for the length of their window, so IV-over-HV premiums look thin and 'volatile' flags fire. After earnings a large move tends to continue (the post-earnings drift), which makes a post-earnings breakout more trustworthy than one on no news; a large move with no report behind it more often partly reverses. Prefer rollup.price_stats@v2.hv20_yz when comparing volatility across names.

### Recent listing, spin-off or long halt

**Signs.** instrument.listed_on or instrument.first_seen within the last year; rollup.price_stats@v2.history_days under 240; rollup.iv_history@v2.history_days under 60.

**Affects.** `feature.pct_from_high_52w`, `feature.pct_from_low_52w`, `feature.near_52w`, `feature.pct_vs_sma_200`, `feature.trend_state`, `feature.iv_rank`, `feature.iv_percentile`, `feature.revenue_growth_yoy`, `feature.pe_ratio`

**What to do.** These fields are null, and a null never passes a hard criterion (missing data never passes), so a screen with hard long-window criteria silently excludes every new name. Use soft or score for those criteria when new listings are wanted, or state instrument.listed_on explicitly.

### Leveraged or inverse fund

**Signs.** instrument.is_leveraged eq true or instrument.is_inverse eq true (instrument.leverage gives the multiple).

**Affects.** `feature.pct_vs_sma_20`, `feature.pct_vs_sma_50`, `feature.pct_vs_sma_200`, `feature.trend_state`, `rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_20d`, `rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_60d`, `feature.atr_pct`, `rollup.price_stats@v2.hv20`, `feature.pct_from_high_52w`, `feature.pct_from_low_52w`

**What to do.** Daily resetting funds drift from their index (volatility decay), so trend and momentum readings describe the fund's path, not the market, and an inverse fund in a 'downtrend' is a rising market. Exclude them from stock screens with instrument.is_leveraged eq false and instrument.is_inverse eq false; screen them deliberately or not at all.

### Low-priced or thin name

**Signs.** rollup.price_stats@v2.close under 5 or rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d under 1000000.

**Affects.** `feature.atr_pct`, `rollup.momentum@v1.rsi_14`, `rollup.momentum@v1.rel_volume`, `rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move`, `feature.breakout_20d`, `rollup.price_stats@v2.hv20`

**What to do.** Every volatility and momentum reading is inflated by the tick size, wide spreads and single orders: a 10% day, a 5x volume day and an RSI of 100 are noise here. Put a price floor and a dollar-volume gate first in every screen; the drafting examples do.

### Index and expiry days

**Signs.** A quarterly expiry Friday (third Friday of March, June, September, December), the Russell reconstitution (the last Friday of June), an S&P index change, a large ETF rebalance.

**Affects.** `rollup.momentum@v1.rel_volume`, `feature.breakout_20d`, `feature.option_chain_volume`

**What to do.** Volume is several times normal across the market with no information in it; a volume rule fires everywhere. Read a breakout or an unusual-volume screen from such a session with that in mind, or require the price condition on its own (the close above the prior high, rollup.momentum@v1.ret_5d) before trusting the volume.

### A null is not a value

**Signs.** The field is often null by construction: feature.pe_ratio (loss makers, ADRs), feature.iv_rank (under 60 sessions of IV), the 52-week fields (under 240 bars), rollup.earnings@v1.days_to_earnings (no announced date, every ETF), feature.option_tier (a failed chain run).

**Affects.** `feature.pe_ratio`, `feature.revenue_growth_yoy`, `feature.iv_rank`, `feature.iv_percentile`, `feature.pct_from_high_52w`, `feature.pct_from_low_52w`, `rollup.earnings@v1.days_to_earnings`, `feature.earnings_before_expiry`, `feature.option_tier`, `feature.market_cap`

**What to do.** A null never passes a hard criterion and a soft one lists it as a near miss; so a hard 'pe_ratio lt 15' drops every unprofitable company and 'days_to_earnings gt 10' drops every ETF. Choose hard only when excluding the unknown is the intent; otherwise soft (listed with the reason) or score (ranked lower). 'not_null' is the explicit way to require the fact.
