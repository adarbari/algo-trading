# Field guide: how to read the catalogue's fields

Generated by `make features-doc` from `config/site/field_guide/` (one file per theme); edit those, not this page.
The same guide is shown by the Builder with each field and sent to the text model that drafts
a screen from a sentence (ADR 0041), so a threshold a person reads here is the threshold the
model proposes.

Every field is read the same way: **how to read it** (what low and high mean, in the field's
unit: a decimal field takes 0.10 for 10%), **the criterion per intent** (what a trader wants,
and the rule-screen criterion that expresses it: the operator, the value, the mode and the
near-miss band of [rules.md](../screeners/rules.md); `hard` rejects a miss, `soft` keeps a
near miss as a WATCH or a risk, `score` only ranks), and **when the reading lies**: the
situations in which the number is right and the conclusion is wrong, each with the field
that exposes it. The fields are on one page per theme (below). The
[situations](#situations-that-fool-several-thresholds) at the end of this page are the
ones that distort many fields at once; a screen that ignores them finds the same false
positives whatever its thresholds. A null never passes a `hard` criterion (missing data never
passes); a field's own catalogue entry ([features.md](features.md)) says when it is null.

## Situations that fool several thresholds

### Pending takeover

**Signs.** One day up 15% to 40% (rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move), then a dead tape: feature.atr_pct under 0.005, rollup.price_stats@v2.hv20 under 0.10, feature.range_20d_pct tiny, rollup.iv_history@v2.iv30 collapsed and feature.iv_rank near 0, the close a few percent under a round number (the offer) for weeks. The merger-arbitrage spread of 2% to 3% that then shrinks toward 0 is all the price will do until the deal closes or breaks.

**Affects.** `rollup.momentum@v1.rsi_14`, `rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_20d`, `rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_60d`, `feature.pct_from_high_52w`, `feature.near_52w`, `feature.trend_state`, `feature.breakout_20d`, `feature.pullback_to_sma20`, `feature.iv_rank`, `feature.iv_percentile`, `feature.iv_hv_ratio`, `feature.atr_pct`, `feature.range_20d_pct`, `rollup.price_stats@v2.hv20`

**What to do.** The catalogue has no deal flag yet (a planned feature). Until then every momentum, breakout, calm-name and cheap-options screen should carry feature.atr_pct gte 0.005 (soft) and treat a name whose one_day_move is most of its ret_20d as an event, not a trend.

### Earnings gap inside the window

**Signs.** rollup.earnings@v1.last_earnings_date within the last 20 to 30 sessions and rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move of 0.05 or more.

**Affects.** `rollup.price_stats@v2.hv20`, `rollup.price_stats@v2.hv30`, `feature.iv_hv_spread`, `feature.iv_hv_ratio`, `rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_20d`, `rollup.momentum@v1.rel_volume`, `feature.breakout_20d`, `feature.atr_pct`, `rollup.momentum@v1.rsi_14`

**What to do.** Realised volatility and the ATR are inflated by one day for the length of their window, so IV-over-HV premiums look thin and 'volatile' flags fire. After earnings a large move tends to continue (the post-earnings drift), which makes a post-earnings breakout more trustworthy than one on no news; a large move with no report behind it more often partly reverses. Prefer rollup.price_stats@v2.hv20_yz when comparing volatility across names.

### Recent listing, spin-off or long halt

**Signs.** instrument.listed_on or instrument.first_seen within the last year; rollup.price_stats@v2.history_days under 240; rollup.iv_history@v2.history_days under 60.

**Affects.** `feature.pct_from_high_52w`, `feature.pct_from_low_52w`, `feature.near_52w`, `feature.pct_vs_sma_200`, `feature.trend_state`, `feature.iv_rank`, `feature.iv_percentile`, `feature.revenue_growth_yoy`, `feature.pe_ratio`

**What to do.** These fields are null, and a null never passes a hard criterion (missing data never passes), so a screen with hard long-window criteria silently excludes every new name. Use soft or score for those criteria when new listings are wanted, or state instrument.listed_on explicitly.

### Leveraged or inverse fund

**Signs.** instrument.is_leveraged eq true or instrument.is_inverse eq true (instrument.leverage gives the multiple).

**Affects.** `feature.pct_vs_sma_20`, `feature.pct_vs_sma_50`, `feature.pct_vs_sma_200`, `feature.trend_state`, `rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_20d`, `rollup.price_stats@v2.ret_60d`, `feature.atr_pct`, `rollup.price_stats@v2.hv20`, `feature.pct_from_high_52w`, `feature.pct_from_low_52w`

**What to do.** Daily resetting funds drift from their index (volatility decay), so trend and momentum readings describe the fund's path, not the market, and an inverse fund in a 'downtrend' is a rising market. Exclude them from stock screens with instrument.is_leveraged eq false and instrument.is_inverse eq false; screen them deliberately or not at all.

### Low-priced or thin name

**Signs.** rollup.price_stats@v2.close under 5 or rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d under 1000000.

**Affects.** `feature.atr_pct`, `rollup.momentum@v1.rsi_14`, `rollup.momentum@v1.rel_volume`, `rollup.price_moves@v1.one_day_move`, `feature.breakout_20d`, `rollup.price_stats@v2.hv20`

**What to do.** Every volatility and momentum reading is inflated by the tick size, wide spreads and single orders: a 10% day, a 5x volume day and an RSI of 100 are noise here. Put a price floor and a dollar-volume gate first in every screen; the drafting examples do.

### Index and expiry days

**Signs.** A quarterly expiry Friday (third Friday of March, June, September, December), the Russell reconstitution (the last Friday of June), an S&P index change, a large ETF rebalance.

**Affects.** `rollup.momentum@v1.rel_volume`, `feature.breakout_20d`, `feature.option_chain_volume`

**What to do.** Volume is several times normal across the market with no information in it; a volume rule fires everywhere. Read a breakout or an unusual-volume screen from such a session with that in mind, or require the price condition on its own (the close above the prior high, rollup.momentum@v1.ret_5d) before trusting the volume.

### A null is not a value

**Signs.** The field is often null by construction: feature.pe_ratio (loss makers, ADRs), feature.iv_rank (under 60 sessions of IV), the 52-week fields (under 240 bars), rollup.earnings@v1.days_to_earnings (no announced date, every ETF), feature.option_tier (a failed chain run).

**Affects.** `feature.pe_ratio`, `feature.revenue_growth_yoy`, `feature.iv_rank`, `feature.iv_percentile`, `feature.pct_from_high_52w`, `feature.pct_from_low_52w`, `rollup.earnings@v1.days_to_earnings`, `feature.earnings_before_expiry`, `feature.option_tier`, `feature.market_cap`

**What to do.** A null never passes a hard criterion and a soft one lists it as a near miss; so a hard 'pe_ratio lt 15' drops every unprofitable company and 'days_to_earnings gt 10' drops every ETF. Choose hard only when excluding the unknown is the intent; otherwise soft (listed with the reason) or score (ranked lower). 'not_null' is the explicit way to require the fact.
