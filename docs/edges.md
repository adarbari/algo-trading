## Earnings-date advances (`earnings_date_shift`)

**Status:** candidate. **Family:** B. **Thesis:** Stocks whose next earnings date is scheduled a week or more earlier than last year's go on to outperform the market in the following month.

- **Outcome:** excess return, over 20 sessions, vs SPY, starting +1 sessions from the event, costs 20 bps
- **Schedule:** on each `earnings_date_shift:earlier` (a next report date a week or more away from last year's (earnings_date_shift@v1); anchor: the session the shifted date was first stored; the next report date is a week or more earlier than last year's)
- **Universe:** `instrument.security_type eq 'COMMON_STOCK'` and `instrument.status eq 'ACTIVE'` and `rollup.price_stats@v2.close gt 5` and `rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d gte 5000000`
- **Top K:** 20
- **Screeners:** `earnings_date_early`
- **Baselines:** `momentum_12_1`, `size_small`
- **Frozen period:** from 2026-04-01
- **Notes:** Long side only: the advancers. The delayers (shorting later-than-last-year dates) are the other half of Johnson and So's spread and need their own screener and a short outcome, so they are a follow-up edge, not this one. The date is the Nasdaq calendar's SCHEDULED one: the calendar never confirms a date, so the "confirmed date" of the paper is a scheduled date here, and a date can move again. The event is the session the shifted date was first stored in a calendar known by the session (earnings_date_shift@v1, 7-day threshold, expected date = last year's report plus 364 days). The history starts at the first stored calendar snapshot (a date first stored at or before it says nothing about when it became known), so the events begin the session after it: the harness's independent-session count, reported beside every number, is small until the calendar has been stored for a few quarters. The hold is 20 sessions from the first close after the date became known, not through the report itself (the report can fall later than 20 sessions). Liquid base: common stock, active, close above $5, 20-day dollar volume of at least $5M (the capacity floor of verdict.toml).

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** Managers schedule good news early and need extra days for bad news (audit, restatement risk, guidance negotiation); the date notice is public weeks ahead but investors read it as a calendar item, not as earnings information (Johnson and So 2018).
2. **Persistence:** A small, unglamorous signal with a few weeks of life each quarter: it needs an expected-date model (vendors sell one) and a one-month trade across thousands of names, so large funds cannot size it; it costs one round trip a quarter per name and liquid names qualify.
3. **Outcome:** Excess return over SPY over the 20 sessions after the entry close (the session after the shifted date first became known), net of 20 bps a round trip (an assumption for names trading $5M a day or more).
4. **Trigger timing:** The event is the session the shifted date was first stored (known_from, the first calendar snapshot carrying it), the anchor; the decision session is D = anchor and the entry session S = anchor + 1 (start_offset_sessions = 1). The names are read at D from earnings_date_shift@v1 (sessions_since_earnings_shift = 0, direction earlier), never from a date stored after D.
5. **Faithful replication:** The source's rule first: advancers (a date earlier than the expected one, the same weekday as last year's report) against delayers from the day the date is known, held through the announcement (Johnson and So 2018). Here earnings_date_early v1 is the long leg only: the earlier-shifted names of the liquid base, the 20 with the largest advance.
6. **Expected size and sample:** About 260 bp between advancers and delayers in the month after the revision (Johnson and So 2018, from the WSH and MIT summaries); the long leg alone is roughly half. A few hundred advancers a quarter, in clusters at the quarter's reporting calendar: independent sessions are the binding count.
7. **Capacity and costs:** Small: a one-month trade with one round trip a quarter per name, liquid names only ($5M a day), so an individual can trade it; the 20 bps round trip is the assumption and shorting is not needed.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** A calendar that moves a date for a reason that is not news (a fiscal-year change, a merger, a vendor's re-estimate), a scheduled date that is not confirmed, and the paper's sensitivity to how the expected date is defined (Skinner 2021). Retire if the frozen-period excess return of the picks is not above the base rate of every eligible name.
9. **Decoys:** Advancers may simply be past winners: the 12-1 momentum baseline; small names shift dates more often: the size baseline. Overlaps momentum at earnings only through holding over the announcement.

**Sources**

- Johnson, T. and So, E., Time Will Tell: Information in the Timing of Scheduled Earnings News, Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis, 2018 <https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022109018000492>
- Skinner, D., Information in Mandatory and Voluntary Earnings Announcement Date Forecasts, ABFER annual conference slides, 2021 <https://www.abfer.org/media/abfer-events-2021/annual-conference/slides-accounting/Information-in-Mandatory-and-Voluntary-Earnings-Announcement-Date-Forecasts_Douglas-Skinner.pdf>
- deHaan, E., Shevlin, T. and Thornock, J., Journal of Accounting and Economics, 2015 (advance-notice length and bad news)

## Earnings gap on heavy volume (`earnings_gap_volume_drift`)

**Status:** candidate. **Family:** B. **Thesis:** A stock that jumps at least 10% over the market on its earnings report on several times its normal volume keeps drifting up for weeks.

- **Outcome:** excess return, over 20, 60 sessions, vs SPY, starting +1 sessions from the event, costs 20 bps
- **Schedule:** on each `earnings_reaction` (a reported result's two-session reaction (earnings_reaction@v1); anchor: the reaction's last session, E+1 (E: the report date))
- **Universe:** `instrument.security_type eq 'COMMON_STOCK'` and `instrument.status eq 'ACTIVE'` and `rollup.price_stats@v2.close gt 5` and `rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d gte 5000000`
- **Top K:** 20
- **Screeners:** `earnings_gap_volume`, `earnings_gap_only`
- **Baselines:** `momentum_12_1`, `size_small`
- **Frozen period:** from 2026-04-01
- **Notes:** Variant of small_cap_earnings_drift (the same schedule, entry and horizons): the question this edge asks is the increment, whether volume-confirmed large reactions drift more than same-size reactions without the volume, so earnings_gap_only (the same reaction criterion, no volume) is listed beside earnings_gap_volume and read against it on the same sessions; the base rate is the other reporters. The gap is the two-session reaction (close before the report to close after it, the PEAD window) of at least 10% over SPY, standing in for the practitioner's opening gap: only closes are stored, and the entry is the next close, not the opening-range break. The volume confirmation is earnings_gap_volume@v1.reaction_volume_ratio of at least 3 (the window's mean dollar volume over the 20 sessions before it), the last report's own, not earnings_reaction's four-report mean. The "dormant base" filter (a name that went nowhere for months) and the non-earnings catalysts (an FDA decision, a contract) are not tested: the idea's novel part beyond PEAD is the volume increment only. Common stock, active, close above $5, 20-day dollar volume of at least $5M (no exchange or cap limit, unlike the small-cap edge).

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** Post-earnings drift plus the high-volume premium: a neglected name re-rates in steps as analysts, funds and momentum screens catch up, and the first day under-reacts because the news is bigger than the pre-event holders (Bernard and Thomas 1989; practitioner playbooks).
2. **Persistence:** A discretionary playbook, not a fund strategy: capacity is small (mid and small caps) and it needs same-day action. The academic cousins persist through underreaction, but no peer-reviewed test of the exact template exists, and drift after earnings has faded in large caps (Martineau 2022).
3. **Outcome:** Excess return over SPY over 20 and 60 sessions after the entry close (the session after the reaction session), net of 20 bps a round trip (an assumption for names trading $5M a day or more): drift is held for weeks.
4. **Trigger timing:** The reaction is two sessions, close(E-1) to close(E+1) (E: the report date); the anchor is E+1 and the decision session D = E+1, so the entry session is S = E+2 (start_offset_sessions = 1). The event names are read at D from earnings_reaction@v1 (sessions_since_reaction = 0) and the volume ratio from earnings_gap_volume@v1 for the same report, never from a report known after D.
5. **Faithful replication:** No published rule with numbers exists: the practitioner description (Kullamagi's episodic pivot) is a gap of at least 10% on earnings or news, day-one volume far above average, after a dormant base. Here earnings_gap_volume v1: reaction of at least +10% over SPY and a window volume ratio of at least 3, the top 20 by reaction, entered at the close of the first session after the reaction.
6. **Expected size and sample:** Not published for the exact template; the academic cousins (PEAD, the high-volume premium, news continuation) are positive, and the filed small-cap PEAD carries the earnings subset. The increment of volume over same-size gaps is the unknown. Reports cluster in about eight seasons a year: independent sessions are the binding count.
7. **Capacity and costs:** Mid and small caps, long only, one to five events a week and large single-name variance; the entry is the next close, not the day-one open, so part of the move is gone. The outcome deducts 20 bps a round trip.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** The reaction can be an offering, a bid or guidance, not the report; a close-to-close reaction misses a gap that faded intraday; PEAD has faded in larger names. Retire if the frozen-period excess return of the volume-confirmed picks is not above both the base rate of the other reporters and the picks of earnings_gap_only.
9. **Decoys:** It may be PEAD with a volume filter: earnings_gap_only is that control, and the 12-1 momentum and size baselines (winners and small names gap more) run on the same sessions; momentum at earnings (filed) overlaps.

**Sources**

- Bernard, V. and Thomas, J., Post-Earnings-Announcement Drift: Delayed Price Response or Risk Premium?, Journal of Accounting Research, 1989 <https://doi.org/10.2307/2491062>
- Martineau, C., Rest in Peace Post-Earnings Announcement Drift, Critical Finance Review, 2022 <https://doi.org/10.1561/104.00000122>
- Gervais, S., Kaniel, R. and Mingelgrin, D., The High-Volume Return Premium, Journal of Finance, 2001
- Pritamani, M. and Singal, V., Return predictability following large price changes and information releases, Journal of Banking and Finance, 2001

## Adverse 8-K item drift (`eight_k_item_drift`)

**Status:** candidate. **Family:** B. **Thesis:** Stocks that disclose a non-reliance on their prior financials (8-K Item 4.02) keep underperforming the market over the following month.

- **Outcome:** excess return, over 20, 60 sessions, vs SPY, starting +1 sessions from the event, costs 20 bps
- **Schedule:** on each `filing_8k:4.02` (an 8-K carrying one Item (filing_events@v1); needs the item as its subtype; anchor: the session the filing became known, the first whose close is after SEC accepted it; Item 4.02, non-reliance on previously issued financial statements)
- **Universe:** `instrument.security_type eq 'COMMON_STOCK'` and `instrument.status eq 'ACTIVE'` and `rollup.price_stats@v2.close gt 5` and `rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d gte 5000000`
- **Top K:** 20
- **Screeners:** `eight_k_adverse_items`
- **Baselines:** `size_small`
- **Frozen period:** from 2026-04-01
- **Published:** mean excess 2.00% over 20 sessions (sec-api.io, Stock Price Reactions to Item 4.02 Disclosures in SEC Form 8-K Filings, 2024, 19 years)
- **Notes:** Short side, owner decision O2 (2026-10-17): a short result on liquid names may be a verdict basis; the outcome is minus the excess return over SPY, less cost and the borrow the site assumes (verdict.toml: 100 bps a year, no borrow data is stored). Item 4.02 is the sharpest item (the strongest figures, and a plain fact needing no text reading); 4.01 (resignation or dismissal) and 5.02 (forced or routine) need the text model to classify, so they are flagged by feature.eight_k_item_flag (4.02, 4.01 or 5.02 within 20 sessions) and tested only when a later PR reads the text. The screener ranks by dollar volume so the harness's short leg (the lowest scores) takes the most liquid filers first, among names that must also clear the capacity floor (capacity_adv_floor_usd). The event is the session the 8-K became known: the first session whose close follows the SEC acceptance instant (a 16:30 filing counts from the next session), recomputed from the stored acceptance time, never the stored known_from. Filings coverage has a documented null history (decision log 2026-10-18: the walks start with the first nightly walk, the D1 backfill reaches further back), so the independent-event count begins small.

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** 8-Ks arrive unscheduled and unstructured, nobody holds a forecast to compare them with, and attention is low: the market underreacts to the bad news they carry (Lerman and Livnat 2010); an Item 4.02 says the issuer's own audit committee no longer stands behind past numbers.
2. **Persistence:** Reading items takes parsing and judgement across hundreds of filings a day, and desks skip small filers; the trade is on the short side, which is costly and capacity-limited, so the mispricing is not competed away quickly. Persistence is a claim to test: the figures below are event studies, not a traded record.
3. **Outcome:** Minus the excess return over SPY over 20 and 60 sessions after the entry close (the session after the filing became known), net of 20 bps a round trip and the assumed borrow: a short position held after a non-reliance filing.
4. **Trigger timing:** The filing is known from the first session whose close follows its SEC acceptance; that session is the anchor and the decision session D = anchor, the entry session S = anchor + 1 (start_offset_sessions = 1). The names are read at D from filing_events@v1 (sessions_since_8k_4_02 = 0), never from a filing accepted after D's close. An amendment (8-K/A) is no new event.
5. **Faithful replication:** The source's rule first: the abnormal return after the Item 4.02 filing (sec-api.io study of more than 8,000 filings, 2004 to 2023; Audit Analytics 2005 to 2016). Here eight_k_adverse_items v1 ranks the liquid filers of the last 20 sessions and the harness shorts the 20 most liquid.
6. **Expected size and sample:** Around 2% over 20 sessions after Item 4.02 (CAR -1.1% on day +1, -2% over 20 days, sec-api.io, more than 8,000 filings 2004 to 2023); the Audit Analytics restatement study finds the later windows noisy, so the claim is a 20 to 30 session one. A few filers a month among liquid names: independent sessions are the binding count.
7. **Capacity and costs:** Shorting a filer that just disclosed a restatement is the costly side: the borrow can be expensive and recalled, and spreads widen on the news; the outcome deducts 20 bps and the site's assumed 100 bps a year, and only names trading $5M a day or more are shorted. An individual can avoid the name more cheaply than short it: the avoid list is the usable form.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** The drift is a short-horizon claim and the 20-session window can hold a rebound; most of the move can already be in the price at the next close; a company filing 4.02 for a minor correction looks the same as a large restatement; distressed names carry the average. Retire if the frozen-period short return of the picks is not above the base rate of every eligible name.
9. **Decoys:** Small size, low price and distress drive both the filing and the drift: the size baseline. Overlaps the late-filer edge (a restatement often follows an NT filing) and the event sensitivity track (ADR 0050), which already attributes unscheduled moves from 8-K items.

**Sources**

- Lerman, A. and Livnat, J., The New Form 8-K Disclosures, Review of Accounting Studies, 2010 <https://doi.org/10.1007/s11142-009-9114-7>
- sec-api.io, Stock Price Reactions to Item 4.02 Disclosures in SEC Form 8-K Filings, 2024 <https://sec-api.io/resources/stock-price-reactions-to-item-4-02-disclosures-in-sec-form-8-k-filings>
- Audit Analytics, Long-Term Capital Market Consequences of Financial Restatements, 2019 <https://blog.auditanalytics.com/long-term-capital-market-consequences-of-financial-restatements/>
- Whisenant, S., Sankaraguruswamy, S. and Raghunandan, K., Auditing: A Journal of Practice and Theory, 22(1), 2003 (reportable events disclosed at an auditor change) <https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=293404>

## Late filers (NT 10-K and NT 10-Q) (`late_filer_nt_drift`)

**Status:** candidate. **Family:** B. **Thesis:** Stocks that file a late-filing notice (Form 12b-25) keep drifting down over the following months.

- **Outcome:** excess return, over 20, 60 sessions, vs SPY, starting +1 sessions from the event, costs 20 bps
- **Schedule:** on each `nt_late_filing` (an NT 10-K or NT 10-Q late-filing notice (filing_events@v1); anchor: the session it became known)
- **Universe:** `instrument.security_type eq 'COMMON_STOCK'` and `instrument.status eq 'ACTIVE'` and `rollup.price_stats@v2.close gt 5` and `rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d gte 5000000`
- **Top K:** 20
- **Screeners:** `nt_late_filer`
- **Baselines:** `size_small`
- **Frozen period:** from 2026-04-01
- **Notes:** Short side, owner decision O2 (2026-10-17): a short result on liquid names may be a verdict basis; the outcome is minus the excess return over SPY, less cost and the assumed borrow (100 bps a year; no borrow data stored). The idea's own form is a filter (exclude late filers from a long screen); the harness tests it as the short side, which is the same claim stated so that a negative drift is a pass. The filers that clear the capacity floor (20-day dollar volume of $5M) are few, since late filers are mostly microcaps. Both NT 10-K and NT 10-Q are one event class (filing_events@v1, forms NT 10-K and NT 10-Q); the reason in the notice (boilerplate, accounting, an internal-control weakness) is in text that is not stored, so the reason-conditioned results of the papers (positive for specific legitimate reasons) are not tested. The event is the first session whose close follows the SEC acceptance. Filings coverage has a documented null history (decision log 2026-10-18).

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** The notice is a weak-form admission of a problem: the company cannot file on time, and small firms with low attention are slow to be marked down until the delayed numbers arrive (Dee, Hillison and Pacini 2010; Impink et al. 2012).
2. **Persistence:** It is a short-side idea in small caps where the borrow is hard, so arbitrage is costly; as a filter it asks nothing of the trader but the exclusion. The signs agree across studies and the magnitudes are modest and confounded by poor fundamentals, so persistence is a claim to test, not a given.
3. **Outcome:** Minus the excess return over SPY over 20 and 60 sessions after the entry close (the session after the notice became known), net of 20 bps a round trip and the assumed borrow.
4. **Trigger timing:** The notice is known from the first session whose close follows its SEC acceptance; that is the anchor and the decision session D, the entry session S = anchor + 1 (start_offset_sessions = 1). The names are read at D from filing_events@v1 (sessions_since_late_filing = 0), never from a notice accepted after D's close.
5. **Faithful replication:** The source's rule first: the abnormal return after the notice and in the following months, by reason (Dee, Hillison and Pacini 2010; Cao, Chen and Higgs 2016). Here nt_late_filer v1 takes the liquid filers of the last 20 sessions, whatever the reason, and the harness shorts the 20 most liquid.
6. **Expected size and sample:** Modest and consistent in sign: a downward drift in the post-filing months for both forms (Cao, Chen and Higgs 2016 and the 2017 study summarised by CPA Practice Advisor), -1.35% when the reason is an internal-control weakness (Impink et al. 2012). The page gives no single number to compare, so no published figure is set. Late filers are few among liquid names: independent sessions are the binding count.
7. **Capacity and costs:** Late filers are mostly small and thin; the liquid ones are shorted at the borrow of a distressed name, which can be dear or recalled. The outcome deducts 20 bps and the site's assumed 100 bps a year; only names trading $5M a day or more are shorted. The usable form for an individual is the exclusion list.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** A boilerplate notice and a legitimate specific one look the same without the text; confounded by distress, low price and small size; the drift may already be in the price by the next close. Retire if the frozen-period short return of the picks is not above the base rate of every eligible name.
9. **Decoys:** Distress, low price and small size: the size baseline. Overlaps the 8-K Item 4.02 edge (a restatement often follows a late filing).

## Pre-earnings run-up in past winners (`pre_earnings_runup_winners`)

**Status:** candidate. **Family:** B. **Thesis:** Stocks with extreme 12-month returns rise in the five sessions before their earnings report.

- **Outcome:** excess return, over 5 sessions, vs SPY, starting -6 sessions from the event, costs 10 bps
- **Schedule:** on each `earnings_expected` (a next report expected, scheduled or from the year-ago report (earnings_expected@v1); anchor: the expected report date's session)
- **Universe:** `instrument.security_type in ('COMMON_STOCK', 'ADR')` and `instrument.status eq 'ACTIVE'` and `rollup.price_stats@v2.close gt 5` and `rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d gte 50000000`
- **Top K:** 20
- **Screeners:** `pre_earnings_runup`
- **Baselines:** `momentum_12_1`, `size_small`
- **Frozen period:** from 2026-04-01
- **Published:** mean excess 1.58% over 5 sessions (Aboody, D., Lehavy, R. and Trueman, B., Limited attention and the earnings announcement returns of past stock market winners, Review of Accounting Studies, 2010)
- **Notes:** Long leg only: the run-up before the report. The give-back after the report (about -1.86% in the 5 days after) is the better half and needs a short or puts on the same winners; it needs a screener ranked the other way (the harness's short leg takes the lowest scores), so it is a follow-up, not a variant here. The window is the five sessions before the expected report date A: entry at the close of A-6, exit at the close of A-1 (start_offset_sessions = -6, horizon 5); the date must be known by the decision session D = A-7 (earnings_expected@v1: SCHEDULED, or the year-ago date plus 364 days: a PRIOR_YEAR date can miss the real report, so a window may not contain it). The paper's winners are the top decile by prior 12-month return; here the picks are the 20 highest 12-1 momentum names of the liquid names reporting in 7 sessions (a rank, not a decile). Momentum at earnings (filed) is the opposite sign over the announcement and must be reconciled in one reading. Liquid base as the earnings announcement premium edge: common stock or ADR, close above $5, 20-day dollar volume of at least $50M.

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** Individual investors buy attention-grabbing winners ahead of the news, and the demand is uninformed (Aboody, Lehavy and Trueman 2010); the winners' bid is the run-up, and the same demand reverses after the report.
2. **Persistence:** Small and short, and it needs a position around the announcement on a few names, so capacity is limited; the earnings announcement premium itself is filed as not found by Heitz et al. 2020, so this is the conditional version and may be decayed.
3. **Outcome:** Excess return over SPY over the five sessions from the close of the session six before the expected report to the close of the one before it, net of 10 bps a round trip: a hold that ends before the report.
4. **Trigger timing:** The expected report date A is read at the decision session D = A - 7 from earnings_expected@v1 (SCHEDULED, or PRIOR_YEAR: the year-ago report plus 364 days); an UNKNOWN date is excluded with a reason, and a date announced after D is not used. The entry session is S = A - 6 (start_offset_sessions = -6, allowed because the date is announced ahead) and the window closes at A - 1.
5. **Faithful replication:** The source's rule first: top-decile prior-12-month winners, the market-adjusted return in the 5 days before the announcement (Aboody, Lehavy and Trueman 2010). Here pre_earnings_runup v1 ranks the liquid names reporting in about 7 sessions by 12-1 momentum and the harness holds the top 20.
6. **Expected size and sample:** +1.58% market-adjusted in the 5 days before for top-decile winners, about -1.86% in the 5 after (Aboody, Lehavy and Trueman 2010, sample to the mid-2000s). Chen, Cohen and Wang find the run-up only before post-close announcement clusters; So and Wang find reversal profits larger before announcements. Reports cluster in a few weeks a quarter: independent sessions are the binding count.
7. **Capacity and costs:** Liquid names only ($50M a day), a five-session hold, two legs of a round trip a quarter; an individual can trade a few names, the 10 bps round trip is the assumption.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** A date that is a year-ago guess (PRIOR_YEAR) and misses the real report; a decayed effect (the premium itself is not found by Heitz et al. 2020); the run-up shows only before post-close announcements. Retire if the frozen-period excess return of the picks is not above the base rate of the other reporters after costs.
9. **Decoys:** Plain momentum and the size effect: winners are momentum names, so the 12-1 momentum baseline is the same ranking at every session, not only before earnings; and the earnings announcement premium of every reporter (the base rate here).

**Sources**

- Aboody, D., Lehavy, R. and Trueman, B., Limited attention and the earnings announcement returns of past stock market winners, Review of Accounting Studies, 2010 <https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/reaccs/v15y2010i2d10.1007_s11142-009-9104-9.html>
- Chen, J., Cohen, L. and Wang, C., Famous Firms, Earnings Clusters, and the Stock Market (working paper) <https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3685452>
- So, E. and Wang, S., News-driven return reversals: Liquidity provision ahead of earnings announcements, Journal of Financial Economics, 2014

## S&P 500 deletions rebound (`sp500_deletions_long`)

**Status:** candidate. **Family:** B. **Thesis:** Stocks dropped from the S&P 500 outperform the market over the following year.

- **Outcome:** excess return, over 20, 60, 250 sessions, vs SPY, starting +1 sessions from the event, costs 20 bps
- **Schedule:** on each `index_change:deletion` (an S&P 500 membership change (index_changes@v1); anchor: the session it is first stored (never the announcement); left the S&P 500 (sp500_removed))
- **Universe:** `instrument.security_type eq 'COMMON_STOCK'` and `instrument.status eq 'ACTIVE'` and `rollup.price_stats@v2.close gt 5` and `rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d gte 5000000`
- **Top K:** all qualified names
- **Screeners:** `sp500_deletion`
- **Baselines:** `momentum_12_1`, `size_small`
- **Frozen period:** from 2026-04-01
- **Notes:** Long side. The anchor is the session the removal was first stored in events/index_change, which is when it became known to us and is later than the announcement and the effective date, so the entry misses the first days; the 12-month rebound is the claim, and it is long enough that a few sessions' lag matters little. The base rate is every eligible name on the event sessions (picks stay within the event names); the S&P 500 additions are the mirror (an event subtype index_change:addition) and the rejected announcement edge. A name removed because it was bought is delisted, and the harness measures a delisted pick only if the delisting return is known (otherwise it is excluded, counted). The 250-session horizon outlasts the frozen period for any change stored since 2026-04-01; the 20 and 60 sessions read it sooner. About 20 to 25 deletions a year: the independent count reaches 40 only over a couple of years, even with the Tiingo history from 2018.

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** Index funds are forced to sell a deleted name at the effective date regardless of price, pushing it below value; deletions are often beaten-down value names demoted into the S&P 400 or 600, and nobody is paid to hold them (Arnott, Kalesnik and Wu 2018).
2. **Persistence:** Index funds are mandated to trade at the effective close, and the demand for a demoted name stays thin; but the same forced selling was front-run by arbitrageurs once it was known, and the announcement effect has disappeared (Greenwood and Sammon 2022), so this edge trades the longer rebound, which is a different claim that needs its own test.
3. **Outcome:** Excess return over SPY over 20, 60 and 250 sessions after the entry close (the session after the removal was first stored), net of 20 bps a round trip: a long hold of the deleted names.
4. **Trigger timing:** The removal is read from events/index_change by the session it was first stored (known, by construction, on or before D) and is the anchor; D = anchor and the entry session S = anchor + 1. The announcement and the effective date are earlier and not read, so no day of the forced selling is traded and nothing leaks forward.
5. **Faithful replication:** The source's rule first: equal-weight the deletions and hold 12 months from the effective date, against the additions (Arnott, Kalesnik and Wu 2018). Here sp500_deletion v1 takes every liquid deletion of the last 20 sessions, held from the first close after the removal was stored, with every eligible name on those sessions as the base rate.
6. **Expected size and sample:** Additions lagged discretionary deletions by more than 22 percentage points on average over the 12 months after the change, October 1989 to December 2017 (Research Affiliates; the figure is the corrected one, read in a 2018 summary because the paper is no longer hosted); the announcement premium fell to 0.8% for additions and -0.6% for deletions in 2010 to 2020 (Greenwood and Sammon). The 22 points are a spread of the two groups, not the long leg alone.
7. **Capacity and costs:** Large and mid caps with liquid shares, long only, about 20 names a year: an individual can hold them all; the 20 bps round trip is the assumption.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** Deletions that are delistings (takeovers) have no rebound to trade; a deletion is often a name that kept falling (momentum), not a value bounce; the demotion to a smaller index brings its own buyers. Retire if the frozen-period excess return of the picks is not above the base rate of every eligible name after costs.
9. **Decoys:** Deletions are value plus long-term reversal and additions are momentum: the 12-1 momentum and size baselines on the same sessions (a matched value or reversal control is not stored).

**Sources**

## Tender-offer drift after expiry (`tender_offer_drift`)

**Status:** candidate. **Family:** H. **Thesis:** Stocks whose own tender offer for their shares has run its course outperform the market for a year or more afterwards.

- **Outcome:** excess return, over 60, 250 sessions, vs SPY, starting +21 sessions from the event, costs 20 bps
- **Schedule:** on each `tender_offer` (an SC TO-I issuer or SC TO-T third-party tender offer (filing_events@v1); anchor: the session it became known)
- **Universe:** `instrument.security_type eq 'COMMON_STOCK'` and `instrument.status eq 'ACTIVE'` and `rollup.price_stats@v2.close gt 5` and `rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d gte 5000000`
- **Top K:** 10
- **Screeners:** `tender_offer_filed`
- **Baselines:** `size_small`
- **Frozen period:** from 2026-04-01
- **Notes:** The anchor is the session the SC TO-I or SC TO-T filing became known (the first session whose close follows its acceptance), not the expiry: the expiry is not stored. A tender offer must stay open at least 20 business days (SEC Rule 14e-1), so the entry is 21 sessions after the anchor (start_offset_sessions = 21; the decision session D is 20 sessions on), the earliest the offer can have expired; a longer or extended offer enters before expiry, and the harness cannot tell. That approximates the paper's "first close after expiry" and is the main limit of this edge. The class joins SC TO-I (the issuer's own offer) and SC TO-T (a third party's, a takeover): a third-party offer is a different event, and its target usually delists, so the issuer-only split is a follow-up (a new column of filing_events). The 250-session horizon outlasts the frozen period for any event filed since 2026-04-01, so only the 60-session read has a frozen slice for a long while. Filings coverage has a documented null history (decision log 2026-10-18); about 20 to 40 events a year make the independent count small.

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** Management signals undervaluation with its own money; arbitrageurs who bought for the tender sell the pro-rated remainder at expiry, which makes the post-expiry dip the cheap entry; long-horizon investors are slow to re-rate (Lakonishok and Vermaelen 1990; Peyer and Vermaelen 2009).
2. **Persistence:** The events are small-cap and infrequent (about 20 to 40 US self-tenders a year), so funds ignore them; Peyer and Vermaelen (2009) show the anomaly survived its 1990 publication. Tender-specific evidence after 2010 is not on file, and the open-market buyback anomaly largely faded after 2003 (Fu and Huang 2016), so persistence is a claim to test.
3. **Outcome:** Excess return over SPY over 60 and 250 sessions after the entry close, 21 sessions after the tender offer became known (about when it can first have expired), net of 20 bps a round trip: a long hold after the offer.
4. **Trigger timing:** The filing is known from the first session whose close follows its SEC acceptance (the anchor); the entry session S = anchor + 21 (start_offset_sessions = 21) and the decision session D = S - 1, 20 sessions after the anchor. The names are read at D from filing_events@v1 (sessions_since_tender_offer = 20), a filing the session had not yet seen is never read. The expiry itself is not read: a tender offer open at least 20 business days has expired by S only if it was not extended.
5. **Faithful replication:** The source's rule first: buy at the first close after expiry and hold two years (Lakonishok and Vermaelen 1990). Here tender_offer_filed v1 takes the liquid names with a tender offer 20 or more sessions old and the harness holds the 10 most liquid for 60 and 250 sessions (the 2-year hold exceeds the stored outcome history).
6. **Expected size and sample:** Abnormal returns of about 9% for the full sample and about 23% for small firms after expiry (Lakonishok and Vermaelen 1990; the page marks the figure as not verified, so no published figure is set); post-2010 tender-specific evidence is not on file, and the page's own assumption is that the effect has halved. Roughly 20 to 40 events a year: independent events are the binding count, and 250-session outcomes need history the store reaches only from 2018.
7. **Capacity and costs:** Small and mid caps, long only, with a capacity of $20k to $100k a name on the page's estimate; the filter here (5M dollars a day) keeps only the liquid end, which is not where the largest drift sat. The outcome deducts 20 bps a round trip.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** A third-party tender offer (a takeover) delists the target and is not the issuer self-tender the thesis is about; an extended offer is entered early; the buyback anomaly faded after 2003. Retire if the frozen-period excess return of the picks is not above the base rate of every eligible name.
9. **Decoys:** Size and value (tender names are small and cheap) and the reversal of a price drop that prompted the offer: the size baseline. Overlaps any buyback idea (variant: tender only).

**Sources**

- Lakonishok, J. and Vermaelen, T., Anomalous price behavior around repurchase tender offers, Journal of Finance, 1990
- Peyer, U. and Vermaelen, T., The nature and persistence of buyback anomalies, Review of Financial Studies, 2009
- Fu, F. and Huang, S., The persistence of long-run abnormal returns following stock repurchases and offerings, Management Science, 2016

## 12-1 momentum (`momentum_12_1`)

**Status:** candidate. **Thesis:** Stocks that rose most over the past year, leaving out the last month, keep outperforming the market over the next month.

- **Outcome:** excess return, over 20 sessions, vs SPY, entered 1 session after the decision session, costs 10 bps
- **Schedule:** month end
- **Universe:** preset `liquid_common_stocks`
- **Top K:** 50
- **Screeners:** `momentum_12_1`
- **Baselines:** `size_small`
- **Frozen period:** from 2026-04-01
- **Published:** decile spread 1.31% over 20 sessions (Jegadeesh, N. and Titman, S., Returns to Buying Winners and Selling Losers: Implications for Stock Market Efficiency, Journal of Finance, 1993, 25 years)
- **Notes:** Month-end start sessions spaced at least 20 sessions apart. rollup.trend_stats@v2.mom_12_1 is stored from about 2025-10-31, so the stored history gives about ten independent sessions, under the 40 the bar asks; the harness reports the count beside every number. The frozen period starts 2026-04-01 (owner decision 2026-10-08, ADR 0053 amendment).

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** Investors underreact to news and herd into winners; limits to arbitrage and disposition effects slow the price from reaching its value (Jegadeesh and Titman 1993; Barberis, Shleifer and Vishny 1998).
2. **Persistence:** The premium is large, old and widely known, and has survived two decades after its publication in most markets, but it crashes in sharp market rebounds (Daniel and Moskowitz 2016): the risk that keeps arbitrageurs from removing it.
3. **Outcome:** Excess return over SPY over the 20 sessions after the entry close (the session after a month-end decision), net of 10 bps a round trip (an assumption; the PRD's proposal for liquid names, ADR 0053 open decisions).
4. **Trigger timing:** mom_12_1 is computed from the close of the decision session D (rollups, nightly), so the first fill is the next session's close: the entry session is S = D + 1 (start_offset_sessions = 1) and the window runs from S's close; the screen is read at D, the outcome at S.
5. **Faithful replication:** The published rule: rank by the return from 12 months to 1 month back and hold the top decile (Jegadeesh and Titman 1993); here the top 50 of the liquid common stocks (liquid_common_stocks).
6. **Expected size and sample:** Historically about 1% a month for the winner-minus-loser decile spread in large US stocks, smaller since 2000 (Daniel and Moskowitz 2016). Ten independent sessions cannot distinguish that from zero: the harness reports the t statistic and the sessions beside it.
7. **Capacity and costs:** Large and liquid names only (price over 5 dollars, 50M dollars of 20-day volume): turnover is monthly and spreads are small; the 10 bps round trip is the assumption.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** Momentum crashes: sharp market rebounds after a drawdown reverse winners and losers (Daniel and Moskowitz 2016). Retire if the frozen-period decile spread is not above zero or the top decile does not beat the buy-and-hold mean of the eligible names.
9. **Decoys:** The same signal in the market's own drift (compare with buy-and-hold of the eligible names), the size and quality tilts of past winners, and the 52-week-high effect (a different screen on the same sessions).

9. **Decoys:** Plain 12-1 momentum on all liquid names; size; the 2-session reaction; survivorship from one universe snapshot (rerun on per-session snapshots before evidenced).

## Small-cap post-earnings drift (`small_cap_earnings_drift`)

**Status:** candidate. **Thesis:** Small Nasdaq-listed stocks keep drifting for weeks in the direction of their earnings-day reaction.

- **Outcome:** excess return, over 20, 60 sessions, vs SPY, starting +1 sessions from the event, costs 40 bps
- **Schedule:** on each `earnings_reaction` (a reported result's two-session reaction (earnings_reaction@v1); anchor: the reaction's last session, E+1 (E: the report date))
- **Universe:** `instrument.security_type eq 'COMMON_STOCK'` and `instrument.exchange eq 'NASDAQ'` and `instrument.status eq 'ACTIVE'` and `feature.market_cap lt 2000000000`
- **Top K:** 20
- **Screeners:** `pead_small_cap`
- **Baselines:** `momentum_12_1`, `size_small`
- **Frozen period:** from 2026-04-01
- **Notes:** Surprise = the excess return over SPY from the close before the report to the close after it (Brandt et al. 2008), earnings_reaction@v1 from events/earnings read by known_from. E is the report date (the group's definition), the window close(E-1) -> close(E+1). Long only. The $2B cap was decided 2026-10-08 (ED4 decisions); the harness reports the micro (under $300M) and small ($300M to $2B) buckets as variants, each a trial of the deflated Sharpe ratio. The large-cap control (from $10B) runs the same screener, whose own cap criterion (under $2B) leaves it no picks: only its base rate (event names of large caps) and the baselines are informative, the screener's row is empty by construction. A long-short variant is not expressible: a variant overrides only the outcome and the universe, and an outcome has no short leg (the harness would need a new kind); left to a later PR.

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** Investors underreact to earnings news, most in small names that few analysts cover and where arbitrage is expensive (Bernard and Thomas 1989; Chordia et al. 2009).
2. **Persistence:** Limits to arbitrage: the drift survives where spreads, short-sale costs and low capacity keep institutions out; it has faded in large caps but not in the smallest names (Martineau 2022).
3. **Outcome:** Excess return over SPY over 20 and 60 sessions after the reaction session, net of 40 bps a round trip (the ED4 decision 2026-10-08: small-cap spreads): drift is held for weeks.
4. **Trigger timing:** The reaction is two sessions, close(E-1) to close(E+1) (E: the report date, the first session on which the report can trade; the window is close(E-1) -> close(E+1)); the anchor is E+1 and the decision session D = E+1, so the entry session is S = E+2 (start_offset_sessions = 1). The event names are read at D from earnings_reaction@v1 (sessions_since_reaction = 0), never from a row known after D.
5. **Faithful replication:** The source's rule first: rank by the announcement-period return (Brandt, Kishore, Santa-Clara and Venkatachalam 2008) and hold the top bucket; here pead_small_cap v1: Nasdaq common stock, market cap under $2B, close at least $3, 20-day dollar volume before the report at least $2M, a complete reaction window of at least +5% over SPY, the top 20 by reaction.
6. **Expected size and sample:** The drift is largest in the smallest names and has decayed in large caps since the 1990s (Martineau 2022). Bars start 2024-10-03 (2018 for the event scope names, ADR 0050): about eight reports per name, thousands of events, but clustered in about eight reporting seasons, so independent sessions are the binding count.
7. **Capacity and costs:** Small caps trade with wide spreads and thin volume: the outcome deducts 40 bps (an assumption) and the harness reports by cap bucket; an individual can trade the liquid end.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** Fat tails (takeovers, dilution, delisting) and a wrong report time that puts the reaction on the wrong session. Retire if the frozen-period excess return of the top bucket is not above the base rate of every eligible name after costs.
9. **Decoys:** Short-term momentum (a big move continues for any reason) and the small-cap illiquidity premium: the 12-1 momentum and size baselines on the same sessions.

- Brandt, M., Kishore, R., Santa-Clara, P. and Venkatachalam, M., Earnings Announcements are Full of Surprises, working paper, 2008 <https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.909563>

## Volatility risk premium (`vrp_short_premium`)

**Status:** candidate. **Thesis:** Option implied volatility on liquid names tends to exceed the volatility that follows, so selling premium where implied is richest earns the gap.

- **Outcome:** expires otm, over 15, 21, 31 sessions, entered 1 session after the decision session, short put struck at delta 0.3, not assigned at the horizon
- **Schedule:** every session
- **Universe:** preset `liquid_optionable_floored`
- **Top K:** 20
- **Screeners:** `vrp_iv_hv`
- **Baselines:** `momentum_12_1`, `size_small`
- **Frozen period:** from 2026-04-01
- **Notes:** The one-IV rule (ADR 0053) governs the outcome (iv_field, the IBKR IV30), not the screener's selection, which reads feature.vrp_iv30. Hit (ED4a, owner intent 2026-10-08): the short put is not assigned, struck at a 0.30 delta at the entry close on the IBKR IV30 read at D, held to the horizon's close (15, 21 or 31 sessions: the 21 to 45 DTE the owner trades, the horizon the tenor). The strike is the exact Black-Scholes delta strike (r = q = 0): K = P exp(-z iv sqrt(T) + iv^2 T / 2), z = N^-1(1 - 0.30), T = horizon / 252, P the entry close; the hit is the window's return above K / P - 1. Reported beside the hit rate, never in it: reference_rate (the mean risk-neutral N(d2), the win rate an efficient premium would give) and touch_rate (the strike reached intraday). No premium and no P&L is modelled: a win rate overstates expectancy, because the rare losses are large. IBKR's IV is personal-use (licence recorded on the result rows), fine for this private evaluation. Variants: delta 0.16, strangle, call, our own IV30, windows with no report expected, and the older realised-to-implied ratio below 1. IV30 (a 30-day tenor) is applied to the 15- and 31-session horizons too: a term-structure approximation, the same in the hit and in the reference rate.

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** Holders of stock pay for crash and variance protection; the dealers who sell it ask a premium for jump and variance risk they cannot diversify (Carr and Wu 2009; Bakshi and Kapadia 2003).
2. **Persistence:** A risk premium, not a mispricing: the seller is paid for losses that cluster in crashes, when capital is scarce, so being known does not remove it.
3. **Outcome:** The short put is not assigned at the horizon's close (expires_otm): how often the option the owner sells finishes out of the money, beside the risk-neutral rate and the share of windows whose strike was touched on the way.
4. **Trigger timing:** IV30 (ibkr_iv@v1, Cboe chains) and HV30 are stored after the close of the decision session D, so the first fill is the next session's close: the entry session is S = D + 1 (start_offset_sessions = 1). The screen and the implied vol are read at D, the outcome at S.
5. **Faithful replication:** The screen is vrp_iv_hv v1 (IV30 minus HV30 at least 10 points, IV30 / HV30 at least 1.25, $50M of 20-day dollar volume, price above $5, no leveraged or inverse fund), the history-evaluable part of vrp_scanner (IV30 at least 50% there). short_premium_liquidity (tradable put and call wings, option_liquidity@v1) and vrp_scanner rejoin once >= 40 independent sessions of chain data exist. The published setting is index variance (Carr and Wu 2009); single names are a step away from it.
6. **Expected size and sample:** Implied variance exceeds realised on average for the S&P 500 and most large stocks (Carr and Wu 2009); the single-name premium is smaller than the index's (Driessen, Maenhout and Vilkov 2009). IV30 history covers 502 sessions (ibkr_iv@v1, roadmap): 20-session windows spaced a horizon apart give about 25 independent sessions, under the 40 the bar asks, unless ED4a counts names within a session; chains are stored from 2026-10-02 only (docs/screeners/vrp-scanner.md), so short_premium_liquidity has days of history.
7. **Capacity and costs:** The cost is the option bid-ask spread, judged live; vrp_scanner (from v2) scores the put's stored spread (under 15% of mid), open interest and volume (over 1,000 each), and v3 requires $50M of 20-day dollar volume. An individual's size is far below the open interest.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** Losses arrive together in volatility spikes (short premium is short the left tail), so the drawdown cap, not the hit rate, decides; an earnings date inside the window raises implied for a reason. Retire if, in the frozen period, the picks' hit rate is not above the base rate of every liquid optionable name, or the cap is breached more often than the base rate.
9. **Decoys:** Implied above realised before every earnings date and after every shock (a known event, not a premium), and high-IV names are small: compare with the size and 12-1 momentum baselines on the same sessions, and log windows without an earnings date as a variant.

**Sources**

- Carr, P. and Wu, L., Variance Risk Premiums, Review of Financial Studies, 2009 <https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhn038>
- Bakshi, G. and Kapadia, N., Delta-Hedged Gains and the Negative Market Volatility Risk Premium, Review of Financial Studies, 2003 <https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhg002>
- Driessen, J., Maenhout, P. and Vilkov, G., The Price of Correlation Risk: Evidence from Equity Options, Journal of Finance, 2009 <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2009.01467.x>
- AQR, Understanding the Volatility Risk Premium (white paper) <https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/White-Papers/Understanding-the-Volatility-Risk-Premium>
- Quantpedia, Volatility Risk Premium Effect (strategy page) <https://quantpedia.com/strategies/volatility-risk-premium-effect/>

## Leveraged ETF rebalancing (`leveraged_etf_rebalancing`)

**Status:** rejected. **Thesis:** Leveraged and inverse funds trade with the day's move near the close, pushing their underlying further the same way into the close.

**Why rejected:** Intraday: the flow moves prices in the last minutes and reverses, which daily bars cannot see or trade, and what is left is weak after costs (Ivanov and Lenkey 2014; Shum et al. 2016); checked 2026-10-07.

- **Outcome:** excess return, over 1 session, vs SPY, entered 1 session after the decision session
- **Schedule:** every session
- **Universe:** `instrument.is_leveraged eq True`
- **Top K:** all qualified names
- **Screeners:** none yet
- **Baselines:** none yet

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** A fund with daily leverage L must trade L(L-1) times the day's return on its assets, buying after up days and selling after down days, near the close (Cheng and Madhavan 2009).
2. **Persistence:** The mandate fixes the trade, so it is predictable; but it is small next to closing volume and offset by the funds' own creations and redemptions (Ivanov and Lenkey 2014).
3. **Outcome:** Excess return of the funds' underlying over SPY over one session; the real window is minutes.
4. **Trigger timing:** The day's move is known only in the last hour of S; a daily close is too late to act.
5. **Faithful replication:** Cheng and Madhavan's rebalancing demand, L(L-1) times the day's return times assets.
6. **Expected size and sample:** Small next to closing volume after creations and redemptions net out (Ivanov and Lenkey 2014).
7. **Capacity and costs:** Needs intraday execution at the close; spreads and impact take what is left.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** Reversal the next morning; fund flows that offset the rebalance.
9. **Decoys:** Plain intraday momentum on volatile days, which needs no fund at all.

**Sources**

- Cheng, M. and Madhavan, A., The Dynamics of Leveraged and Inverse Exchange-Traded Funds, Journal of Investment Management, 2009 <https://joim.com/article/the-dynamics-of-leveraged-and-inverse-exchange-traded-funds/>

**Status:** rejected. **Thesis:** Stocks added to the S&P 500 rise around their inclusion as index funds buy them.

**Why rejected:** Under 1% since 2010 (Greenwood and Sammon, The Disappearing Index Effect), inside costs and the announcement-to-inclusion window; checked 2026-10-07.

- **Outcome:** excess return, over 20 sessions, vs SPY, starting +1 sessions from the event

- **Top K:** all qualified names
- **Screeners:** none yet
- **Baselines:** none yet

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** Index funds must buy an added name by the effective date; the price pressure was the original effect (Shleifer 1986; Harris and Gurel 1986).
2. **Persistence:** Weak: arbitrageurs trade between announcement and effective date and S&P spreads the demand, so the effect has been competed away.

6. **Expected size and sample:** Under 1% since 2010 (Greenwood and Sammon); a few dozen additions a year.
7. **Capacity and costs:** Large caps, cheap to trade, but the window is crowded by index arbitrageurs.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** The pressure reverses after inclusion; a leak front-runs the announcement.
9. **Decoys:** Additions follow strong past returns and growth in size: the momentum and size baselines.
