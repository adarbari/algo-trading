## Asset growth (an avoid list) (`asset_growth`)

**Status:** candidate. **Family:** A. **Thesis:** Companies whose total assets grew fastest over the last year underperform the market over the following months, and slow growers outperform.

- **Outcome:** excess return, over 20, 60 sessions, vs SPY, entered 1 session after the decision session, costs 10 bps
- **Schedule:** month end
- **Universe:** preset `liquid_common_stocks`
- **Top K:** 50
- **Screeners:** `asset_growth`
- **Baselines:** `size_small`, `momentum_12_1`
- **Frozen period:** from 2026-04-01
- **Notes:** Month-end start sessions spaced at least 20 sessions apart. The statement groups and the rollups of this edge are stored only from the nightly that first carries them, so the stored history gives few independent sessions, under the 40 the bar asks; the harness reports the count beside every number. The frozen period starts 2026-04-01 (owner decision 2026-10-08, ADR 0053 amendment). The main outcome is the avoid list: the bottom 50 of the ranking (the fastest asset growers) held short, minus the excess return, with the site's borrow cost (ADR 0053 amendment, outcome sides; a short result on liquid names may be a verdict basis, owner decision O2). The long side (slowest growers) and the spread are variants; the spread is diagnostic only. asset_growth is total assets over the assets a year back (within 40 days of a year) less one, from facts accepted by the session; an acquisition and an offering both lift it and nothing separates them.

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** Managers over-invest and build empires, and investors extrapolate growth; on the rational side, low discount rates cause high investment (Cooper, Gulen and Schill 2008). The effect also captures financing: the asset growers are often issuers (Cooper, Gulen and Ion).
2. **Persistence:** Known as the investment factor (the CMA factor of Fama and French 2015) yet still priced in large caps through the 2010s, because it is mostly a short-leg, glamour-avoidance effect: the fast growers are costly to short, and an avoid list asks nobody to.
3. **Outcome:** Minus the excess return over SPY over the 20 and 60 sessions after the entry close (the session after a month-end decision) of the 50 fastest asset growers among the liquid common stocks, less 10 bps a round trip and the assumed borrow cost: the value of avoiding them.
4. **Trigger timing:** Every statement figure is read as of the session its filing's acceptance was public (statements_accepted, H2), never at its period end, so the month-end decision session D sees only filings accepted by D. The entry session is S = D + 1 (start_offset_sessions = 1): the first fill is the next session's close and the window runs from S's close; the screen is read at D, the outcome at S.
5. **Faithful replication:** Cooper, Gulen and Schill: asset growth = total assets(t-1) / total assets(t-2) - 1; deciles sorted in June, held 12 months, equal- and value-weighted. Here the stored year-over-year growth to the latest balance sheet at each month end, the fastest 50 as the avoid list, held 20 and 60 sessions: a monthly, not annual, sort.
6. **Expected size and sample:** The decile spread was about 1.7% a month equal-weighted and 1.0% value-weighted, 1968-2003 (approximate, not verified against the paper); the investment factor has been weak since 2017 (about 0.2% a month 2008-2024, approximate). On liquid names the short leg is a fraction of that. A few independent sessions cannot distinguish that from zero: the harness reports the t statistic and the sessions beside it.
7. **Capacity and costs:** An avoid list, not a short book: the cost of the main outcome is the borrow assumption (verdict.toml) plus 10 bps a round trip; fast growers are often hard to borrow, which is exactly why the premium persists, so the result is the value of not owning them, not a tradable short.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** Growth rallies (2020-2021 asset-heavy growth) punish the avoid list; acquisitive growers and fresh offerings are read as the same thing. Retire if the frozen-period short-side result is not above zero.
9. **Decoys:** Net share issuance and the value tilt (issuers are the fast growers; the net_share_issuance edge), size, and acquisitions (test excluding the largest jumps).

**Sources**

- Cooper, M., Gulen, H. and Schill, M., Asset Growth and the Cross-Section of Stock Returns, Journal of Finance, 2008 <https://quantpedia.com/strategies/asset-growth-effect>
- Cooper, M., Gulen, H. and Ion, M., The Use of Asset Growth in Empirical Asset Pricing Models, working paper, 2019 <https://finance.unibocconi.eu/sites/default/files/files/media/attachments/Cooper_Gulen_Ion_Manuscript20190507084119.pdf>
- Hou, K., Xue, C. and Zhang, L., Replicating Anomalies, Review of Financial Studies, 2020 <https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v33y2020i5p2019-2133..html>

## Cash-based operating profitability (`cash_based_operating_profitability`)

**Status:** candidate. **Family:** A. **Thesis:** Stocks that earn the most cash operating profit on their assets keep outperforming the market over the following months.

- **Outcome:** excess return, over 20, 60 sessions, vs SPY, entered 1 session after the decision session, costs 10 bps
- **Schedule:** month end
- **Universe:** preset `liquid_common_stocks`
- **Top K:** 50
- **Screeners:** `cash_op_profitability`, `composite_value`
- **Baselines:** `size_small`, `momentum_12_1`
- **Frozen period:** from 2026-04-01
- **Published:** decile spread 0.47% over 20 sessions (Ball, G., Gerakos, J., Linnainmaa, J. and Nikolaev, V., Accruals, cash flows, and operating profitability in the cross section of stock returns, Journal of Financial Economics, 2016, 51 years)
- **Notes:** Month-end start sessions spaced at least 20 sessions apart. The statement groups and the rollups of this edge are stored only from the nightly that first carries them, so the stored history gives few independent sessions, under the 40 the bar asks; the harness reports the count beside every number. The frozen period starts 2026-04-01 (owner decision 2026-10-08, ADR 0053 amendment). The second screener, composite_value, is the catalogue's variant: cheap on a two-part value yield among cash-profitable names ('only useful combined with profitability'); the harness scores it beside the main one on the same sessions. cash_op_profitability is built from stored lines (EBIT less the gap between net income and operating cash flow, over total assets): the paper's line by line accrual changes and its R&D add-back are not stored, so it is a proxy, not a replication; decile sorts are the harness's, not the paper's NYSE breakpoints. long_short, where run, is diagnostic only (ADR 0053 amendment, outcome sides).

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** Investors extrapolate reported earnings; the accrual part of earnings mean-reverts, and the cash part is durable. Removing accruals from profitability keeps the signal of lasting cash generation and drops the part that reverses, so holders of low-profit story stocks are systematically on the wrong side (Ball, Gerakos, Linnainmaa and Nikolaev 2016).
2. **Persistence:** Profitability is partly a risk-like premium (profitable firms are not cheap; Fama and French adopted it as a factor in 2015) and partly a limit to arbitrage on the short side. It is well known yet the long leg, quality growth, is a comfortable trade, so there is little pressure to arbitrage it away.
3. **Outcome:** Excess return over SPY over the 20 and 60 sessions after the entry close (the session after a month-end decision), net of 10 bps a round trip (an assumption; the PRD's proposal for liquid names, ADR 0053 open decisions).
4. **Trigger timing:** Every statement figure is read as of the session its filing's acceptance was public (statements_accepted, H2), never at its period end, so the month-end decision session D sees only filings accepted by D. The entry session is S = D + 1 (start_offset_sessions = 1): the first fill is the next session's close and the window runs from S's close; the screen is read at D, the outcome at S.
5. **Faithful replication:** The published rule: sort on cash-based operating profitability (operating profit less accruals, over total assets) and hold the top decile, rebalanced annually after the filings (Ball et al. 2016). Here the top 50 of the liquid common stocks, ranked on a stored-line proxy; the proxy and what it omits are in the field's description and the notes, and the source's own number is the published figure beside the result.
6. **Expected size and sample:** The published long-short decile spread is 0.47% a month (value-weighted, 1963-2014, t = 3.2), a three-factor alpha of 90 bp a month for the high decile; profitability premia after publication are smaller (McLean and Pontiff 2016 put the average haircut near 58%). A few independent sessions cannot distinguish that from zero: the harness reports the t statistic and the sessions beside it.
7. **Capacity and costs:** Large and liquid names only (price over 5 dollars, 50M dollars of 20-day volume): the signal moves slowly, turnover is low and the spread is small; the 10 bps round trip is the assumption. No short leg in the main outcome.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** Profitability is a quality tilt: it lags in junk rallies and value reversals, and a growth or mega-cap tech tilt can stand in for it. Retire if the frozen-period decile spread is not above zero or the top 50 does not beat the buy-and-hold mean of the eligible names.
9. **Decoys:** A growth and large-cap tech tilt (the size and 12-1 momentum baselines on the same sessions), gross profitability and accruals taken alone (the composite_mispricing_score components), and the quality composite (quality_minus_junk): the test is whether cash profitability adds beyond them.

**Sources**

- Ball, G., Gerakos, J., Linnainmaa, J. and Nikolaev, V., Accruals, cash flows, and operating profitability in the cross section of stock returns, Journal of Financial Economics, 2016 <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2016.03.002>
- Novy-Marx, R., The Other Side of Value: The Gross Profitability Premium, Journal of Financial Economics, 2013 <https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w15940/w15940.pdf>
- Chen, A. and Zimmermann, T., Open Source Cross-Sectional Asset Pricing, Critical Finance Review, 2022 <https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3604626>

## Financial distress (an avoid list) (`distress_failure_probability`)

**Status:** candidate. **Family:** A. **Thesis:** Stocks with the highest predicted bankruptcy risk underperform the market over the following months, and the safest outperform.

- **Outcome:** excess return, over 20, 60 sessions, vs SPY, entered 1 session after the decision session, costs 10 bps
- **Schedule:** month end
- **Universe:** preset `liquid_common_stocks`
- **Top K:** 50
- **Screeners:** `distress_ohlson`
- **Baselines:** `size_small`, `momentum_12_1`
- **Frozen period:** from 2026-04-01
- **Notes:** Month-end start sessions spaced at least 20 sessions apart. The statement groups and the rollups of this edge are stored only from the nightly that first carries them, so the stored history gives few independent sessions, under the 40 the bar asks; the harness reports the count beside every number. The frozen period starts 2026-04-01 (owner decision 2026-10-08, ADR 0053 amendment). Only what the stored statements support is built: ohlson_o is a simplified Ohlson O-score (the INTWO and CHIN terms need last year's net income and the price-index deflator is a per-session constant, all omitted), a rank input, not a probability. Campbell, Hilscher and Szilagyi's failure probability needs market terms (excess return, volatility, relative size, market to book, price) from another group and is not built; this edge is therefore the accounting-only distress screen (Ohlson 1980 and Dichev 1998 style), and the harness's verdict on it says nothing about the market-based model. The main outcome is the avoid list: the 50 most distressed held short (ADR 0053 amendment, outcome sides; owner decision O2), with the long side and the spread as variants (diagnostic only).

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** Investors overpay for the option-like upside of distressed equity (lottery demand), and limits to arbitrage (hard to short, low institutional ownership) keep the price from falling to value (Campbell, Hilscher and Szilagyi 2008; Dichev 1998).
2. **Persistence:** Short-side and small-cap concentrated, and weaker after 2008: Hou, Xue and Zhang find distress anomalies largely insignificant value-weighted, and 2020-2021 showed the crash risk of the short leg. What persists is the avoid list: it asks nobody to short, and distressed names stay costly to short.
3. **Outcome:** Minus the excess return over SPY over the 20 and 60 sessions after the entry close (the session after a month-end decision) of the 50 highest O-score names among the liquid common stocks, less 10 bps a round trip and the assumed borrow cost: the value of avoiding them.
4. **Trigger timing:** Every statement figure is read as of the session its filing's acceptance was public (statements_accepted, H2), never at its period end, so the month-end decision session D sees only filings accepted by D. The entry session is S = D + 1 (start_offset_sessions = 1): the first fill is the next session's close and the window runs from S's close; the screen is read at D, the outcome at S.
5. **Faithful replication:** Campbell, Hilscher and Szilagyi: a dynamic logit of 12-month failure on accounting and market terms, deciles, monthly rebalance, long low-distress and short high-distress. Here the Ohlson O-score alone from stored statements, the 50 highest as the avoid list: a partial replication, with the omitted terms named above and in the field's description.
6. **Expected size and sample:** The top 5% distressed stocks earned about 0.7% a month below the bottom (Campbell, Hilscher and Szilagyi 2008, 1981-2003; approximate, not verified), concentrated in small caps and weaker value-weighted (Hou, Xue and Zhang 2020). On liquid names expect little. A few independent sessions cannot distinguish that from zero: the harness reports the t statistic and the sessions beside it.
7. **Capacity and costs:** An avoid list, not a short book: distressed names are hard and expensive to borrow, which is why the premium persists; the cost of the main outcome is the borrow assumption plus 10 bps a round trip, so the result is the value of not owning them.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** Distressed rallies (2020-2021) and rescue financings reverse the short leg violently; an accounting-only score misses market-implied distress. Retire if the frozen-period short-side result is not above zero.
9. **Decoys:** Low-price, idiosyncratic-volatility and lottery names (the same cluster), size and leverage; the size and 12-1 momentum baselines on the same sessions.

**Sources**

- Campbell, J., Hilscher, J. and Szilagyi, J., In Search of Distress Risk, Journal of Finance, 2008
- Campbell, J., Hilscher, J. and Szilagyi, J., Predicting Financial Distress and the Performance of Distressed Stocks, Journal of Investment Management, 2011 <https://joim.com/article/predicting-financial-distress-and-the-performance-of-distressed-stocks/>
- Dichev, I., Is the Risk of Bankruptcy a Systematic Risk?, Journal of Finance, 1998
- Hou, K., Xue, C. and Zhang, L., Replicating Anomalies, Review of Financial Studies, 2020 <https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v33y2020i5p2019-2133..html>

## Net share issuance (`net_share_issuance`)

**Status:** candidate. **Family:** A. **Thesis:** Companies that shrink their share count outperform the market over the following months, and companies that issue shares underperform.

- **Outcome:** excess return, over 20, 60 sessions, vs SPY, entered 1 session after the decision session, costs 10 bps
- **Schedule:** month end
- **Universe:** preset `liquid_common_stocks`
- **Top K:** 50
- **Screeners:** `net_share_issuance`
- **Baselines:** `size_small`, `momentum_12_1`
- **Frozen period:** from 2026-04-01
- **Notes:** Month-end start sessions spaced at least 20 sessions apart. The statement groups and the rollups of this edge are stored only from the nightly that first carries them, so the stored history gives few independent sessions, under the 40 the bar asks; the harness reports the count beside every number. The frozen period starts 2026-04-01 (owner decision 2026-10-08, ADR 0053 amendment). The score is the log change of the split-adjusted share count over a year (net_issuance_pct_12m, Pontiff and Woodgate's definition) from the cover-page or weighted-average count of fundamentals@v4 (a cover count against a cover count, never mixed); the screener ranks lowest first, so the main outcome holds the top 50 net repurchasers. The issuers are the variant issuers_short (the bottom 50 held short, an avoid list: ADR 0053 amendment, outcome sides, with the site's borrow cost); the spread is diagnostic only. The 3-year composite issuance of Daniel and Titman is not built (it needs a five-year return history joined to market value).

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** Managers time the market: they issue when they believe shares are overvalued and repurchase when undervalued, and investors do not discount the signal fully. Issuers are also often glamour, high-asset-growth names (Pontiff and Woodgate 2008).
2. **Persistence:** One of the few anomalies Fama and French (2008) found in every size group. It is partly mechanical dilution and partly informed managers, and it persists because the short leg (serial issuers) is costly to short and the long leg (steady repurchasers) is a boring trade; it moves slowly, so it is cheap to trade.
3. **Outcome:** Excess return over SPY over the 20 and 60 sessions after the entry close (the session after a month-end decision), net of 10 bps a round trip (an assumption; the PRD's proposal for liquid names, ADR 0053 open decisions); the short variant is minus that excess return, less cost and borrow.
4. **Trigger timing:** Every statement figure is read as of the session its filing's acceptance was public (statements_accepted, H2), never at its period end, so the month-end decision session D sees only filings accepted by D. The entry session is S = D + 1 (start_offset_sessions = 1): the first fill is the next session's close and the window runs from S's close; the screen is read at D, the outcome at S.
5. **Faithful replication:** Pontiff and Woodgate: net issuance is the log change of the split-adjusted shares outstanding over the prior 12 months; decile sorts, one-month hold, monthly rebalance. Here the top 50 (the most negative) of the liquid common stocks, held 20 and 60 sessions: a long-only slice of their decile, since a net buyback is the actionable side for an individual.
6. **Expected size and sample:** Issuance predicts returns more significantly than size, book to market or momentum after 1970 (Pontiff and Woodgate 2008; the abstract states no decile spread and none is cited here). The effect is concentrated in issuers (the short leg) and in small caps, so on liquid names expect less. A few independent sessions cannot distinguish that from zero: the harness reports the t statistic and the sessions beside it.
7. **Capacity and costs:** Large and liquid names only; the count changes slowly, turnover is low. The short variant needs borrow (an assumed cost from verdict.toml) and heavy issuers can be hard to borrow: it is an avoid list, not a trade. 10 bps round trip is the assumption.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** Buyback names are not a uniform tilt: they cluster in mature cash-rich companies and lag growth rallies (the S&P 500 Buyback Index lagged the index in the decade to 2024); a cover-page count artefact or an acquisition paid in shares is read as an issue. Retire if the frozen-period decile spread is not above zero or the top 50 does not beat the buy-and-hold mean of the eligible names.
9. **Decoys:** Asset growth and the value tilt (issuers are high asset growers; the asset_growth edge and the 12-1 momentum and size baselines on the same sessions), and an acquisition-driven count change (test excluding the largest rises).

**Sources**

- Pontiff, J. and Woodgate, A., Share Issuance and Cross-Sectional Returns, Journal of Finance, 2008 <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2008.01335.x>
- Fama, E. and French, K., Dissecting Anomalies, Journal of Finance, 2008
- Daniel, K. and Titman, S., Market Reactions to Tangible and Intangible Information, Journal of Finance, 2006
- Chen, A. and Zimmermann, T., Open Source Cross-Sectional Asset Pricing, Critical Finance Review, 2022 <https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3604626>

## Quality minus junk (`quality_minus_junk`)

**Status:** candidate. **Family:** A. **Thesis:** Stocks with high profitability and low leverage and distress risk keep outperforming the market, and junk lags.

- **Outcome:** excess return, over 20, 60 sessions, vs SPY, entered 1 session after the decision session, costs 10 bps
- **Schedule:** month end
- **Universe:** preset `liquid_common_stocks`
- **Top K:** 50
- **Screeners:** `qmj`
- **Baselines:** `size_small`, `momentum_12_1`
- **Frozen period:** from 2026-04-01
- **Notes:** Month-end start sessions spaced at least 20 sessions apart. The statement groups and the rollups of this edge are stored only from the nightly that first carries them, so the stored history gives few independent sessions, under the 40 the bar asks; the harness reports the count beside every number. The frozen period starts 2026-04-01 (owner decision 2026-10-08, ADR 0053 amendment). qmj_score is built from what the stored statements support: profitability in full (gross profit over assets, ROE, ROA, cash flow over assets, gross margin, low accruals) and safety only as low leverage and low Ohlson O-score; the paper's growth part (five-year changes), low beta, Altman Z and earnings volatility are not built (no stored five-year history of the measures, beta and volatility are price based and another group). Not size-neutral and not within industry, as the paper is. long_short, where run, is diagnostic only.

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** Quality is under-priced relative to its justified premium: investors overpay for junk because of lottery preferences and benchmark-relative incentives, and leverage-constrained managers prefer high-beta junk to levering up quality (Asness, Frazzini and Pedersen 2019).
2. **Persistence:** Risk explanations are weak (quality has lower beta and did well in crises) and the long leg is a boring large-cap tilt that mandates do not chase; crowding has grown since quality ETFs (2015) but the 2010s and 2022 still paid. The premium is largely profitability (Novy-Marx 2014), which is why the harness tests whether the composite beats cash profitability alone.
3. **Outcome:** Excess return over SPY over the 20 and 60 sessions after the entry close (the session after a month-end decision), net of 10 bps a round trip (an assumption; the PRD's proposal for liquid names, ADR 0053 open decisions).
4. **Trigger timing:** Every statement figure is read as of the session its filing's acceptance was public (statements_accepted, H2), never at its period end, so the month-end decision session D sees only filings accepted by D. The entry session is S = D + 1 (start_offset_sessions = 1): the first fill is the next session's close and the window runs from S's close; the screen is read at D, the outcome at S.
5. **Faithful replication:** Asness, Frazzini and Pedersen: quality = z(profitability) + z(growth) + z(safety), long the top 30%, short the bottom 30%, size-neutral, monthly rebalance on data lagged to be public. Here the top 50 of the liquid common stocks on qmj_score (profitability and a leverage-and-distress safety, no growth part): a partial replication, with the omissions named in the field and the notes.
6. **Expected size and sample:** About 0.39% a month for the US long-short factor, t above 5 (Asness, Frazzini and Pedersen 2019; approximate, not re-verified), 4.7% a year 1964-2023 by the AQR data; a long-only top 50 earns a fraction of the spread. A few independent sessions cannot distinguish that from zero: the harness reports the t statistic and the sessions beside it.
7. **Capacity and costs:** Huge capacity on the mega-cap long leg; price over 5 dollars and 50M dollars of 20-day volume here. Turnover is low; the 10 bps round trip is the assumption. No short leg in the main outcome.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** Quality lags when junk rallies (2023's mega-cap tech was high quality, but low-quality rebounds and rate shocks hurt it) and when it is expensive. Retire if the frozen-period decile spread is not above zero or the top 50 does not beat the buy-and-hold mean of the eligible names.
9. **Decoys:** Cash-based operating profitability alone (Novy-Marx finds most quality definitions are dominated by gross profitability), low volatility and low beta, and a large-cap growth tilt (the size and 12-1 momentum baselines on the same sessions).

**Sources**

- Asness, C., Frazzini, A. and Pedersen, L., Quality Minus Junk, Review of Accounting Studies, 2019
- Novy-Marx, R., The Quality Dimension of Value Investing, working paper, 2014
- Alpha Architect, Value and Quality Stocks (summary of the AQR data) <https://alphaarchitect.com/value-and-quality-stocks/>

## BDC price to NAV reversion (`bdc_price_to_nav_reversion`)

**Status:** candidate. **Family:** H. **Thesis:** Business development companies trading at a deep discount to their last reported net asset value outperform those at premiums over the next quarter.

- **Outcome:** excess return, over 60 sessions, vs SPY, entered 1 session after the decision session, costs 30 bps
- **Schedule:** month end
- **Universe:** preset `bdc_listed`
- **Top K:** 10
- **Screeners:** `bdc_price_to_nav`
- **Baselines:** none yet
- **Frozen period:** from 2026-04-01
- **Notes:** Month-end start sessions spaced at least 60 sessions apart (one quarter, the NAV cadence). The NAV is not a new source: the net asset value per share of a BDC is its stockholders' equity over its shares, so price to NAV is pb_ratio of the stored statements (a BDC's equity is its net assets), public from its 10-Q or 10-K acceptance (statements_accepted, H2). D5's events/fund_nav (Tiingo NAV series) does not carry BDCs, whose NAV is quarterly. The universe is a hand list of listed BDCs (the reference snapshot has no BDC type and the SEC company table no SIC for them), kept equal in the selection preset and the screener by a fitness test. About 38 names: 10 picks is a quarter of the list, so the decile spread is over a handful of names a session and the harness's t is weak; the catalogue's rule (discount against the name's own three-year average and peers) needs a NAV history that is not stored, so v1 ranks the plain price to NAV, and says so. The discount must be read with the NAV trend (a falling NAV earns its discount): not conditioned in v1.

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** BDC holders are retail and buy on yield and sell on dividend cuts; there is no institutional arbitrage because BDCs are excluded from most indexes (the acquired-fund-fee rule drove them out of the S&P and Russell indexes in 2014) and are hard to borrow, and the net asset value is published only quarterly, so the price drifts away from it.
2. **Persistence:** Index exclusion and the 2014 rule are structural, the quarterly NAV cadence is regulatory and the sector is small (about 50 listed BDCs, about 70 billion dollars), too small for large funds: the closed-end fund literature finds discounts predict returns (Pontiff 1995), and the one cited BDC datum is that the discount move dominated a year of price and NAV returns.
3. **Outcome:** Excess return over SPY over the 60 sessions (one quarter) after the entry close (the session after a month-end decision), net of 30 bps a round trip (an assumption for small, wide-spread names).
4. **Trigger timing:** Every statement figure is read as of the session its filing's acceptance was public (statements_accepted, H2), never at its period end, so the month-end decision session D sees only filings accepted by D. The entry session is S = D + 1 (start_offset_sessions = 1): the first fill is the next session's close and the window runs from S's close; the screen is read at D, the outcome at S. The NAV used is the last quarterly report accepted by D, up to four months old; the price is D's close.
5. **Faithful replication:** No canonical academic rule. The practitioner metric is Closed-End Fund Advisors' three-year relative discount (the current discount against the name's own trailing three-year average); this catalogue's formalisation ranks by price to NAV relative to the name and to peers and holds the bottom quintile, rebalanced quarterly after the 10-Q. v1 is the simpler plain price to NAV on the hand list, top 10 lowest, with the omitted history named in the notes: a partial replication of a rule that is itself a formalisation.
6. **Expected size and sample:** Closed-end funds with a 20% discount earned about 6% a year more over the next 12 months (Pontiff 1995); a systematic CEF discount strategy earned 18.2% a year gross long-short, Sharpe 1.9, 582% turnover, 1984-2011 (Patro, Piccotti and Wu 2017, via CXO Advisory). No BDC-specific published figure exists, so there is no published bar. About 38 names and a handful of picks: the harness's t will be wide, and it reports the sessions beside it.
7. **Capacity and costs:** Long-only, 20 thousand to 200 thousand dollars a name; BDC dividends are large (ordinary income and qualified-dividend mix); shorting premium BDCs is impractical (borrow, dividend owed). The 30 bps round trip is the assumption.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** Discounts cluster in credit-quality losers: a deep discount on a falling NAV is earned, not an opportunity (rising non-accruals, a dividend cut), and the NAV can be marked up or down only quarterly. Floating-rate assets give interest-rate beta and a small-cap and value exposure. Retire if the frozen-period decile spread is not above zero or the picks do not beat the buy-and-hold mean of the listed BDCs.
9. **Decoys:** The sector's interest-rate and credit beta (the buy-and-hold mean of the listed BDCs is the base), small-cap and value exposure (the size baseline is not run on the hand list), and credit-quality losers (compare the picks' NAV change).

**Sources**

## Earnings announcement premium (`earnings_announcement_premium`)

**Status:** candidate. **Thesis:** Stocks earn more in the days around a scheduled earnings report than at other times.

- **Outcome:** excess return, over 6 sessions, vs SPY, starting -5 sessions from the event, costs 10 bps
- **Schedule:** on each `earnings_expected` (a next report expected, scheduled or from the year-ago report (earnings_expected@v1); anchor: the expected report date's session)
- **Universe:** `instrument.security_type in ('COMMON_STOCK', 'ADR')` and `instrument.status eq 'ACTIVE'` and `rollup.price_stats@v2.close gt 5` and `rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d gte 50000000`
- **Top K:** all qualified names
- **Screeners:** `eap_all`, `eap_volume`
- **Baselines:** `momentum_12_1`, `size_small`
- **Frozen period:** from 2026-04-01
- **Notes:** Window: from five sessions before the scheduled report to one after (six sessions, start_offset_sessions -5; the PRD's proposal, ADR 0053 open decisions). The date must be known by the decision session D = S - 1 (earnings_expected@v1: SCHEDULED, or PRIOR_YEAR), never be taken from the actual report date; the entry session is S = A - 5 for the expected report A. Liquid base gates: common stock or ADR, active, close above $5, 20-day ADV at least $50M. Cost 10 bps a round trip (decided 2026-10-08). The top_k is "all" at the edge level (a preset carries no top-k; the ED4 decision's top 50 is not expressible per screener). The volume claim (Frazzini and Lamont) is tested by eap_volume's picks (earnings volume ratio of at least 2, pre-declared) against eap_all's and the base rate. The window is built from the EXPECTED date: a PRIOR_YEAR date can miss the real report, so a window may not contain it; the share of windows that contained the actual report is a diagnostic (not a filter), a follow-up in the evaluation report. The Savor-Wilson variant (start_offset_sessions -2, three sessions: the returns of days -1, 0 and +1, the [-1, +1] window) needs the h=3 outcomes backfill: horizons are stored from the documents, so rerun the outcomes backfill before reading it.

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** Announcements draw attention-driven buying, and the announcement's own risk, which cannot be diversified away in a reporting season, is priced (Frazzini and Lamont 2007; Savor and Wilson 2016).
2. **Persistence:** Compensation for holding risk that concentrates on known dates: earning it means holding the announcement, which arbitrage cannot avoid.
3. **Outcome:** Excess return over SPY from five sessions before the scheduled report to one after: a hold entered ahead of the date and closed after the reaction.
4. **Trigger timing:** The expected report date is read at the decision session D (the day before the entry session S = A - 5): earnings_expected@v1 gives SCHEDULED or PRIOR_YEAR (Frazzini and Lamont's year-ago date + 364 days); an UNKNOWN date is excluded with a reason, and a date confirmed after D is not used.
5. **Faithful replication:** Frazzini and Lamont's rule first: hold every stock with a report expected in the window, equally weighted; eap_all is that rule on the liquid names; their split by expected volume is eap_volume; the Savor and Wilson window (returns of the sessions -1, 0 and +1) is the savor_wilson variant.
6. **Expected size and sample:** Published as a positive abnormal return in announcement months, largest for names with high expected announcement volume (Frazzini and Lamont 2007), and found in most markets (Barber, De George, Lehavy and Trueman 2013). About eight reports per name in the stored bars, clustered in about eight seasons.
7. **Capacity and costs:** One entry and one exit per report, no short leg; the cost is the stock's spread, small for liquid names.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** Moved or estimated dates, and a market sell-off in a season. Retire if the frozen-period excess return is not above the base rate of the same names outside their windows.
9. **Decoys:** Market beta in reporting seasons and the size effect: the size and 12-1 momentum baselines on the same sessions, and the same names outside their windows.

## 12-1 momentum (`momentum_12_1`)

**Status:** candidate. **Thesis:** Stocks that rose most over the past year, leaving out the last month, keep outperforming the market over the next month.

- **Outcome:** excess return, over 20 sessions, vs SPY, entered 1 session after the decision session, costs 10 bps
- **Schedule:** month end
- **Universe:** preset `liquid_common_stocks`
- **Top K:** 50
- **Screeners:** `momentum_12_1`
- **Baselines:** `size_small`
- **Frozen period:** from 2026-04-01
- **Published:** decile spread 1.31% over 20 sessions (Jegadeesh, N. and Titman, S., Returns to Buying Winners and Selling Losers: Implications for Stock Market Efficiency, Journal of Finance, 1993, 25 years)
- **Notes:** Month-end start sessions spaced at least 20 sessions apart. rollup.trend_stats@v2.mom_12_1 is stored from about 2025-10-31, so the stored history gives about ten independent sessions, under the 40 the bar asks; the harness reports the count beside every number. The frozen period starts 2026-04-01 (owner decision 2026-10-08, ADR 0053 amendment).

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** Investors underreact to news and herd into winners; limits to arbitrage and disposition effects slow the price from reaching its value (Jegadeesh and Titman 1993; Barberis, Shleifer and Vishny 1998).
2. **Persistence:** The premium is large, old and widely known, and has survived two decades after its publication in most markets, but it crashes in sharp market rebounds (Daniel and Moskowitz 2016): the risk that keeps arbitrageurs from removing it.
3. **Outcome:** Excess return over SPY over the 20 sessions after the entry close (the session after a month-end decision), net of 10 bps a round trip (an assumption; the PRD's proposal for liquid names, ADR 0053 open decisions).
4. **Trigger timing:** mom_12_1 is computed from the close of the decision session D (rollups, nightly), so the first fill is the next session's close: the entry session is S = D + 1 (start_offset_sessions = 1) and the window runs from S's close; the screen is read at D, the outcome at S.
5. **Faithful replication:** The published rule: rank by the return from 12 months to 1 month back and hold the top decile (Jegadeesh and Titman 1993); here the top 50 of the liquid common stocks (liquid_common_stocks).
6. **Expected size and sample:** Historically about 1% a month for the winner-minus-loser decile spread in large US stocks, smaller since 2000 (Daniel and Moskowitz 2016). Ten independent sessions cannot distinguish that from zero: the harness reports the t statistic and the sessions beside it.
7. **Capacity and costs:** Large and liquid names only (price over 5 dollars, 50M dollars of 20-day volume): turnover is monthly and spreads are small; the 10 bps round trip is the assumption.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** Momentum crashes: sharp market rebounds after a drawdown reverse winners and losers (Daniel and Moskowitz 2016). Retire if the frozen-period decile spread is not above zero or the top decile does not beat the buy-and-hold mean of the eligible names.
9. **Decoys:** The same signal in the market's own drift (compare with buy-and-hold of the eligible names), the size and quality tilts of past winners, and the 52-week-high effect (a different screen on the same sessions).

## Volatility risk premium (`vrp_short_premium`)

**Status:** candidate. **Thesis:** Option implied volatility on liquid names tends to exceed the volatility that follows, so selling premium where implied is richest earns the gap.

- **Outcome:** expires otm, over 15, 21, 31 sessions, entered 1 session after the decision session, short put struck at delta 0.3, not assigned at the horizon
- **Schedule:** every session
- **Universe:** preset `liquid_optionable_floored`
- **Top K:** 20
- **Screeners:** `vrp_iv_hv`
- **Baselines:** `momentum_12_1`, `size_small`
- **Frozen period:** from 2026-04-01
- **Notes:** The one-IV rule (ADR 0053) governs the outcome (iv_field, the IBKR IV30), not the screener's selection, which reads feature.vrp_iv30. Hit (ED4a, owner intent 2026-10-08): the short put is not assigned, struck at a 0.30 delta at the entry close on the IBKR IV30 read at D, held to the horizon's close (15, 21 or 31 sessions: the 21 to 45 DTE the owner trades, the horizon the tenor). The strike is the exact Black-Scholes delta strike (r = q = 0): K = P exp(-z iv sqrt(T) + iv^2 T / 2), z = N^-1(1 - 0.30), T = horizon / 252, P the entry close; the hit is the window's return above K / P - 1. Reported beside the hit rate, never in it: reference_rate (the mean risk-neutral N(d2), the win rate an efficient premium would give) and touch_rate (the strike reached intraday). No premium and no P&L is modelled: a win rate overstates expectancy, because the rare losses are large. IBKR's IV is personal-use (licence recorded on the result rows), fine for this private evaluation. Variants: delta 0.16, strangle, call, our own IV30, windows with no report expected, and the older realised-to-implied ratio below 1. IV30 (a 30-day tenor) is applied to the 15- and 31-session horizons too: a term-structure approximation, the same in the hit and in the reference rate.

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** Holders of stock pay for crash and variance protection; the dealers who sell it ask a premium for jump and variance risk they cannot diversify (Carr and Wu 2009; Bakshi and Kapadia 2003).
2. **Persistence:** A risk premium, not a mispricing: the seller is paid for losses that cluster in crashes, when capital is scarce, so being known does not remove it.
3. **Outcome:** The short put is not assigned at the horizon's close (expires_otm): how often the option the owner sells finishes out of the money, beside the risk-neutral rate and the share of windows whose strike was touched on the way.
4. **Trigger timing:** IV30 (ibkr_iv@v1, Cboe chains) and HV30 are stored after the close of the decision session D, so the first fill is the next session's close: the entry session is S = D + 1 (start_offset_sessions = 1). The screen and the implied vol are read at D, the outcome at S.
5. **Faithful replication:** The screen is vrp_iv_hv v1 (IV30 minus HV30 at least 10 points, IV30 / HV30 at least 1.25, $50M of 20-day dollar volume, price above $5, no leveraged or inverse fund), the history-evaluable part of vrp_scanner (IV30 at least 50% there). short_premium_liquidity (tradable put and call wings, option_liquidity@v1) and vrp_scanner rejoin once >= 40 independent sessions of chain data exist. The published setting is index variance (Carr and Wu 2009); single names are a step away from it.
6. **Expected size and sample:** Implied variance exceeds realised on average for the S&P 500 and most large stocks (Carr and Wu 2009); the single-name premium is smaller than the index's (Driessen, Maenhout and Vilkov 2009). IV30 history covers 502 sessions (ibkr_iv@v1, roadmap): 20-session windows spaced a horizon apart give about 25 independent sessions, under the 40 the bar asks, unless ED4a counts names within a session; chains are stored from 2026-10-02 only (docs/screeners/vrp-scanner.md), so short_premium_liquidity has days of history.
7. **Capacity and costs:** The cost is the option bid-ask spread, judged live; vrp_scanner (from v2) scores the put's stored spread (under 15% of mid), open interest and volume (over 1,000 each), and v3 requires $50M of 20-day dollar volume. An individual's size is far below the open interest.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** Losses arrive together in volatility spikes (short premium is short the left tail), so the drawdown cap, not the hit rate, decides; an earnings date inside the window raises implied for a reason. Retire if, in the frozen period, the picks' hit rate is not above the base rate of every liquid optionable name, or the cap is breached more often than the base rate.
9. **Decoys:** Implied above realised before every earnings date and after every shock (a known event, not a premium), and high-IV names are small: compare with the size and 12-1 momentum baselines on the same sessions, and log windows without an earnings date as a variant.

- Bakshi, G. and Kapadia, N., Delta-Hedged Gains and the Negative Market Volatility Risk Premium, Review of Financial Studies, 2003 <https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhg002>
- Driessen, J., Maenhout, P. and Vilkov, G., The Price of Correlation Risk: Evidence from Equity Options, Journal of Finance, 2009 <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2009.01467.x>

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** A fund with daily leverage L must trade L(L-1) times the day's return on its assets, buying after up days and selling after down days, near the close (Cheng and Madhavan 2009).
2. **Persistence:** The mandate fixes the trade, so it is predictable; but it is small next to closing volume and offset by the funds' own creations and redemptions (Ivanov and Lenkey 2014).
3. **Outcome:** Excess return of the funds' underlying over SPY over one session; the real window is minutes.
4. **Trigger timing:** The day's move is known only in the last hour of S; a daily close is too late to act.
5. **Faithful replication:** Cheng and Madhavan's rebalancing demand, L(L-1) times the day's return times assets.
6. **Expected size and sample:** Small next to closing volume after creations and redemptions net out (Ivanov and Lenkey 2014).
7. **Capacity and costs:** Needs intraday execution at the close; spreads and impact take what is left.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** Reversal the next morning; fund flows that offset the rebalance.
9. **Decoys:** Plain intraday momentum on volatile days, which needs no fund at all.

## S&P 500 index changes (`sp500_index_changes`)

**Status:** rejected. **Thesis:** Stocks added to the S&P 500 rise around their inclusion as index funds buy them.

**Why rejected:** Under 1% since 2010 (Greenwood and Sammon, The Disappearing Index Effect), inside costs and the announcement-to-inclusion window; checked 2026-10-07.

- **Outcome:** excess return, over 20 sessions, vs SPY, starting +1 sessions from the event
- **Schedule:** on each `index_change` (an S&P 500 membership change (index_changes@v1); anchor: the session it is first stored (never the announcement))
- **Universe:** `instrument.security_type eq 'COMMON_STOCK'` and `instrument.status eq 'ACTIVE'`
- **Top K:** all qualified names
- **Screeners:** none yet
- **Baselines:** none yet

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** Index funds must buy an added name by the effective date; the price pressure was the original effect (Shleifer 1986; Harris and Gurel 1986).
2. **Persistence:** Weak: arbitrageurs trade between announcement and effective date and S&P spreads the demand, so the effect has been competed away.
3. **Outcome:** Excess return over SPY over 20 sessions from the announcement.
4. **Trigger timing:** Only the announcement is tradable; events/index_change records membership as our reference snapshot sees it, after the announcement, so it is late for this edge.
5. **Faithful replication:** Greenwood and Sammon's event study of additions, announcement to effective date.
6. **Expected size and sample:** Under 1% since 2010 (Greenwood and Sammon); a few dozen additions a year.
7. **Capacity and costs:** Large caps, cheap to trade, but the window is crowded by index arbitrageurs.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** The pressure reverses after inclusion; a leak front-runs the announcement.
9. **Decoys:** Additions follow strong past returns and growth in size: the momentum and size baselines.

## Russell reconstitution (`russell_reconstitution`)

**Status:** blocked. **Thesis:** Stocks moving between the Russell 1000 and 2000 at the annual reconstitution feel predictable pressure from the funds that track them.

**Why blocked:** Alive, but too few events: one a year to 2025 and two a year from 2026 give about 16 events from 2010 and 2 more a year, under the 40 independent events the bar asks until the mid-2030s, even with full membership history. We also hold no Russell membership (events/index_change is the S&P 500 only), and the universe (ADR 0013) lacks the small caps needed to rebuild the rank-day cut-off, so membership needs FTSE Russell's lists.

- **Outcome:** excess return, over 20 sessions, vs SPY, starting +1 sessions from the event
- **Schedule:** on each `index_change` (an S&P 500 membership change (index_changes@v1); anchor: the session it is first stored (never the announcement))
- **Universe:** `instrument.security_type eq 'COMMON_STOCK'` and `instrument.status eq 'ACTIVE'`
- **Top K:** all qualified names
- **Screeners:** none yet
- **Baselines:** none yet
- **Published:** mean excess 5.00% over 20 sessions (Chang, Y.-C., Hong, H. and Liskovich, I., Regression Discontinuity and the Price Effects of Stock Market Indexing, Review of Financial Studies, 2015, 17 years)
- **Notes:** The schedule is a placeholder: index_change is the S&P 500 only, and the harness has no field to read it from (a fitness test keeps an open edge off such a class). The thesis needs its own event class, russell_reconstitution (ADR 0050 market structure), read from FTSE Russell's add / delete lists: anchor the session after the preliminary list, known_from its publication after the close, so a 20-session window runs from the list to the effective date (Madhavan's window). Calendar (FTSE Russell, checked 2026-10-10): rank day the last business day of April (May before 2023); preliminary lists after the close on the 4th Friday of May, updated weekly; effective after the close on the 4th Friday of June (2023-06-23, 2024-06-28, 2025-06-27, 2026-06-26). Semi-annual from 2026: rank day 2026-10-30, preliminary lists 2026-11-13, effective after the close on 2026-12-11 (first announced as the 2nd Friday of November; no standing rule for later Decembers, so the dates come from the lists, never a calendar rule). Plan: ADR 0050 amendment and the source and licence decision (owner), the source and ingestion task, the feature group and event field, then this schedule.

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** Funds tracking the Russell 2000 must buy names that enter it and sell those that leave on the reconstitution date, ranked on a known date before (Madhavan 2003; Chang, Hong and Liskovich 2015).
2. **Persistence:** The assets tracking the Russell indexes keep the demand large and the date fixed; competition shrinks it, but it was still measurable in the 2000s (Chang, Hong and Liskovich 2015).
3. **Outcome:** Excess return over SPY over 20 sessions around the reconstitution date.
4. **Trigger timing:** Membership is predictable from the rank day's market caps, weeks before the date.
5. **Faithful replication:** Madhavan's additions and deletions around the rank day and reconstitution date.
6. **Expected size and sample:** Measurable but shrinking in the published studies (Madhavan 2003; Chang et al. 2015); one event a year.
7. **Capacity and costs:** Small caps at the 1000 / 2000 boundary; a crowded trade on one day.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** Front-running moves the pressure earlier each year; the semi-annual schedule from 2026 (a December reconstitution) splits the demand across two dates and may shrink each.
9. **Decoys:** Size and past returns decide membership: the size and momentum baselines.
