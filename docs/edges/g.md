## Above the earnings-anchored VWAP (capital-gains overhang proxy) (`anchored_vwap_reversion_retest`)

**Status:** candidate. **Family:** G. **Thesis:** Stocks trading well above the average price paid since their last earnings report outperform the market over the next month.

- **Outcome:** excess return, over 20, 60 sessions, vs SPY, entered 1 session after the decision session, costs 10 bps
- **Schedule:** month end
- **Universe:** `instrument.security_type in ('COMMON_STOCK', 'ADR')` and `instrument.status eq 'ACTIVE'` and `rollup.price_stats@v2.close gt 5` and `rollup.price_stats@v2.adv_usd_20d gte 50000000` and `feature.capital_gains_overhang not_null`
- **Top K:** 50
- **Screeners:** `earnings_vwap_overhang`
- **Baselines:** `momentum_12_1`, `size_small`
- **Frozen period:** from 2026-04-01
- **Notes:** THE SIGNAL IS A PROXY. The source's overhang is (price - reference price) / price where the reference price is a turnover-weighted average of past prices over one to five years; the stored history cannot give that (no multi-year turnover history). The proxy is capital_gains_overhang: the close against the volume-weighted average price since the last earnings report (rollup.anchored_vwap@v2, at most 126 sessions back, so one to two quarters), the page's weaker 'Ready' version. It shares the sign of the idea (holders in profit or loss) but not its weighting or its horizon, and it is not the paper's measure. The retest in the edge's id (price falling back to the average from above) is not tested here: the screen ranks by distance above the average, which selects the most extended names, not the ones retesting it; a screener ranked by closeness to the average is a follow-up. A report a few sessions old gives an average near the price, so the value is near zero for those names. The universe is the liquid common stocks and ADRs with an earnings-anchored VWAP, so every name is scored. Month-end start sessions, spaced at least 20 sessions apart.

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** Holders in a gain sell too early and holders in a loss hold on (the disposition effect), so the price underreacts to news until the pressure fades; the aggregate cost basis of a stock's holders is the reference point (Grinblatt and Han 2005). The anchored VWAP is the practitioner's expression of that cost basis (Shannon 2023).
2. **Persistence:** The disposition effect is among the most persistent behavioural regularities, and the overhang predictor is replicated in the open asset-pricing data, but its strength after publication is reduced and not verified exactly; it also overlaps momentum, which it partly explains, so persistence beyond 12-1 momentum is a claim to test.
3. **Outcome:** Excess return over SPY over 20 and 60 sessions after the entry close (the session after a month-end decision), net of 10 bps a round trip (an assumption, as for 12-1 momentum): a long hold of the names furthest above their post-earnings average price.
4. **Trigger timing:** The anchored VWAP is computed from the bars through the close of the decision session D and from earnings reports known by D (ADR 0050), so the first fill is the next session's close: S = D + 1 (start_offset_sessions = 1); the screen is read at D, the outcome at S.
5. **Faithful replication:** The source's rule first: rank by the overhang and hold the top (Grinblatt and Han 2005, monthly, long the highest). Here earnings_vwap_overhang ranks the liquid names by the earnings-VWAP proxy and the edge holds the top 50. The proxy is not the paper's multi-year turnover-weighted reference price (no turnover history is stored), so this is not a faithful replication.
6. **Expected size and sample:** About 1% a month for the highest minus the lowest overhang in 1963 to 1996, which the paper says explains momentum (recalled; the page marks it as not verified, and it is a two-group spread, so no published figure is set); the proxy's size is unknown and likely smaller. The stored history gives few independent month-end sessions: the harness reports the t statistic and the count.
7. **Capacity and costs:** Liquid names only (price over 5 dollars, 50M dollars of 20-day volume), monthly turnover, long only: an individual can hold it. The 10 bps round trip is the assumption.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** The overhang is largely momentum, so its picks may only be 12-1 winners; a fresh anchor makes the value noise; a post-earnings gap up puts the whole universe's winners at the top, then the gap reverses. Retire if the frozen-period excess return of the picks is not above the base rate of every eligible name.
9. **Decoys:** 12-1 momentum (the overhang is said to subsume part of it: the momentum_12_1 baseline runs on the same sessions), the 52-week-high effect, and size: the size_small baseline.

**Sources**

- Grinblatt, M. and Han, B., Prospect theory, mental accounting, and momentum, Journal of Financial Economics, 2005 <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2004.06.007>
- An, L., Asset pricing when traders sell extreme winners and losers, Review of Financial Studies, 2016
- Shannon, B., Maximum Trading Gains with Anchored VWAP, 2023

## Reversal into earnings (`earnings_neutral_reversal`)

**Status:** candidate. **Family:** G. **Thesis:** Among stocks about to report, last days' losers beat last days' winners over the report window.

- **Outcome:** excess return, over 3 sessions, vs SPY, starting -2 sessions from the event, costs 10 bps
- **Schedule:** on each `earnings_expected` (a next report expected, scheduled or from the year-ago report (earnings_expected@v1); anchor: the expected report date's session)
- **Universe:** preset `liquid_common_stocks`
- **Top K:** 20
- **Screeners:** `pre_earnings_reversal`, `earnings_neutral_reversal`
- **Baselines:** `momentum_12_1`, `size_small`
- **Frozen period:** from 2026-04-01
- **Published:** decile spread 1.45% over 3 sessions (So, E. and Wang, S., News-Driven Return Reversals: Liquidity Provision Ahead of Earnings Announcements, Journal of Financial Markets, 2014, 16 years)
- **Notes:** The window is the 3 sessions of days -1, 0 and +1 around the expected report A, entered at the close of A - 2 (start_offset_sessions -2, the same window as the Savor-Wilson variant of the earnings announcement premium; horizon 3 is stored). The decision session is D = A - 3, so the return the screener reads is one session older than the paper's (the paper uses days -4 to -2 and enters at the close of -2): the harness cannot read a signal at the entry close itself. The date comes from earnings_expected@v1 (SCHEDULED, or last year's plus 364 days), known by D, so a name whose date moved is a miss, not a lookahead. The paper's size filter is the top quintile: the liquid_common_stocks floors ($50M a day) are the nearest rule, wider than the paper's quintile. pre_earnings_reversal ranks the 3-session return, losers first (long the losers: the paper also shorts the winners, which is diagnostic only); earnings_neutral_reversal ranks the 20-session return without earnings reaction days (earnings_neutral_reversal@v1), the plan's "earnings week reversal". Both rollups need the backfill once the document merges, and the earnings history of the store (EDGAR 8-K Item 2.02 and the vendors') sets the sessions; reports cluster in four seasons a year, so the independent sessions are fewer than the events.

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** Liquidity providers demand higher compensation for holding inventory into a known information event; the price pressure before the event is reversed as the event resolves the risk (So and Wang 2014). The compensation is for a real risk, the announcement, so nobody fixes it.
2. **Persistence:** It is a risk premium for inventory risk around scheduled news. It needs daily rebalancing across about a thousand announcers and short positions, so it is unattractive to large funds; but out-of-sample replications report the alpha deteriorating.
3. **Outcome:** Excess return over SPY for the 3 sessions from the close two sessions before the expected report (days -1, 0 and +1), net of 10 bps a round trip.
4. **Trigger timing:** The expected report date A is read at D = A - 3 (earnings_expected@v1, SCHEDULED or PRIOR_YEAR; UNKNOWN is excluded with a reason; a date confirmed after D is not used). The 3-session and the 20-session returns are read at D's close; the entry session is S = A - 2.
5. **Faithful replication:** So and Wang: the largest size quintile, rank by the average return over days -4 to -2, long the bottom quintile and short the top, hold days -1, 0 and +1, equal-weighted. Here the 20 lowest-return names (long only) of the liquid common stocks with a report expected, from the 3-session return (pre_earnings_reversal) and from the earnings-neutral month (earnings_neutral_reversal).
6. **Expected size and sample:** 1.45% for the low-minus-high 3-day return against 0.22% in pseudo-announcement windows, 1996-2011, before costs, Sharpe 1.73 as annualised by Quantpedia; the long-only half and the 2012-on sample are smaller, and Quantpedia's out-of-sample test is slightly negative (So and Wang 2014; Jansen and Nikiforov report profit in 40 of 42 years).
7. **Capacity and costs:** Three-day holds, 5 to 30 names a night in a season, small for a retail account; a report is a gap risk the 10 bps a round trip do not cover; the short leg is not tested.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** The alpha having decayed, and bid-ask bounce in the pre-earnings return. Retire if the frozen-period excess return of the picks is not above that of the other names reporting in the window.
9. **Decoys:** Pre-announcement drift from leaks (it predicts continuation: the opposite sign), momentum at earnings (a longer-horizon edge, opposite sign) and the small-cap PEAD (another size bucket): the 12-1 momentum and size baselines, and the other names reporting in the window.

**Sources**

- So, E. and Wang, S., News-Driven Return Reversals: Liquidity Provision Ahead of Earnings Announcements, Journal of Financial Markets, 2014 <https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2275982>
- Quantpedia, Reversal During Earnings Announcements (strategy page) <https://quantpedia.com/strategies/reversal-during-earnings-announcements/>

## Index ETF weak-close reversal (`ibs_etf_mean_reversion`)

**Status:** candidate. **Family:** G. **Thesis:** An index ETF that closes near the low of its day rises the next session on average, so holding it only after such a close earns more per day invested.

- **Outcome:** portfolio return, over 1 session, vs a blend of 50% SPY and 50% QQQ, entered 1 session after the decision session, costs 4 bps
- **Schedule:** every session
- **Universe:** `instrument.symbol in ('SPY', 'QQQ')`
- **Top K:** 2
- **Screeners:** `etf_low_ibs`
- **Baselines:** none yet
- **Frozen period:** from 2026-04-01
- **Notes:** VERDICTS ARE UNMEASURED until the screen is run over the stored history and evaluated; the stored daily bars give internal bar strength as rollup.trend_stats@v2.close_range_pos, so no new feature is built. LIMITS: (1) the source's list is about twenty index ETFs; two (SPY, QQQ) are used so the equal-weight benchmark is exact (IWM and the sector funds are left out). (2) The benchmark is the two funds held always, so a book that holds them about a fifth of the sessions and cash otherwise trails by the drift of the market it skips: the value is a TIMING comparison, and a negative mean is expected from the cash days alone. The harness has no cash benchmark; the right test is the mean per invested session against the same days of the benchmark, which the weights recorded in the run allow and the verdict does not read: the read of the result needs that care (flagged for review). (3) One- session holds, a fund held at half when only one passes, the other half cash; cost_bps 4 a round trip assumed; the source's long-short version is not built. The idea is a timing overlay for other edges' entries, weak alone.

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** End-of-day order imbalance (market-on-close flow, hedging, futures-cash arbitrage) overshoots into the close and reverses overnight; it is index level, so there is no stock-specific information to lose (Pagonidis 2013).
2. **Persistence:** The effect is a few basis points a trade held one day, which rules out capacity-seeking funds, and it is partly the overnight premium in disguise; it faded after about 2011, so persistence is a claim to test.
3. **Outcome:** The book's return over the one session from the close after each decision session, less half SPY and half QQQ over the same session, net of the cost on the weight change; a session with no weak close is cash at the bill yield. Price only: the stored forward return is close to close and carries no dividend or distribution, so the book and the benchmark both miss their yield and cash earns the Treasury bill.
4. **Trigger timing:** The internal bar strength is read at the close of D from that day's own high, low and close; the book is held from the next session's close S (start_offset_sessions = 1) for one session. Nothing after D enters a pick.
5. **Faithful replication:** Pagonidis (2013): buy at the close when the internal bar strength is under 0.2 (or the lowest quintile) and exit at the next close, over about twenty equity index ETFs since 1993; the low-IBS days dominated total returns (from memory, not re-verified). Here: SPY and QQQ, under 0.2.
6. **Expected size and sample:** Strong before 2010; practitioner follow-ups report a diminished but positive SPY and QQQ effect through the 2010s and a weak one since 2020 (memory). A few basis points a trade.
7. **Capacity and costs:** Unlimited capacity in two funds with near-zero spread cost; about 50 trades a year; cost_bps 4 a round trip assumed against a few basis points of edge.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** The edge is of the order of the cost and the overnight premium; it shares fate with RSI(2) and the short-term index reversal cluster. Retire if the frozen-period picks-series t is under 2.
9. **Decoys:** The overnight (close to open) premium and the VIX regime; the benchmark mix and the invested-session comparison in the run record are the nulls.

**Sources**

- Pagonidis, A., The IBS Effect: Mean Reversion in Equity ETFs, SSRN, 2013
- Connors, L. and Alvarez, C., Short Term Trading Strategies That Work, TradingMarkets, 2009
