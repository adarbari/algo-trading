**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** Bear markets last longer than a year at the median, so a slow exit still avoids most of the damage; the same under-reaction and forced selling as trend (Antonacci 2013; Faber 2007).
2. **Persistence:** The rule costs return in sideways and V-shaped markets (2011, 2015 to 2016, 2020, 2023), which is when holders abandon it; it is out of the market most of the time it beats buy and hold only in the few deep falls (Zakamulin). A claim of drawdown control, not of alpha.
3. **Outcome:** The five-fund book's return over the 21 sessions from the close after each month end, less the equal five-fund mix's over the same sessions, net of the cost on the weight change; a fund whose trend is down is held as cash at the bill yield. Price only: the stored forward return is close to close and carries no dividend or distribution, so the book and the benchmark both miss their yield and cash earns the Treasury bill.
4. **Trigger timing:** The trailing returns and the 200-session mean are read at the month-end close D; the book is held from the next session's close S for 21 sessions. Nothing after D enters a pick.
5. **Faithful replication:** Antonacci (2013): monthly, hold the asset if its 12-month excess return over the bill is above 0, else bills; eight asset classes, 1973 to 2012, 0.2 percent a switch. Faber (2007): month-end close above the 10-month average, 20 percent per asset, bills otherwise. Here: five ETFs, raw returns and a 200-day stand-in, the three averaged.
6. **Expected size and sample:** Antonacci (index data, in sample): for a 60/40 mix the Sharpe ratio rises from 0.47 to 0.72 and the drawdown falls from 29 to 13 percent; for an equal-weight five-asset mix the Sharpe ratio rises from 0.62 to 1.06. Faber in sample 1972 to 2005: Sharpe 0.81; out of sample 2006 to 2025 (a 200-day variant): 0.68 (Concretum). Expect the timing to lose to a passive mix on return in most years.
7. **Capacity and costs:** Unlimited capacity in five liquid funds; about 5 to 8 trades a year in all; a few basis points a trade (6 assumed). The tax cost of the switches is not deducted.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** The 12-month lookback was the best of 2 to 18 months in sample, and the 2020 fall was too fast to exit before the bottom. Retire if the frozen-period picks-series t is under 2 or the book does not lower the passive mix's drawdown in the run record.
9. **Decoys:** Pure beta timing, so a rising market makes the book trail its benchmark by design: the informative figures are the drawdown and the Sharpe ratio, in the run record, against the passive five. The 1970s bond bear and 2008 dominate the published samples.

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** Term premia move with inflation uncertainty and supply, investors anchor on the level of yields, and the Fed reacts slowly; bond mandates are fixed to a benchmark duration, so few can act on carry and trend together (Ilmanen, Expected Returns, 2011).
2. **Persistence:** Timing on the slope alone has repeatedly failed out of sample (Thornton and Valente 2012), so the rule asks for trend as well; the survivor is the pair, which few bond mandates are allowed to run. The 1982 to 2020 fall in yields lifted every duration rule, so persistence is a claim to test.
3. **Outcome:** The book's return over the 21 sessions from the close after each month end, less half TLT and half IEF over the same sessions, net of cost on the weight change; a fund that fails the curve or the trend test is held as cash. Price only: the stored forward return is close to close and carries no coupon, so the book and the benchmark both miss their yield and cash earns the Treasury bill.
4. **Trigger timing:** The curve slope (a macro value known by the session) and the 252-session return are read at the month-end close D; the book is held from the next session's close S for 21 sessions. Nothing after D enters a pick.
5. **Faithful replication:** Ilmanen (Expected Returns, ch. 9): monthly, overweight duration when the slope plus the roll-down is above 0 and the 12-month bond excess return is above 0. Here: TLT and IEF, the 10-year minus 3-month or minus 2-year slope above 0 and the raw 12-month return above 0, the two screeners averaged, no roll-down, a switched-off slot in cash.
6. **Expected size and sample:** Not verified here. Fama-Bliss and Campbell-Shiller find the term spread predicts excess bond returns at annual horizons, but Thornton and Valente (2012) find no systematic economic value out of sample; carry plus trend holds (Koijen et al., Baltussen et al.). The page records that in 2022 the curve flattened and trend turned down together, which would have sidestepped TLT's fall (not verified here).
7. **Capacity and costs:** Very large capacity in two funds; a few switches a year; spreads are a cent or two; cost_bps 6 assumed. The coupon forgone while in cash is the real drag and is not modelled.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** The slope is a weak signal (the one that fails alone), both funds read it, and two funds leave few independent choices. A bond bull market from 2010 to 2020 flatters any rule that holds duration. Retire if the frozen-period picks-series t is under 2.
9. **Decoys:** Duration beta and the secular fall in rates, and the tsmom_multi_asset_etf edge (TLT and IEF are two of its nine funds): compare with it, and with a plain 12-month trend on TLT alone (trend_positive_12m).
