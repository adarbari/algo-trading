**Status:** candidate. **Family:** E. **Thesis:** An ETF whose own trailing return is positive keeps rising over the next month, so holding only the rising funds of nine asset classes, weighted by inverse volatility, beats a 60/40 stock and bond mix.

- **Outcome:** portfolio return, over 21 sessions, vs a blend of 60% SPY and 40% AGG, entered 1 session after the decision session, costs 6 bps
- **Schedule:** month end
- **Universe:** `instrument.symbol in ('SPY', 'EFA', 'EEM', 'AGG', 'TLT', 'IEF', 'GLD', 'DBC', 'VNQ')`
- **Top K:** 9
- **Screeners:** `trend_positive_3m`, `trend_positive_6m`, `trend_positive_12m`
- **Baselines:** none yet
- **Frozen period:** from 2026-04-01
- **Notes:** VERDICTS ARE UNMEASURED until the screens are run over the stored history (the outcome rows of all nine funds start with the store on 2010-01-04, so that is the common start; the grid needs every fund each session, ADR 0074, so the latest-starting fund sets the sample) and the parent's evaluation runs. LIMITS, so nothing implies the full source rule: (1) long only: the source goes short a falling asset and scales each leg to 40 percent volatility with futures; this book holds a rising fund or its slot is cash at the Treasury bill yield, never short and never levered. (2) The trend test is a raw price return above 0, not an excess return over the bill, and it uses 3, 6 and 12 months of 60, 120 and 252 sessions: Hurst, Ooi and Pedersen's 1, 3 and 12 months and the 9-month member of a 3, 6, 9 and 12 grid are not built (stored return columns are 1, 3, 10, 20, 60, 120 and 252 sessions; a 189-session column would change a stored feature group). (3) Weights are one over hv60 (rollup.vol_stats@v1, 60 sessions of close-to-close volatility read at D), shared among the rising funds with each unfilled slot of nine held in cash: relative sizing, not a volatility target, so the book's own volatility moves with how many funds rise. (4) Nine funds only, price only (no distributions, so bond coupons are missing from the book and the benchmark), cost_bps 6 a round trip assumed for liquid ETFs. (5) The benchmark is the page's passive 60/40 of SPY and AGG, two of the nine funds, so a book that is half cash is not rewarded for beating a stock-only index. The ensemble of the three lookbacks is ONE trial, the members are never a verdict basis, and the harness reads the book against its benchmark on the picks-series t (a Promising edge needs t at or above 2, Works 3). The trend leg of trend_carry_value_combo is this edge: no separate document, since carry needs futures curves and value needs country CAPE, neither stored. No [published] figure: the paper Sharpe ratios are of futures books the harness does not measure. verdict.toml [oos_haircut] trend = 0.45 prints the in-sample Sharpe ratio, what it becomes after the haircut and the out-of-sample one beside the result, as display only.

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** Prices under-react to news and then over-react, and mandates and rebalancing rules force institutions to sell what rose and buy what fell; hedgers in futures pay speculators to carry trend risk (Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen 2012; Hurst, Ooi and Pedersen 2017).
2. **Persistence:** The premium is paid for deep drawdowns (16 above 10 percent since 2000 in the SG Trend Index, 20 percent from May 2024 to May 2025) and for years of nothing in the 2010s; allocation is pro- cyclical, so outflows follow droughts and keep the premium. Huang, Li, Wang and Zhou (2020) find little predictability asset by asset, so persistence is a claim to test.
3. **Outcome:** The book's return over the 21 sessions from the close after each month end, less the return of 60 percent SPY and 40 percent AGG over the same sessions, net of the cost on the weight change; cash is the Treasury bill yield (ADR 0074). Price only: the stored forward return is close to close and carries no dividend or distribution, so the book and the benchmark both miss their yield and cash earns the Treasury bill.
4. **Trigger timing:** The trailing returns and hv60 are read at the month-end close D from stored bars; the book is held from the next session's close S (start_offset_sessions = 1) for 21 sessions. No fact after D enters a weight or a pick.
5. **Faithful replication:** The source's rule (Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen 2012): the sign of the trailing 12-month excess return, each position scaled to 40 percent ex-ante volatility, held a month, over 58 futures. Hurst, Ooi and Pedersen (2017) average 1, 3 and 12 months. Here: nine ETFs, long only, 3, 6 and 12 months of raw return, inverse-volatility weights, cash for a falling fund.
6. **Expected size and sample:** Hurst, Ooi and Pedersen: positive net returns in every decade since 1880 and a net Sharpe ratio near 0.4 per asset (futures, both sides); the practitioner expectation after decay for a long-only ETF version is 0.3 to 0.5. The harness's figure over the ETF history is untested here.
7. **Capacity and costs:** Capacity is large in nine liquid funds; about one switch per fund a year; a round trip costs a few basis points (6 assumed). Tax drag on the switches is the real cost and is not deducted.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** Long-only trend is mostly equity beta in a bull market and gives up the short crisis alpha. Retire if the frozen-period picks-series t stays under 2, or the ensemble's out-of-sample Sharpe falls under the haircut figure (45 percent of the in-sample).
9. **Decoys:** Beta (a book that is mostly SPY in rising markets), a 2010s sample dominated by the US bull market, and a 1970s-style bond bear it does not contain. The benchmark mix and the decoy battery are the nulls; compare the book with SPY alone in the run record.

## Buying SPY after a VIX spike (`vix_mean_reversion_entry`)

**Status:** candidate. **Family:** E. **Thesis:** SPY earns more over the 63 sessions after a VIX spike with an inverted term structure than over the same number of sessions at other times.

- **Outcome:** excess return, over 63 sessions, entered 1 session after the decision session
- **Schedule:** on market:vix spike
- **Universe:** `instrument.symbol in ('SPY',)`
- **Top K:** all qualified names
- **Screeners:** none yet
- **Baselines:** none yet
- **Frozen period:** from 2026-04-01
- **Notes:** VERDICTS ARE UNMEASURED until the D0 Tiingo outcomes backfill (due 2026-11-09) gives SPY its outcome history. The trigger is the market_triggers@v1 spike (ADR 0075): the first session with a VIX close of at least 30 and VIX / VIX3M above 1 after at least 21 sessions without one, both from the macro table (FRED VIXCLS and VXVCLS, personal use only, so the edge's evidence is personal-licence). The decision session D is the session the reading was first known (the day after its close, the series' release lag) and the entry session S = D + 1; the fixed thresholds are the feature's, a change is a version bump. The value of a window is SPY's raw return over the 63 sessions from S's close, compared with SPY's own non-overlapping 63-session stretches outside every window (ADR 0073); a trigger within 63 sessions of the last counted one is skipped, so windows never overlap. The source's 21 to 63 session range is cut to one horizon, 63, because the page says the next month is worse and months three to twelve are better; one instrument and one horizon are one trial (deflated Sharpe ratio). Counts: the spike is rare (the page puts independent spikes since 1990 at about 25) and the stored outcome history reaches 2018, so a handful of windows fall in the range, far under the 40 independent sessions the bar asks (MIN_INDEPENDENT_SESSIONS): the document cannot reach evidenced, and every figure is shown with its count. A stretch near the range's end can change when the range is extended, because the base avoids triggers that are known only later (ADR 0075). No cost is deducted from a timed position (ADR 0073). BIAS: the out-of-window base includes the 63-session stretches that lead into each spike (the falls that caused it), which lowers the base and biases in-window minus out-of-window towards a pass; read a pass with that in mind. Not tested: the page's hit-rate tables (not verified), a VIX-only trigger without the inversion, and any exit rule. No [published] figure: the page's 65 to 75 percent hit rate at three months is from practitioner tables it marks as not verified.

**Quality bar**

1. **Mechanism:** A volatility spike is forced de-risking (volatility-targeting funds, dealers' gamma, margin calls) that overshoots, and the price of insurance is then at its widest; buyers of equity risk are paid for providing liquidity while the sellers follow rules (Johnson 2017 on the VIX term structure; Cheng 2019 on the VIX premium). The equity-timing form is a practitioner rule, not an academic result.
2. **Persistence:** The selling is rule-driven at exactly the moment a discretionary investor is afraid to buy, and the distribution is bimodal (2008 and 2020 kept falling), which keeps capital away. The practitioner tables behind the rule are not verified and their events are few and clustered, so persistence is a claim to test.
3. **Outcome:** SPY's raw return (benchmark none) over the 63 sessions after a VIX spike, from the entry close (the session after the day the spike was first known), against its own return over the same number of sessions outside every window; no cost is deducted from a timed position (ADR 0073).
4. **Trigger timing:** The spike is read at the decision session D from market_triggers@v1 (sessions_since_vix_spike = 0): a VIX close of at least 30 with VIX / VIX3M above 1 after 21 quiet sessions, known from the session after its date (the series' one-day release lag, no revisions). The entry session S = D + 1 (start_offset_sessions = 1). A stale VIX or VIX3M reading (more than 3 sessions old) is UNKNOWN, never 'no spike'. No fact after D places a window.
5. **Faithful replication:** The practitioner rule: buy SPY when VIX is above 30 and VIX / VIX3M above 1 and hold 21 to 63 sessions (the page's Cboe and SentimentTrader tables). Here the same trigger is the one in market_triggers@v1 and SPY is held 63 sessions, compared with its own other stretches; no exit rule and no cash leg are tested.
6. **Expected size and sample:** The page quotes hit rates of 65 to 75 percent at three months for VIX above 30 entries since 1990, not verified and over 20 to 30 clustered events, and gives no return figure, so none is set. The range here holds a handful of independent spikes: a t statistic cannot separate a few percentage points over 63 sessions from the market's own drift.
7. **Capacity and costs:** Unlimited capacity in one index fund, a few round trips a decade, a cost of 1 to 2 bps a side; long only and unlevered. Nothing is deducted in this version. The risk is the drawdown after entry: 2008 and 2020 kept falling for weeks.
8. **Failure modes and retirement:** The base includes the falls leading into each spike, which biases the comparison towards a pass; the spike sits at the start of a long bear market (2008), so a fixed-horizon entry loses; the events are too few and clustered for any t statistic; the effect is the mean reversion of equity returns after any large drawdown. Retire if the frozen-period in-window minus out-of-window mean is not above zero, or the Welch t over the independent windows stays under 2.
9. **Decoys:** Mean reversion after any 10 percent drawdown, and the volatility-managed portfolios result (Moreira and Muir 2017) that says to hold less when volatility is high: the two agree only because they differ in horizon (next month against months three to twelve). Overlaps the vrp_short_premium edge, which sells puts on the same spikes. Test against the 'any 63-session window' null, which the 1,000 random draws of the harness are.

- Cheng, I.-H., The VIX premium, Review of Financial Studies, 2019
- Moreira, A. and Muir, T., Volatility-managed portfolios, Journal of Finance, 2017
